## Summary

Adverum Biotechnologies entered into a definitive agreement to be acquired by Eli Lilly and Company for $3.56 per share in cash plus a non-tradable contingent value right (CVR) worth up to $8.91 per share. The transaction was structured as a tender offer followed by a second-step merger under Section 251(h) of the DGCL. The deal was driven by Adverum's critical need for capital, as its cash runway was estimated to expire in October 2025, and Lilly's interest in Adverum's lead gene therapy candidate, Ixo-vec, for wet age-related macular degeneration.

## Interaction History

Negotiations began in mid-2023 as Adverum sought a partner to fund Phase 3 trials for Ixo-vec. Adverum contacted approximately 30 parties. Party A was a lead contender for a license until June 2025. Lilly initially pursued a licensing deal (July 2025) but shifted to an acquisition structure in September 2025 due to Adverum's precarious cash position. Adverum's management, led by CEO Laurent Fischer and COO Peter Soparkar, negotiated with Lilly's business development team. Lilly's initial acquisition offer of $91M upfront was significantly lower than Adverum's counter of $175M. Lilly eventually moved to a $100M upfront aggregate value ($3.56/share) and $250M aggregate CVR value ($8.91/share). Adverum's board accepted the offer on October 23, 2025, noting that the only alternative was an orderly liquidation (estimated at $0.03/share). To bridge the closing period, Lilly provided a $65M secured promissory note.

## Rationale

The Adverum Board determined the deal was the best alternative to maximize value given the company's 'highly constrained cash runway' (expiring October 2025). Management's dissolution analysis suggested a liquidation value of only $0.03 per share. Lilly sought to integrate Adverum's gene therapy platform and Ixo-vec into its ophthalmology portfolio.
