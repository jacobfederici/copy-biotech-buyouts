## Summary

Nabriva Therapeutics AG underwent a corporate redomiciliation from Austria to Ireland, executed via a voluntary exchange offer by a newly formed Irish entity, Nabriva Therapeutics plc. Under the terms of the offer, holders of Nabriva AG common shares and ADSs exchanged their securities for ordinary shares of the new Irish parent company on a one-for-one basis (adjusting for the ADS ratio). Following the successful conclusion of the exchange offer on June 23, 2017, Nabriva Ireland became the ultimate holding company of the group, and the entity's tax residency was moved to Ireland.

## Interaction History

The transaction was structured as a internal corporate reorganization rather than a third-party acquisition. On April 11, 2017, the Supervisory Board and Management Board of Nabriva AG met to discuss the formal recommendations for a redomiciliation to Ireland. They unanimously determined that moving the parent company to Ireland would be in the best interest of the group. On May 23, 2017, Nabriva Ireland (the new holding entity) commenced a tender offer to acquire all outstanding shares of Nabriva AG. The offer was supported by the board's recommendation in the SC 14D9 filing. The offer concluded on June 23, 2017, with a sufficient number of shares tendered to establish Nabriva Ireland as the new parent company. Subsequent filings confirmed the termination of the old ADS deposit agreement and the completion of a mandatory exchange for remaining fractional interests.

## Rationale

The redomiciliation was intended to relocate the holding company to Ireland to provide a more favorable corporate and tax environment for the group's global operations and future commercialization of lefamulin. The board stated the exchange offer was in the best interest of the current and future subsidiaries and shareholders.

Nabriva Therapeutics AG (later Nabriva Therapeutics plc) did not have a history of credible, reported buyout or acquisition rumors from major financial news outlets (such as Bloomberg, Reuters, or the Wall Street Journal) prior to its eventual wind-down and asset sales. 

While the company was frequently the subject of retail investor speculation on social media and message boards—often fueled by its commercial agreement with Merck & Co. for Sivextro—no confirmed pre-deal rumors with specific prices or buyers were reported by reputable publications.

The following timeline lists the key "strategic" events and speculative mentions that appeared before the company's assets were sold or the company was wound down:

    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** N/A (Confirmed Deal)
