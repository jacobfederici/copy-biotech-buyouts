## Summary

Sirtris Pharmaceuticals, Inc. entered into a definitive merger agreement with GlaxoSmithKline plc (GSK) and its subsidiary SmithKline Beecham Corporation, under which GSK acquired all outstanding shares of Sirtris for $22.50 per share in cash. The transaction was structured as a tender offer followed by a second-step merger, valuing the company at approximately $720 million on a fully diluted basis, to integrate Sirtris' sirtuin-activation platform into GSK's research and development portfolio.

## Interaction History

Negotiations began as collaboration talks regarding the SIRT1 activation program. Between 2005 and 2007, Sirtris met with several pharmaceutical companies. In January 2008, GSK's Moncef Slaoui expressed interest in a business combination rather than just a partnership. On April 1, 2008, GSK proposed $18.50-$19.50 per share. Sirtris CEO Christoph Westphal and the Transaction Committee (Richard Aldrich, John Clarke, Richard Pops) countered that $22.50 might be acceptable. GSK submitted a formal $22.50 offer on April 11, 2008, which it characterized as its 'best and final.' Sirtris engaged J.P. Morgan to conduct a market check, contacting other potential buyers, but no superior proposals emerged. Negotiations over the next week focused on retention mechanisms for key personnel (Westphal and Sinclair), the definition of Material Adverse Effect, and a $22.5 million termination fee. The Sirtris Board approved the deal on April 21, 2008, following a fairness opinion from J.P. Morgan.

## Rationale

The Sirtris Board determined the merger provided the highest value reasonably obtainable for stockholders, offering a 77.4% premium over the recent closing price. The board considered the risks of remaining independent, including capital needs for research and development and clinical trial uncertainties. GSK sought to acquire Sirtris's proprietary sirtuin-activating compounds and scientific platform to treat metabolic and aging-related diseases.
