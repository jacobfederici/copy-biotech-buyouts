## Summary

Gilead Sciences, Inc. acquired Myogen, Inc. for $52.50 per share in an all-cash tender offer followed by a short-form merger, valuing the transaction at approximately $2.3 billion. The acquisition was driven by Myogen's late-stage cardiovascular pipeline, specifically ambrisentan for pulmonary arterial hypertension and darusentan for resistant hypertension.

## Interaction History

Negotiations began in late 2005 with a market check by Goldman Sachs. Myogen initially focused on a licensing deal for ambrisentan, eventually partnering with GSK in March 2006. During this time, 'Company A' expressed interest in an acquisition but was rebuffed. In April 2006, Gilead (represented by Lazard) initiated contact. Formal due diligence began in May 2006. In July 2006, Gilead proposed $50.00 per share. Negotiations became competitive in August and September 2006 as Company A re-entered the process. Gilead initially lowered its bid to $45.00 after diligence but was forced to increase it to $51.00 and finally $52.50 to beat Company A's final $52.00 bid. Key individuals included Dr. J. William Freytag (Myogen CEO), Dr. John F. Milligan (Gilead CFO), and Dr. John C. Martin (Gilead CEO). The Myogen Board conducted multiple meetings to evaluate the fiduciary duties and the relative value of the bids, ultimately choosing Gilead's higher cash offer.

## Rationale

The Myogen Board recommended the deal because the $52.50 cash price provided immediate certainty of value at a significant premium (49.7% over the last closing price). They considered the risks of remaining independent, including clinical trial and regulatory hurdles for ambrisentan and darusentan, and concluded the Gilead offer was the highest price reasonably attainable after a competitive process with Company A and a broader market check.

## Buyout Rumors

Source: OpenRouter web search (Gemini 3 Flash Preview). Pre-deal rumors only.

The acquisition of Myogen, Inc. (MYOG) by Gilead Sciences, announced on October 2, 2006, was widely characterized by financial analysts and news outlets as a "surprise" that caught the market "off-guard." Consequently, there were no specific, widely reported buyout rumors or leaked deal terms in the public domain prior to the official announcement.

While no pre-deal rumors exist in the form of specific reports naming Gilead as a buyer or citing a specific price, the following market activity and general speculation occurred in the period leading up to the deal:

*   **Early 2006:** **General Sector Speculation**
    *   **Reporting Outlets:** Various (e.g., *BioWorld*, *MarketWatch*)
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** General "Big Pharma" or partners like GlaxoSmithKline (GSK).
    *   **Details:** Following Myogen’s successful Phase III data for its drug ambrisentan in late 2005 and its subsequent $100 million licensing deal with GlaxoSmithKline for ex-U.S. rights in early 2006, analysts frequently cited Myogen as a potential "takeover target" due to the scarcity of late-stage cardiovascular assets. However, no specific buyer or price was reported.

*   **September 21, 2006:** **Unusual Options Activity**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** Academic/Forensic Analysis (e.g., *McGill University/SEC Litigation reviews*)
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Not specified.
    *   **Rumored Price:** Not specified.
    *   **Details:** Retrospective forensic analysis of market data identified a significant spike in out-of-the-money call option volume for MYOG on this date. While not reported as a "rumor" in the press at the time, this activity indicated that "informed trading" was occurring approximately ten days before the Gilead announcement.

*   **October 2, 2006:** **Confirmed Deal Announcement**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *Business Wire* / *The New York Times*
    *   **Acquirer:** Gilead Sciences, Inc.
    *   **Price:** $52.50 per share ($2.5 billion total).
    *   **Details:** Gilead officially announced the acquisition. Media coverage (e.g., *The New York Times*, *LA Times*) explicitly noted that the deal was a surprise and that Gilead had not previously been linked to Myogen in public speculation.
