## Summary

Targanta Therapeutics Corporation entered into a definitive merger agreement with The Medicines Company following a period of severe financial distress triggered by a negative FDA advisory committee vote and a subsequent complete response letter for its lead antibiotic candidate, oritavancin. The transaction was structured as a cash tender offer of $2.00 per share plus contingent payment rights of up to $4.55 per share tied to regulatory approvals and sales milestones. The deal closed on February 25, 2009, after approximately 98% of shares were tendered, making Targanta a wholly-owned subsidiary of The Medicines Company.

## Interaction History

Negotiations began in late 2008 as Targanta sought capital. Following the FDA's negative feedback on oritavancin in November 2008, Targanta's financial position became 'distressed,' with cash expected to be exhausted by Q3 2009. The Medicines Company (MDCO), led by CEO Clive Meanwell and CFO Glenn Sblendorio, initially proposed a stock-for-stock deal on Dec 16, 2008. Targanta's CEO Mark Leuchtenberger and CFO George Eldridge, advised by Leerink Swann, pushed for higher value and cash consideration. MDCO moved to an all-cash tender offer structure on Jan 6, 2009, to provide a faster closing. On Jan 8, 2009, MDCO issued a 'best and final' offer of $2.00 cash plus $4.55 in CVRs. Targanta's board, excluding William Crouse (who recused himself due to his seat on MDCO's board), evaluated the offer against a backdrop of failed outreach to 25 other potential partners, only six of whom showed even preliminary interest. The board approved the deal on Jan 11, 2009, citing the lack of viable financing alternatives and the risk of total insolvency.

## Rationale

Targanta's board recommended the deal because the company was in a 'distressed financial situation' following the FDA's Complete Response Letter for oritavancin. Management estimated cash would run out by Q3 2009, and the cost of the newly required Phase III trial was prohibitive. The board determined that the $2.00 upfront cash (a 96% premium to the prior day's close) and the CVRs provided the best available value for stockholders compared to the high risk of insolvency or highly dilutive financing. The Medicines Company sought the acquisition to add oritavancin to its hospital-based therapeutic portfolio, leveraging its track record of drug commercialization.

The deal value is explicitly stated in the 8-K as approximately $50M upfront plus $95.5M in contingent payments. Shares outstanding (20,991,316) were sourced from the SC 14D9 Item 1(b).
