## Summary

CoTherix, Inc. entered into a definitive merger agreement with Actelion US Holding Company on November 19, 2006, under which Actelion commenced a tender offer to acquire all outstanding shares of CoTherix for $13.50 per share in cash. The transaction followed a competitive process involving negotiations with Actelion and the exercise of a right of first negotiation by Schering AG, the licensor of CoTherix's primary product, Ventavis. The deal was valued at approximately $388.58 million based on outstanding shares and was unanimously recommended by the CoTherix board of directors.

## Interaction History

Negotiations began in August 2006 when Actelion's CEO Jean-Paul Clozel met with CoTherix Chairman Bradford Goodwin. Actelion initially proposed $10.00 per share, which was rejected by the CoTherix board on August 18. A subsequent offer of $12.25 on August 23 was also rejected. After CoTherix management, led by CEO Donald Santel, insisted on higher value, Actelion proposed $13.00 on October 9 and finally $13.50 on October 10. A significant complication arose due to the 'Development and License Agreement' with Schering AG for Ventavis, which granted Schering a right of first negotiation in the event of a change in control. Schering exercised this right on November 1, 2006, forcing a pause in Actelion talks. However, after conducting due diligence, Schering relinquished its rights on November 17. CoTherix immediately re-engaged Actelion, which confirmed its $13.50 offer. CIBC World Markets acted as financial advisor to CoTherix, contacting other third parties during the process, though no other superior proposals emerged.

## Rationale

The CoTherix board recommended the deal because the $13.50 price represented a 21% premium over the recent closing price and a 72% premium over the price one month prior. The board also considered the risks of remaining independent, including the challenges of marketing a single product and the uncertainties of clinical trials. Actelion viewed the combination as strategically sensible given their existing technical assistance on the STEP trial and shared focus on pulmonary arterial hypertension.
