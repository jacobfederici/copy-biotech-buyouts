## Summary

On August 10, 2020, Pfenex Inc. (NYSE American: PFNX), a San Diego-based biopharmaceutical company operating the Pfenex Expression Technology protein production platform, entered into a Merger Agreement with Ligand Pharmaceuticals Incorporated under which Ligand's wholly-owned subsidiary Pelican Acquisition Sub, Inc. commenced a tender offer to acquire all outstanding shares of Pfenex at $12.00 per share in cash plus one non-transferable contingent value right (CVR) per share entitling holders to $2.00 in cash upon FDA granting a therapeutic equivalence ("TE") rating to PF708 (Bonsity, teriparatide injection) by December 31, 2021. The Offer expired on September 29, 2020; 24,744,327 shares (approximately 72% of outstanding shares) were validly tendered. The Minimum Condition was satisfied. On October 1, 2020, Pelican Acquisition Sub merged with and into Pfenex pursuant to Section 251(h) of the DGCL without a stockholder vote, and Pfenex became a wholly-owned subsidiary of Ligand. Pfenex's shares were delisted from NYSE American and ceased trading on September 30, 2020.

## Interaction History

The interaction history begins with a commercial outreach by Pfenex's Chief Business Officer Patrick Lucy to Ligand CEO John Higgins on April 30, 2018, exploring whether Ligand would be interested in acquiring certain royalty income streams held by Pfenex, including royalties payable under a Merck license agreement. Following execution of a non-disclosure agreement, discussions continued for approximately one month; the parties agreed not to pursue a transaction in June 2018.

Ligand re-initiated contact in late February 2020, when Todd Pettingill (Senior Director, Corporate Development) emailed Lucy. On March 5–6, 2020, Lucy held calls with Pettingill and Matt Korenberg (Ligand EVP Finance and CFO), in which Korenberg and Pettingill indicated that, subject to high-level diligence, Pfenex could be an attractive and realistic acquisition target for Ligand. They agreed the two companies' CEOs should eventually meet. On March 6, Lucy informed CEO Evert Schimmelpennink, who instructed Lucy to tell Ligand that Pfenex was not interested in further discussions.

Over the following weeks, Ligand made further attempts to continue discussions. Schimmelpennink repeatedly instructed Lucy to decline sharing non-public information. After Pettingill requested non-public information on March 30, April 16, and April 24, 2020, Lucy emailed Korenberg and Pettingill on April 27, suggesting that Ligand connect directly with Schimmelpennink. That same day, Korenberg emailed Schimmelpennink, stating that Ligand had conducted high-level diligence, outlined Ligand's acquisition track record, and expressed interest in exploring an acquisition of Pfenex.

On April 14, 2020, the FDA issued a General Advice Letter to Alvogen indicating that additional CUHF study data from experienced Forteo users would be required before a TE determination for PF708 could be made, potentially delaying the milestone by 9–12 months and reducing the maximum milestone from $20M to $7.5M. The stock declined from $8.94 (April 14) to $6.45 (April 15) on this news.

On April 29, 2020, Schimmelpennink and Korenberg held a telephonic conversation in which Schimmelpennink stated he believed in Pfenex's long-term strategy and that Pfenex needed to discuss the matter with the Board before proceeding further. Any initial Ligand valuation would need to be based on publicly available information.

On May 7, 2020, the Board held its regularly scheduled Q2 meeting. In executive session, Schimmelpennink informed the Board of Ligand's interest. The Board discussed strategic alternatives including future debt or equity financing, non-dilutive transactions, partnerships, and a potential sale. The Board determined to halt its inorganic growth strategy due to the TE Rating delay impact and related cash runway concerns.

On May 13, 2020, Pfenex received Ligand's first written non-binding IOI at $10.50–$12.50 per share cash, with a notation that Ligand would immediately terminate discussions if Pfenex commenced a competitive process. Closing price was $6.40 that day. The Board convened a special meeting on May 15, at which Wilson Sonsini presented fiduciary duties. The Board discussed the offer, assessed standalone risks (delayed TE Rating, dilutive capital raises, COVID-19 uncertainty), and concluded the higher end of the range was sufficient to engage but did not make a counter offer. The Board decided not to contact other potential acquirors, citing low probability of credible competing interest, leak risk, and the risk of Ligand terminating discussions. The Board formed the Strategic Transaction Committee (STC), consisting of Board Chairman Jason Grenfell-Gardner, Magda Marquet, and Phillip M. Schneider.

On May 18, the STC met with William Blair regarding engagement. William Blair disclosed its minority equity positions in both Pfenex and Ligand, confirmed firewalls were in place between advisory and investment teams, and confirmed it had not provided investment banking services to Ligand in the prior two years. The STC authorized William Blair's engagement.

On May 19, Schimmelpennink told Korenberg that Pfenex would not counter on price but would provide limited due diligence under a more restrictive CDA to encourage Ligand to improve its offer. Korenberg expressed Ligand's willingness to potentially increase the offer value. On May 20, Pfenex and Ligand executed a Confidential Disclosure Agreement with standstill provisions that would fall away in the event of a competing offer.

On May 26, Pfenex entered into a formal engagement letter with William Blair. On May 29, the STC, with William Blair and Wilson Sonsini, reviewed a list of 18 potential acquirors (7 pharma, 5 CDMO, 6 private equity). After extensive discussion weighing the risk of leakage, disruption, and the low probability of any party matching Ligand's offer given Pfenex's early-stage pipeline and out-licensed later-stage assets, the STC determined not to contact other potential acquirors. The STC directed management to conduct a management presentation with Ligand.

On June 4, Pfenex held a management presentation with Ligand, Schimmelpennink and Higgins both in attendance, during which Pfenex provided preliminary revenue and expense assumptions for its partnerships and out-licensed programs. On June 5, Pfenex opened a virtual data room to Ligand. On June 8, the Board confirmed the appropriateness of the revenue and expense assumptions presented to Ligand and directed management to develop a financial projections model.

On June 10, Pfenex received a revised IOI from Ligand at up to $14.00 per share ($12.00 + $2.00 CVR, TE Milestone by June 30, 2021). The STC determined it was insufficient. William Blair rejected it on June 11 but noted Pfenex's willingness to continue working with Ligand. On June 12, Alvogen launched Teriparatide Injection commercially (PF708/Bonsity). Closing price $9.05.

On June 15, Schimmelpennink and Higgins met in person. Schimmelpennink stated that Pfenex's long-term development strategy would be limited until Pfenex secured adequate financing to fund early-stage programs. On June 18, Ligand and Pfenex conducted diligence sessions on discovery platform capabilities and partnerships.

On June 22, Pfenex received a new IOI from Ligand at $13.65 per share, all cash. On June 24, the Board held a special meeting. William Blair presented a preliminary DCF analysis indicating implied equity value of $12.16–$14.07 per share. After reviewing standalone risks — including the TE Rating timeline, commercial launch risks, and the need to raise dilutive capital — the Board instructed William Blair to reject $13.65 and counter at $15.00 all-cash, while also noting that if no all-cash deal at $15.00 was achievable, the Board would consider $14.00 with a CVR.

On June 25, William Blair and Wilson Sonsini sent the first draft Merger Agreement to Ligand providing for a tender offer, fiduciary out, and flexibility for either all-cash or CVR structure. Separately, Schimmelpennink and Matthew Foehr (Ligand President and COO) met for a facility tour.

On July 1, Latham sent a revised Merger Agreement re-iterating $13.65 all-cash, described as Ligand's best offer. Pfenex rejected the offer. On July 4, William Blair told Barclays that Pfenex still required $15.00. Barclays re-confirmed $13.65. Schimmelpennink instructed William Blair to terminate discussions and close the data room.

Over the period July 4–14, multiple internal discussions took place within Pfenex and among STC members. The parties concluded that cutting off discussions had not compelled Ligand to improve its offer. The STC and management decided to re-engage at up to $14.00 per share.

On July 15, William Blair contacted Korenberg; Ligand indicated it was considering re-engaging and might be willing to consummate an acquisition at up to $14.00. On July 19, Schimmelpennink, Korenberg, and Foehr discussed Pfenex's business plans and latest FDA feedback.

On July 22, Ligand representatives informed Pfenex that Ligand's board had determined that $14.00 all-cash was unacceptable and that even the prior $13.65 was too high and no longer supportable. The STC instructed management to continue engaging and await a formal revised offer.

On July 28, the FDA provided feedback via General Advice Letter on the methodology for the new CUHF study required for a TE Rating. On July 29, Ligand verbally offered $12.00 + $2.00 CVR (TE Milestone). The Board held a special meeting, approved continuing negotiations at $12.00–$12.25 + $1.75–$2.00 CVR (total $14.00), confirmed management projection assumptions, and discussed standalone risks. Korenberg also noted that Ligand management might discuss $12.25 + $1.75 CVR with its board, but on July 30, Korenberg informed William Blair that Ligand's board did not approve that variant and re-confirmed $12.00 + $2.00 only.

On July 31, Higgins emailed Schimmelpennink re-affirming $12.00 + $2.00 CVR. Board members individually confirmed approval to proceed on that basis.

On August 1, Schimmelpennink and Higgins agreed to continue on the basis of $12.00 + $2.00 CVR with the TE Milestone deadline extended to December 31, 2021. Higgins confirmed Ligand had substantially completed diligence. Schimmelpennink, at STC direction, requested written confirmation.

On August 3, Ligand submitted a revised IOI at $12.00 + $2.00 CVR (TE Milestone by December 31, 2021). Ligand resumed diligence. Wilson Sonsini sent revised Merger Agreement and initial CVR Agreement to Latham.

From August 3 to 10, Pfenex and Ligand, with Wilson Sonsini and Latham, negotiated the terms of the Merger Agreement, CVR Agreement, and Support Agreement. Key negotiated terms included: closing conditions, fiduciary out provisions governing Pfenex's ability to consider unsolicited competing proposals, and the $17.5M termination fee payable by Pfenex to Ligand if Pfenex terminates to accept a superior proposal.

On August 5, the Board's regularly scheduled Q3 meeting confirmed approval to proceed. On August 6, Pfenex announced its Merck research and collaboration agreement on its earnings call. Closing price August 7 was $8.31. On August 10, the Board held a special meeting at which Wilson Sonsini presented fiduciary duties and reviewed material Merger Agreement terms. William Blair presented its financial analysis and rendered a verbal fairness opinion (later confirmed in writing) that the $12.00 cash + $2.00 CVR is fair from a financial point of view to the holders of Shares. The Board unanimously approved the transaction and resolved to recommend that stockholders accept the Offer. The Merger Agreement and Support Agreement were executed on August 10, 2020, following the Board meeting. A joint press release was issued that evening.

No other bidders or competing proposals are disclosed in the SC 14D9 at any point during the process.

## Rationale

The SC 14D9 states that the Board unanimously determined the Merger Agreement and transactions were advisable, fair to, and in the best interest of Pfenex and its stockholders. The filing identifies the following reasons cited by the Board.

The Board viewed the cash portion of $12.00 per share as representing a 56.7% premium to the pre-announcement closing price, with additional potential value of $2.00 per share via CVR if the TE Rating milestone is achieved. The Board considered this premium in the context of Pfenex's need to raise additional operating capital in the near-term following the FDA's April 2020 requirement for a new CUHF study, which reduced the anticipated revenue from PF708 sales (Bonsity) and eliminated the prospect of the larger near-term TE milestone payment (the maximum had declined from $20M to $7.5M). The Board stated it believed any future equity financing would be highly dilutive and might be available only on unfavorable terms.

The Board had conducted an assessment of potential alternative acquirors on multiple occasions — including on May 29, June 24, and July 29, 2020 — and each time determined, after discussion with William Blair, that given Pfenex's early-stage internal pipeline, its out-licensing of later-stage assets, and its current revenue profile, no other party would have credible or apparent interest in engaging at or near the price range proposed by Ligand.

The Board assessed risks of Pfenex as a standalone company, including: regulatory risk around the additional CUHF study and the uncertain timeline for a TE Rating; commercial risk around the Bonsity launch; the future need to raise capital in uncertain markets; and the impact of COVID-19 on capital markets and the business generally.

The Board also noted: that the transaction is not subject to a financing condition; that the Merger Agreement contains a customary fiduciary out allowing Pfenex to respond to unsolicited superior proposals; that the termination fee of $17.5M would be payable by Pfenex to Ligand only if Pfenex terminated to accept a superior proposal; and that the CVR milestone's achievability is primarily the responsibility of Alvogen, not Ligand, which limits Ligand's control over whether the CVR milestone is satisfied.

William Blair's fairness opinion was issued on August 10, 2020, based on, among other things, a DCF analysis of Pfenex's long-term management projections (previously reviewed and confirmed by the Board on June 8 and July 29, 2020), public company trading analysis, and precedent transactions analysis. The Board noted that the DCF analysis produced preliminary implied equity value of $12.16–$14.07 per share (presented at the June 24 meeting), and the Offer Price of up to $14.00 was within that range.

No pre-deal rumors exist for Pfenex Inc. (PFNX).

The acquisition of Pfenex by Ligand Pharmaceuticals was announced on **August 10, 2020**, and appears to have been a "clean" deal with no prior public reporting of a potential buyout, specific acquirers, or rumored price premiums. Financial news outlets and market analysts did not report on any specific takeover speculation or leaks leading up to the definitive agreement.

The stock's movement and news cycle prior to the announcement were focused on operational milestones, such as:
*   **June 2020:** The U.S. commercial launch of Teriparatide Injection.
*   **June 2020:** Positive European CHMP opinion for its biosimilar PF708.
*   **August 6, 2020:** The company's Q2 2020 earnings report, which occurred just four days before the deal was announced and contained no mention of a pending sale.

The deal was valued at $12.00 per share in cash plus a $2.00 Contingent Value Right (CVR), representing a 57% premium over the closing price on the day of the announcement. Following the announcement, several law firms (e.g., Halper Sadeh LLP, Levi & Korsinsky) launched standard investigations into the "adequacy of the price," but these were post-deal reactions rather than pre-deal rumors.
