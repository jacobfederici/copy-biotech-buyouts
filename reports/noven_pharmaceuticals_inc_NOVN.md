## Summary

Noven Pharmaceuticals entered into a definitive merger agreement with Hisamitsu Pharmaceutical Co., Inc. on July 14, 2009, under which Hisamitsu's subsidiary Northstar Merger Sub, Inc. launched a cash tender offer for all outstanding shares of Noven at $16.50 per share. The transaction, valued at approximately $361.93 million, aimed to combine Noven's transdermal technology platform and U.S. market presence with Hisamitsu's global operations. The offer successfully closed on August 20, 2009, with Hisamitsu acquiring a majority of the shares, followed by a second-step merger.

## Interaction History

Negotiations were led by Peter Brandt (Noven CEO) and Hirotaka Nakatomi (Hisamitsu CEO), supported by J.P. Morgan (Noven) and Lazard (Hisamitsu). Initial discussions focused on collaborative manufacturing but shifted to acquisition in March 2009. Hisamitsu's initial $14.00 bid was rejected as too low. Noven used a detailed management presentation on May 12-13 to argue for higher value, leading to a $15.50 bid. Noven countered at $17.00, eventually settling at $16.50 after Hisamitsu demanded exclusivity. A late-stage attempt by Noven to raise the price following positive Mesafem Phase 2 data was rebuffed by Hisamitsu, which instead offered better 'deal certainty' by assuming the risk of generic challenges to Noven's products. The Board prioritized this certainty given the risks to the Novogyne joint venture and the Daytrana product line.

## Rationale

The Noven Board recommended the deal due to significant near-term risks, including potential generic competition for key products and technical issues with Daytrana. They noted the company's pipeline was early-stage and would not provide significant cash flow for several years. For Hisamitsu, the acquisition provided a transdermal technology platform and a strategic entry point into the U.S. pharmaceutical market.
