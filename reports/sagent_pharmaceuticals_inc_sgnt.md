## Summary

Sagent Pharmaceuticals, Inc., a Delaware corporation and NASDAQ-listed generic injectable pharmaceutical company headquartered in Schaumburg, Illinois, was acquired by Nichi-Iko Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., a Japanese pharmaceutical company, through a cash tender offer at $21.75 per share. The merger agreement was signed on July 10, 2016, following a competitive multi-party auction process begun in early 2016 that evaluated proposals from at least five parties (identified as Party A, Party B, Party C, Party D, and Nichi-Iko). Purchaser Shepard Vision, Inc. commenced the tender offer on August 1, 2016. As of the expiration on August 26, 2016, approximately 85.6% of outstanding shares had been tendered. The merger was effected on August 29, 2016 pursuant to Section 251(h) of the DGCL without a stockholder vote. Sagent was delisted from NASDAQ prior to market open on August 30, 2016 and became a wholly owned subsidiary of Nichi-Iko.

## Interaction History

### Early Strategic Review and Advisor Engagements (August 2014 – March 2015)

In August 2014, the Sagent Board formed the Strategic Committee, a standing committee of three independent directors, to facilitate the review and evaluation of strategic opportunities. In October 2014, Sagent engaged Perella Weinberg Partners LP as financial advisor to assist the Board and management in identifying and evaluating strategic alternatives; in February 2015, Sagent further engaged Morgan Stanley & Co. LLC in connection with evaluating potential strategic alternatives. Both firms had prior relationships with Sagent from earlier engagements.

In December 2014, a large strategic competitor majority-owned by a financial sponsor approached then-Chairman and CEO Jeffrey Yordon about a potential stock-for-stock acquisition of Sagent. The parties engaged in preliminary discussions during late January and February 2015 but mutually determined to terminate discussions around mid- to late March 2015. The filing does not identify this party by name or disclose the terms of any proposal.

In late March 2015, Yordon announced his retirement. The Board conducted a CEO search, culminating in August 2015 with the appointment of Allan Oberman as CEO and a Board member.

### Party A Discussions (Spring 2015 – July 2016)

In Spring 2015, a Chinese-based pharmaceutical company (identified only as "Party A" in the filing), with whom Sagent had prior discussions under a February 2014 confidentiality agreement, contacted a then-senior executive of Sagent to express interest in a business combination. The executive communicated this to the Board. Between July and September 2015, Party A submitted multiple non-binding expressions of interest at indicative equity valuation ranges reflecting a premium to the then-current trading price, but noted that a final valuation was subject to due diligence. Party A proposed using a new special purpose entity owned by a consortium including Party A and certain equity financing sources.

At the September 30, 2015 Board meeting, the Board discussed the non-U.S. listed international pharmaceutical company that had been in discussions during Summer 2015 (through a series of meetings and calls) and determined not to pursue that transaction. The Board also discussed Party A and agreed to direct management to engage further with Party A, allow data room access subject to a confidentiality agreement, and seek clarity on committed financing and creditworthy backstop.

On October 7, 2015, senior management and Morgan Stanley representatives met in Schaumburg with Party A senior management and its financial advisor for a preliminary due diligence meeting. On October 26, 2015, Sagent and Party A entered into a confidentiality agreement with standstill.

On November 21, 2015, Party A submitted a non-binding proposal to acquire Sagent for an equity value of $900 million (~$27.45/share), contingent on six weeks of confirmatory due diligence and completion of financing through a combination of private equity funds and bank loans, with a 60-day exclusivity requirement. On December 4, 2015, the Board held a special meeting at which Kirkland reviewed fiduciary duties and the Board discussed the proposal. The Board continued to be concerned about Party A's absence of committed financing and lack of a creditworthy backstop, but determined to allow Party A data room access and an in-person management presentation, while instructing Morgan Stanley to tell Party A to provide a revised expression demonstrating committed financing.

In mid-December 2015, Sagent senior management and advisors met in New York with Party A senior management and advisors over several days for an in-person management presentation; Party A was given data room access. On December 22, 2015, Board Chairman Robert Flanagan met with Party A senior representatives in New York.

On February 3, 2016, Party A submitted a non-binding proposal for equity value of $850 million (~$25.53/share), requiring 60-day exclusivity.

On March 24, 2016, Party A submitted a non-binding proposal for equity value of $675 million (~$20.32/share), proposing to finance through a combination of debt and equity. Party A offered a letter of credit to support a reverse termination fee and proposed a 30-day exclusivity period.

On April 6, 2016, Party A submitted a letter adding details: a $47.25 million reverse termination fee (~7% of equity value), backed by a letter of credit, payable in certain circumstances excluding CFIUS failure; required employment agreement negotiations with key management with waivers of change-of-control benefits. The Board's concerns about Party A's closing certainty risk remained throughout—absent committed financing, potential Chinese regulatory approvals, CFIUS risk, and proposed single-step merger structure—and these concerns are cited throughout the interaction history as the primary basis for the Board's skepticism about Party A relative to Nichi-Iko.

At the April 7, 2016 Strategic Committee meeting, the Committee determined not to pursue Party C further, assessed the competitive process, and authorized Perella Weinberg and Morgan Stanley to contact potential parties in a process designed to identify the best available transaction.

On June 20, 2016, Party A submitted a proposal for equity value of $651 million (~$19.45/share) with equity commitment letters, and an improved merger agreement: reverse termination fee of 4%, strengthened regulatory obligations, dissenter's rights condition at 10%, and arbitration rights.

On June 30, 2016, Party A submitted a revised proposal at equity value of $660 million (~$19.62/share): termination fee reduced to 3.9%, reverse termination fee increased to 6%, broader arbitration, no dissenters' rights condition, and removal of management employment agreement signing requirement.

On July 4, 2016, Party A submitted a new proposal at equity value of $725 million (~$21.50/share) with reverse termination fee of 10%. Party A sent a letter on July 7 offering its Chairman to meet in New York but without indication of willingness to improve price or terms. Party A's most recent offer of $21.50/share, with inferior deal certainty compared to Nichi-Iko's $21.75 offer, was the basis for the Board's final comparison on July 10.

### Nichi-Iko Engagement (January 2016 – July 10, 2016)

In late January 2016, Eiichi Machida, a senior executive adviser of Nichi-Iko, contacted a representative of Perella Weinberg, having seen the January 2016 Reuters news report about Sagent's potential exploration of a sale. Mr. Machida expressed Nichi-Iko's interest in a business transaction but made no specific proposal. Perella Weinberg told Machida that contrary to the report, Sagent was not running a sale process; Mr. Flanagan was informed.

On March 15, 2016, Mr. Machida sent an email to Perella Weinberg expressing that Nichi-Iko CEO Yuichi Tamura was interested in acquiring a majority stake of Sagent for cash, without specifying a price, and expressing confidence about financing availability.

On April 6, 2016, Machida met with Flanagan and Perella Weinberg at Perella Weinberg's offices in New York. Flanagan told Machida that Sagent would only be interested in an acquisition of the entire company at a significant premium to the current market price. At the conclusion, Machida said he would seek additional guidance from Nichi-Iko's senior management. On April 7, 2016, Flanagan and Perella Weinberg met again with Machida at Kirkland's offices; Machida confirmed Nichi-Iko was interested in proceeding on the full-company acquisition terms described by Flanagan.

Beginning in early to mid-April 2016, at the Strategic Committee's instruction, Perella Weinberg and Morgan Stanley notified Nichi-Iko and Party A, among others, that their interest would be considered in connection with a formal process with a binding offer deadline of April 20, 2016. On April 20, Nichi-Iko indicated it expected to offer between $18.00 and $20.00/share.

On April 30, 2016, Sagent and Nichi-Iko entered into a confidentiality agreement with standstill.

On May 3, 2016, Flanagan and Nichi-Iko representatives including Tamura held an introductory meeting in New York at Perella Weinberg's offices; JP Morgan (Nichi-Iko's financial advisor) and Paul Weiss (Nichi-Iko's U.S. counsel) also attended. On May 17, 2016, senior management of Sagent provided Nichi-Iko a management presentation in the Chicago area; non-risk-adjusted forecasts and underlying assumptions were shared.

In early to mid-June 2016, Nichi-Iko provided a revised draft merger agreement adopting the tender offer merger structure. Kirkland informed Paul Weiss that Nichi-Iko's draft increased deal conditionality in an unacceptable manner and gave guidance on expected changes including eliminating third-party consents and licenses conditions, lowering the termination fee, and strengthening Nichi-Iko's regulatory obligations.

On June 23, 2016, Nichi-Iko submitted a proposal at $18.00/share with an improved merger agreement: eliminating the third-party consents and licenses condition, reducing the termination fee from 5% to 3.5%, and strengthening regulatory obligations. It also requested a 21-day exclusivity period.

On June 24, 2016, the Strategic Committee reviewed the proposals. Perella Weinberg conveyed JP Morgan's articulated reasons for Nichi-Iko's inability to reach $20/share, including concerns about achievability of non-risk-adjusted forecasts and the $40M Teva ANDA acquisition. The Strategic Committee determined to instruct advisors to tell both Nichi-Iko and Party A to provide their best and final offers by June 30. Nichi-Iko suggested it would be better able to improve if granted a 30-day exclusivity period, which Sagent declined.

On June 30, 2016, Nichi-Iko submitted a proposal at $20.00/share with the same merger agreement terms but indicating a willingness to cooperate further, and requesting a 14-day exclusivity period. Nichi-Iko representatives told Perella Weinberg that $20/share was final.

On July 1, 2016, the Strategic Committee determined to inform Nichi-Iko that Sagent would work toward finalizing a definitive agreement but could not agree to formal exclusivity, and to tell Party A it could be working with another bidder but should consider improvements without assuming an opportunity to rebid. Perella Weinberg arranged a dinner between Nichi-Iko and Sagent management in Chicago for July 5 and a due diligence meeting for July 6.

On July 4, 2016, following Party A's new proposal at ~$21.50/share with a 10% reverse termination fee, Perella Weinberg contacted Machida and informed him Sagent had received a higher proposal and Nichi-Iko would likely need to improve its price meaningfully.

On July 5, Oberman, Singer, Machida, and one other Nichi-Iko senior executive met in Chicago with Perella Weinberg to discuss Nichi-Iko's strategic rationale for the transaction. No compensation arrangement terms were discussed. On July 6, Sagent management provided an updated management presentation to Nichi-Iko including business updates on delays in certain product approvals; due diligence was completed. JP Morgan confirmed to Perella Weinberg that Nichi-Iko had completed its due diligence and was targeting signing within approximately three weeks.

On July 6, the Strategic Committee discussed how to most effectively guide Nichi-Iko to improve its price, and also discussed the benefits and risks of further engagement with Party A. That same day, Kirkland sent Paul Weiss a revised merger agreement reducing the termination fee from 3.5% to 3%, permitting Sagent to waive standstills for unsolicited alternative proposals, and confirming no financing condition.

On July 7, Perella Weinberg told Nichi-Iko it must meaningfully improve its price given the higher competing bid. In the Kirkland-Paul Weiss negotiation teleconference, Paul Weiss communicated that Nichi-Iko would accept reducing the termination fee to 3.3% but was insisting Sagent could not waive standstills for parties to make unsolicited takeover proposals. Paul Weiss stated that Nichi-Iko's position was that parties who had been instructed to provide their best and final offer should not be permitted to re-enter the process after signing. The Board held a special meeting that day and discussed the open issues including the timing to close under each bidder's proposed structure. Director Shlomo Yanai's role as independent advisor to Perella Weinberg was noted; each director confirmed no relationship that would affect disinterestedness. Party A's July 7 letter offered a Chairman meeting but without a willingness to improve price.

On July 8, the Board held a special meeting. Oberman and Singer presented the risk-adjusted Company Projections (2016–2020), noting key differences from the prior non-risk-adjusted forecasts including product launch delays and risk adjustments from recent regulatory developments. The Board determined these represented the best currently available estimate of standalone financial performance and authorized their use by the financial advisors. The Board also reviewed and approved retention programs for senior management and key revenue-producing personnel. The Board discussed Nichi-Iko's continued insistence on the no-standstill-waiver provision and agreed to reconvene when Nichi-Iko submitted a revised proposal, expected the next day. Flanagan contacted the Supporting Stockholders on a confidential basis about the Nichi-Iko discussions and the support agreement requirement; the Supporting Stockholders spoke with Singer and Perella Weinberg. By the end of July 8 negotiations, substantially all merger agreement terms were resolved except the standstill-waiver issue.

On the morning of July 10, 2016, Nichi-Iko submitted a proposal for $21.75/share on the terms of the merger agreement, including the no-standstill-waiver position, with an expiration that afternoon. The Board convened immediately with Morgan Stanley, Perella Weinberg, and Kirkland. The Board reviewed the Nichi-Iko $21.75 proposal and compared it to Party A's most recent $21.50 proposal. Perella Weinberg provided an update on Nichi-Iko discussions; Morgan Stanley summarized the Party A position, noting Party A had not improved its price since July 4. Kirkland presented fiduciary duties and compared the Nichi-Iko and Party A merger agreement terms, including greater closing certainty with Nichi-Iko (no Chinese regulatory approvals, no CFIUS risk, no dissenter's rights condition, tender offer structure, absence of financing condition). Perella Weinberg and Morgan Stanley each rendered oral fairness opinions (confirmed in writing later that day) that $21.75/share was fair from a financial point of view to common stockholders. The Board discussed whether to accept Nichi-Iko's offer, continue negotiations with either party, or not pursue a transaction at the time. Following discussion, the Board unanimously approved the Merger Agreement, declared the transaction advisable, and recommended that stockholders accept the Offer and tender shares. On the evening of July 10, Nichi-Iko's board of directors approved the Merger Agreement; all parties executed the Merger Agreement and the Tender and Support Agreement.

### Board Conflict Note

Director Shlomo Yanai serves as an independent advisor to Perella Weinberg. The Board discussed this relationship at the July 7, 2016 Board meeting; each director confirmed no relationships with Party A or Nichi-Iko or any financial or legal advisors that would affect their disinterestedness. The filing does not indicate that Yanai recused from any vote.

### Supporting Stockholders

## Rationale

The Board identified the following considerations in favor of the transaction, sourced from the SC 14D9 (accession 0001193125-16-665570), Item 4:

The Board cited the risks of remaining independent, including the regulatory approval process for pipeline products (risk of non-approval or delay), risks related to successfully developing and manufacturing products, and general market conditions that could reduce share value. The Board noted that the consideration consisted entirely of cash, providing liquidity and certainty of value. The Board considered the Offer Price represented a 40% premium to the July 8, 2016 closing price, a 48% premium to the 30-day VWAP, a 67% premium to the 90-day VWAP, and a 37% premium to the 52-week VWAP. The Board concluded that the process conducted—contacting eight pharmaceutical companies and two financial sponsors, receiving indications of interest, conducting management presentations, soliciting binding proposals, and negotiating with multiple parties—demonstrated adequate market canvassing and that no party was prepared to offer a higher price at lower closing risk than Nichi-Iko.

The Board also considered that Party A's most recent proposal of ~$21.50/share was lower than Nichi-Iko's $21.75/share and carried greater closing risk (Chinese governmental approvals, CFIUS risk, no financing condition commitment, single-step merger structure resulting in a longer expected closing timeline, and proposed dissenter's rights condition). The Board weighed the terms of the Merger Agreement, including the absence of a financing condition, Nichi-Iko's obligation to use reasonable best efforts to obtain regulatory approvals (limited to HSR filing), the structure as a short-form tender offer under Section 251(h) DGCL (expected ~two months to close versus ~four to five months for Party A's single-step structure), the $24.3 million termination fee (~3.3% of equity value), and the Board's retained ability to respond to unsolicited bona fide superior proposals despite the no-standstill-waiver provision.

Both Perella Weinberg and Morgan Stanley rendered fairness opinions on July 10, 2016, that the $21.75 cash per share consideration was fair from a financial point of view. Perella Weinberg's DCF analysis using the Company Projections yielded an implied per share equity reference range of approximately $20.07 to $23.27. Morgan Stanley's DCF analysis yielded an implied range of $17.50 to $23.50.

The Board's stated negative considerations included the loss of stockholder participation in future earnings and growth, the risk of non-completion and associated disruption, operational restrictions during the pendency period, the no-solicitation provision and $24.3 million termination fee, potential adverse market perception from announcement, tax consequences of the cash consideration, and potential differing interests of directors and officers.
