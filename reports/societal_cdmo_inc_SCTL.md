## Summary

Societal CDMO, Inc. was acquired by CoreRx, Inc., a portfolio company of QHP Capital, L.P., for $1.10 per share in an all-cash tender offer followed by a merger. The transaction aimed to combine Societal's CDMO capabilities with CoreRx's existing operations to create a more robust service provider in the small molecule pharmaceutical space.

## Interaction History

Negotiations began in late 2023 after SCTL initiated a strategic review of its San Diego facility. Raymond James introduced SCTL CEO David Enloe to QHP Capital partners Ashton Poole and Jeffrey Edwards in October 2023. Initial proposals from Parent in January 2024 included a 'rollover' component which the SCTL Board rejected. Parent subsequently moved to an all-cash offer. SCTL also received lower-value bids from 'Party A' ($0.60-$0.70) and 'Party B' ($0.42). On January 18, 2024, Parent lowered its bid from $1.20 to $1.10 citing transaction expenses and capitalization diligence. SCTL entered exclusivity on January 19. Final negotiations focused on the termination fee (settled at $5 million) and the 'Expense Reimbursement Obligation' which SCTL successfully removed from the final agreement. The SCTL Board approved the $1.10 offer on February 25, 2024.

## Rationale

The SCTL Board determined the cash offer provided immediate liquidity and certainty of value, mitigating risks associated with SCTL's standalone plan, including cash requirements, debt covenant compliance, and the need for dilutive future financing. CoreRx sought to expand its CDMO footprint and capabilities.

## Evidence

- On April 8, 2024, CoreRx, Inc... completed the previously announced acquisition of Societal CDMO, Inc. (0001193125-24-089300)
- The SCTL Board unanimously recommends that SCTL’s shareholders accept the Offer and tender their Shares to Purchaser (0001193125-24-064340)

## Decision Notes

The deal was completed via a tender offer under Section 321(f) of the PBCL. Shares outstanding taken from the 14D-9 filing dated March 11, 2024. Fully diluted equity value of $130M is based on the Raymond James fairness opinion assumptions.
