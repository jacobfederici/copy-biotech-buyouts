## Summary

On December 11, 2017, Repros Therapeutics Inc. entered into an Agreement and Plan of Merger with Allergan Sales, LLC, a wholly-owned subsidiary of Allergan plc, and Celestial Merger Sub, Inc. Pursuant to the agreement, Celestial Merger Sub commenced a tender offer to acquire all outstanding shares of Repros common stock for $0.67 per share in cash. The tender offer expired on January 29, 2018, with approximately 60.80% of shares validly tendered. The transaction closed on January 31, 2018 via a short-form merger under Section 251(h) of the DGCL, resulting in Repros becoming a wholly-owned subsidiary of Allergan. The total consideration paid for the outstanding shares was approximately $26.6 million.

## Interaction History

Beginning in early 2016, members of Repros management engaged in preliminary conversations with representatives of Allergan regarding a potential transaction involving a license to Allergan of Proellex. In April 2017, the parties recommenced discussions and executed a mutual non-disclosure agreement. In early June 2017, Dr. Dillaha (Repros) and an Allergan representative met at the BIO CEO & Investor conference to discuss potential transactions at a high level.

Over the summer, Repros also engaged in preliminary discussions with several other companies (referred to as Parties A, B, C, D, and E in the filings), but none resulted in an offer. On September 1, 2017, Repros received from Allergan a non-binding expression of interest to acquire 100% of the equity interests in Repros for an aggregate equity value of $18 million (approximately $0.45 per share). On September 5, 2017, the Repros Board met, with representatives of Morgan Lewis, and deemed the $18 million offer insufficient. The Board discussed strategic options and approved the engagement of Stifel as a financial advisor.

On September 19, 2017, Stifel communicated a counteroffer to Allergan for a transaction involving an exclusive license to Proellex for $45 million upfront and a 3% royalty. On September 29, 2017, Allergan submitted a revised expression of interest to acquire Repros for $25 million (approximately $0.66 per share).

On October 4, 2017, the Repros Board met with Stifel and Morgan Lewis and determined to seek a higher purchase price as well as additional value relating to enclomiphene. Stifel communicated a counter-offer to Allergan proposing an aggregate equity value of $30 million plus additional consideration, such as a CVR, relating to enclomiphene. On October 5, 2017, Allergan expressed a willingness to increase their offer to $30 million but indicated they would not provide any additional consideration. After considering the certainty of closure and risks of pursuing other structures, the Repros Board unanimously agreed on October 6, 2017, to pursue the transaction at the $30 million aggregate equity value.

From mid-October through early December, legal counsels for both parties (Morgan Lewis for Repros, Covington for Allergan) negotiated the merger agreement. Key points of negotiation included the treatment of outstanding warrants, the size of the termination fee, and the ability of Repros to solicit alternative transactions. Allergan insisted on retiring all warrants at closing and rejected a go-shop provision, ultimately agreeing on a $1.0 million termination fee for an unsolicited alternative transaction. Concurrently, Repros negotiated with warrant holders to amend the warrants to permit repurchase at closing.

On December 11, 2017, the Repros Board held a meeting. Stifel delivered its oral opinion, subsequently confirmed in writing, that the $0.67 per share consideration was fair from a financial point of view. By unanimous vote, the Repros Board approved and declared advisable the merger agreement. The merger agreement was executed later that day, and the transaction was announced on the morning of December 12, 2017.

## Rationale

The Repros Board cited the following reasons for recommending the transaction: the Company had not previously launched a commercial drug and faced risks associated with clinical trials, regulatory approvals (FDA and EMA), and commercial infrastructure. The Company did not generate sufficient cash and would require significant additional funds through dilutive equity financings. Following intensive arm's-length negotiations, the Board believed it had obtained the highest price per share Allergan was willing to pay. The all-cash consideration provided certainty and immediate value, representing an approximately 42.6% premium to the December 11, 2017 closing price. The Board also noted the lack of third-party approvals or financing conditions, and considered Stifel's fairness opinion.
