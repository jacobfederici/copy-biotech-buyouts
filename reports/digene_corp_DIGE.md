## Summary

Digene Corporation entered into a definitive merger agreement with QIAGEN N.V. on June 3, 2007, following a competitive strategic review process. The transaction was structured as a cash and stock tender offer for all outstanding shares, followed by a second-step merger. The consideration was fixed at $61.25 per share or 3.545 QIAGEN shares, prorated to ensure an aggregate mix of 55% cash and 45% stock. The deal closed on July 30, 2007, making Digene an indirect wholly owned subsidiary of QIAGEN.

## Interaction History

Negotiations began in early 2007 following a strategic planning process initiated by Digene's new CEO, Daryl Faulkner. QIAGEN, an existing OEM supplier for Digene, expressed interest in March 2007. After a mutual NDA was signed on April 5, 2007, QIAGEN submitted a non-binding proposal on April 30. Digene's financial advisor, JPMorgan, conducted a market check by contacting 11 other companies; while one company submitted a preliminary cash bid of $58-$63, they withdrew on May 31. QIAGEN refined its offer multiple times, eventually settling on $61.25 per share with a 55% cash and 45% stock mix. The Digene Board, advised by Ballard Spahr (legal) and JPMorgan (financial), focused on the lack of a collar for the stock portion and the proration mechanics. On June 2, 2007, the Board met in Philadelphia, received a fairness opinion from JPMorgan, and unanimously approved the deal.

## Rationale

The Digene Board recommended the deal based on the enhanced ability to grow the HPV and women's health business through QIAGEN's global reach and sample processing technologies. The combination was expected to create cost and revenue synergies by merging R&D teams and sales channels. For stockholders, the deal provided a significant premium and the option to participate in the future growth of the combined company through the stock component.

## Evidence

- At a meeting held on June 2, 2007, the Digene Board unanimously: (1) approved and declared advisable the Merger Agreement... (2) recommended acceptance of the Offer (SC 14D9, Item 4).
- On July 30, 2007, Digene Corporation ('Digene') merged with and into Energy Merger Sub, Inc., a wholly owned subsidiary of QIAGEN N.V. (8-K, 2007-07-31).

## Buyout Rumors

Source: OpenRouter web search (Gemini 3 Flash Preview). Pre-deal rumors only.

The acquisition of Digene Corporation (DIGE) by Qiagen N.V. was officially announced on **June 3, 2007**. While the deal was described by executives as a "merger of friendly neighbors" due to a decade-long partnership, there were several instances of market speculation and historical takeover attempts prior to the final agreement.

The following is a chronological timeline of buyout rumors, acquisition speculation, and failed takeover attempts for Digene Corporation:

*   **June 2002**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** Federal Trade Commission (FTC) / Various Financial News
    *   **Rumored/Actual Acquirer:** Cytyc Corporation
    *   **Rumored Price:** Approximately $554 million (stock-for-stock)
    *   **Details:** Cytyc Corporation launched a formal bid to acquire Digene. However, the deal was blocked by the FTC on antitrust grounds, as it would have combined the dominant provider of Pap tests (Cytyc) with the only provider of DNA-based HPV tests (Digene), potentially stifling competition in cervical cancer screening.
*   **Late 2006 – Early 2007**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** General Market Speculation / Analyst Reports (e.g., Baker 2006)
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Roche Holding AG
    *   **Rumored Price:** Not specified
    *   **Details:** Speculation circulated that Roche was a natural buyer for Digene. Roche held a cross-licensing agreement with Digene for HPV technology and was actively developing its own competitive HPV test. Analysts noted that Digene’s "molecular monopoly" on FDA-approved HPV testing made it a prime target for larger diagnostics companies like Roche or Hologic looking to consolidate the women's health market.
*   **May 2007**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** Options Market Activity / Trading Floor Rumors
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Unspecified "Strategic Buyer" (later confirmed as Qiagen)
    *   **Rumored Price:** Not specified; stock saw increased volatility
    *   **Details:** In the weeks leading up to the June announcement, there was noted "unusual" activity in Digene’s call options and a steady rise in share price. Market observers attributed this to renewed speculation that the company was finally "in play" following the retirement of its long-time founders (Evan Jones and Charles Fleischman) in 2006, which was seen as removing a major barrier to a sale.
*   **June 3, 2007 (Pre-Announcement Speculation)**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** 24/7 Wall St. / TheDeal.com
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Qiagen N.V.
    *   **Rumored Price:** $61.25 per share
    *   **Details:** Just prior to the official press release, rumors solidified around Qiagen as the buyer. The reports noted that Qiagen already manufactured the "Rapid Capture System" used by Digene, making the two companies "technological partners for more than a decade" and the most logical merger candidates.
