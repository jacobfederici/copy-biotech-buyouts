## Summary

Avanir Pharmaceuticals entered into a definitive agreement to be acquired by Otsuka Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd. for $17.00 per share in cash via a tender offer followed by a second-step merger under Section 251(h) of the DGCL. The transaction was valued at approximately $3.5 billion on a fully diluted basis and aimed to expand Otsuka's central nervous system portfolio.

## Timeline

- 2013-05-13: Avanir and Otsuka enter into a mutual non-disclosure agreement.
- 2014-01-30: Otsuka suspends discussions due to internal factors.
- 2014-07-01: Otsuka proposes to resume discussions and expresses interest in an acquisition.
- 2014-09-15: Avanir announces positive Phase II results for AVP-923 in Alzheimer's agitation.
- 2014-10-16: Otsuka submits a written non-binding proposal to acquire Avanir for $15.00 per share in cash.
- 2014-10-28: Otsuka verbally increases its proposal to $16.25 per share.
- 2014-10-30: Otsuka verbally increases its proposal to $17.00 per share.
- 2014-11-26: Avanir announces that AVP-825 was not approved by the FDA (Complete Response Letter).
- 2014-12-01: Avanir Board unanimously approves the Merger Agreement at $17.00 per share.
- 2014-12-02: Public announcement of the transaction.
- 2014-12-12: Tender offer formally commences.
- 2015-01-12: Tender offer expires at midnight.
- 2015-01-13: Merger completed; Avanir becomes a wholly owned subsidiary of Otsuka.

## Interaction History

Negotiations began in May 2013 regarding a licensing deal but shifted toward an acquisition in mid-2014. Keith Katkin (CEO of Avanir) led negotiations with Otsuka's representatives. Otsuka initially offered $15.00 per share on October 16, 2014. Avanir's board, advised by Centerview Partners and Latham & Watkins, conducted a targeted market check with three other parties (Parties C, D, and E). Party D declined interest, Party C withdrew after diligence, and Party E remained unresponsive. Concurrently, Avanir explored acquiring 'Party A' in an all-stock deal as a standalone alternative. Otsuka raised its bid to $16.25 on October 28 and finally to $17.00 on October 30. Final negotiations focused on the termination fee, which was reduced from 3.5% to 2.57% ($90 million). The board approved the deal on December 1, 2014, following the failure of the AVP-825 FDA approval and a demand from Party A for a higher acquisition price.

## Rationale

The Avanir Board determined the cash offer provided certainty of value and a significant premium (48% over the unaffected price) compared to the risks of remaining independent. These risks included the need for significant capital for clinical trials, regulatory uncertainties (highlighted by the AVP-825 CRL), and the challenges of commercializing products in Europe. Otsuka sought to strengthen its CNS portfolio and leverage Avanir's commercial infrastructure and Phase III-ready assets.

## Evidence

- On December 1, 2014, the Company Board unanimously: (i) determined that the Offer and the Merger are in the best interests of the Company and its stockholders... (iii) resolved to recommend that the Company’s stockholders accept the Offer (0001193125-14-437003).
- On January 13, 2015, Parent completed its acquisition of the Company by consummating the Merger without a meeting of stockholders... in accordance with Section 251(h) (0001193125-15-008886).
