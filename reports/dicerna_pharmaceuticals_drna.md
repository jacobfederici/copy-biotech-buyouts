## Summary

Novo Nordisk A/S, through its wholly owned indirect Delaware subsidiary NNUS New Research, Inc., acquired all outstanding shares of Dicerna Pharmaceuticals, Inc. at $38.25 per share in cash pursuant to a tender offer and subsequent short-form merger under DGCL Section 251(h). Novo had a pre-existing collaboration and equity relationship with Dicerna dating to November 2019 and had sought to expand that relationship beginning in August 2021. Dicerna's senior management declined a proposed collaboration expansion, after which Novo indicated interest in an acquisition. Novo submitted four successive proposals at $32.50, $35.75, $38.00, and ultimately $38.25 per share between October 7 and November 17, 2021. A limited parallel process engaged two unnamed global pharmaceutical companies; one passed and one indicated interest only up to $33.00 per share. The Dicerna Board unanimously approved the Merger Agreement on November 17, 2021. The tender offer expired on December 27, 2021, with approximately 82.6% of shares tendered. The merger was completed on December 28, 2021, after which Dicerna continued as a wholly owned subsidiary of Novo and was delisted from Nasdaq. The deal consideration represented an approximately 80% premium to Dicerna's closing price of $21.28 on November 17, 2021.

## Interaction History

Beginning in August 2021, Novo approached Dicerna regarding what the SC 14D9 describes as a "potential significant expansion of their relationship" beyond the 2019 Novo Collaboration Agreement. Novo was seeking a new collaboration and licensing agreement around Dicerna's GalXC Plus technology and joint development to improve the GalXC Plus Platform (the "Novo Expansion"). Dicerna's senior management declined to pursue the Novo Expansion, determining it was not in Dicerna's strategic interests. Between August and September 2021, members of Dicerna's senior management discussed Novo's interest with the Dicerna Board.

Later in September 2021, Novo indicated to Dicerna that, absent an expanded collaboration, it was interested in exploring a potential acquisition. At the regularly scheduled Dicerna Board meetings on September 21-22, 2021, which included SVB Leerink and Skadden as invited attendees, SVB Leerink presented preliminary illustrative financial analyses and Skadden reviewed fiduciary duties. The Board discussed Novo's interest and the potential desirability of a strategic transaction in light of Dicerna's standalone prospects and risks.

On October 7, 2021, Lars Jørgensen, CEO of Novo, called Dr. Douglas M. Fambrough III, President and CEO of Dicerna, to discuss an acquisition. That afternoon Jørgensen submitted a letter with a non-binding proposal to acquire all outstanding Dicerna common stock at $32.50 per share in cash, subject to due diligence and other conditions (the October 7 Proposal). The same evening, the Dicerna Board met with senior management, Centerview Partners (which the filing identifies as a financial advisor alongside SVB Leerink), Skadden, and Goodwin Procter. The Board reviewed the proposal and authorized management to proceed with financial analysis work with advisors and to consider the possibility of contacting additional parties.

Between October 11 and 15, 2021, at the direction of Board members, Dr. Fambrough and Centerview contacted Company A and Company B, two unnamed global pharmaceutical companies. Company B responded on October 15 that it was not interested. On October 18, the Board met and was informed Company A had not communicated. The Board reviewed updated management financial projections prepared for the RNAi platform in addition to Dicerna's programs and received updated financial analyses from Centerview and SVB Leerink. The Board concluded the October 7 Proposal was not acceptable and directed Dr. Fambrough to advise Novo the proposal was insufficient but Dicerna would be open to a proposal providing appropriate recognition of its value.

On October 19, Dr. Fambrough called Jørgensen and communicated that $32.50 was not sufficiently compelling. Jørgensen said Novo would consider an increase.

On October 21, Novo submitted a revised proposal at $35.75 per share (October 21 Proposal). At the October 22 Board meeting, the Board discussed the revised proposal and Company A's upcoming call. Skadden summarized key terms of a proposed NDA with Novo. The Board determined to proceed with Novo and provide due diligence access, but only if Novo increased its offer to at least $38.00 per share. The Board decided against contacting additional parties, weighing confidentiality risks and the likelihood that a broader process could undermine Novo's interest or cause disruption.

On October 25, Dr. Fambrough called Jørgensen with the $38.00 threshold. Jørgensen acknowledged Novo had stated its belief that $35.75 fully valued the Company, but agreed to move to $38.00 in the interest of completing the transaction expeditiously and without delay; Jørgensen also expressed a desire to close before year-end.

On October 26, Jørgensen called Dr. Fambrough again and stated Novo would commit to $38.00 if Dicerna would agree to four weeks of exclusivity. Dr. Fambrough told Jørgensen the Board was unlikely to accept the exclusivity request and would discuss it. That same day, Dr. Fambrough spoke with Company A's executive, who indicated Company A potentially had interest in an acquisition. Centerview spoke separately with Company A representatives, who said Company A expected to respond later that day.

On October 27, the Board met and decided Dicerna should not provide Novo the requested exclusivity but should provide due diligence access without it. Dr. Fambrough communicated this to Novo. Later that day, Jørgensen submitted a revised proposal at $38.00 per share that did not include the exclusivity request (October 27 Proposal). Separately, Company A indicated its interest to both Centerview and Dr. Fambrough. Skadden sent Company A's counsel an initial draft NDA. Novo also sent Dicerna a draft NDA; on October 27-28, Dicerna signed separate NDAs with Novo (containing a standstill) and Company A (no standstill). On October 28, Skadden sent Davis Polk (Novo's counsel) an initial draft Merger Agreement.

On October 29, Dicerna opened a virtual data room for Novo's due diligence review. Novo and its advisors participated in calls and in-person meetings with Dicerna management. That same day, Dicerna provided separate management presentations to both Novo and Company A by teleconference.

On November 5, Company A provided a verbal indication at $29.00 per share. Dr. Fambrough told Company A he believed the Board would require a significantly higher price for due diligence access. Centerview reinforced this message with Company A. That evening the Board met, confirmed that Company A needed to move significantly, and determined not to contact additional parties based on the assessment that it was unlikely additional parties would engage competitively, a leak could be disruptive, and the Novo process was advanced.

On November 8, Company A revised its verbal indication to $33.00 per share. Dr. Fambrough again communicated the Board's position. The Board, after reviewing the updated indication, concluded Company A had not moved sufficiently and directed Centerview to convey that $33.00 was still insufficient and that Company A should submit an improved offer promptly if it wished to pursue a transaction. Davis Polk sent Skadden an updated Merger Agreement draft.

During November 10-11, Novo and Dicerna management and Centerview held in-person meetings at Dicerna's Lexington offices. On November 12, following discussion of material open issues, Skadden sent Davis Polk an updated Merger Agreement and Disclosure Schedules, and the Board met to review progress. The Board authorized Dr. Fambrough to seek a price above $38.00. From November 12 through November 17, Skadden and Davis Polk negotiated the termination fee, Novo's antitrust commitment level, and equity award treatment.

On November 13, Jørgensen called Dr. Fambrough to discuss progress. Jørgensen confirmed Novo's due diligence was nearly complete. Dr. Fambrough raised the importance of full equity award acceleration, which the Board had instructed him to pursue. He also reminded Jørgensen of his earlier indication that Novo could find value above $38.00, and requested Novo increase its offer to $40.00. Jørgensen said Novo would respond on November 15.

On November 16, Jørgensen called Dr. Fambrough and verbally indicated Novo would increase to $38.25 per share but not beyond, and would agree to Dicerna's proposed equity treatment. Davis Polk sent Skadden an updated draft reflecting $38.25. The Board met that evening; Centerview confirmed no further communication from Company A since November 8. The Board approved pursuing the transaction at $38.25. The Board confirmed no conflicts of interest from Centerview or SVB Leerink and approved engagement letters with both, which were executed the same day.

On November 17, Skadden and Davis Polk finalized the Merger Agreement. The Board held two teleconference meetings. In the first, Skadden reviewed the final terms and fiduciary duties; Centerview and SVB Leerink reviewed their financial analyses. In the second, Centerview and SVB Leerink each rendered oral fairness opinions (subsequently confirmed in writing) that $38.25 was fair from a financial point of view. The Board unanimously approved the Merger Agreement, the Offer, and the Merger, and resolved to recommend tender. The Merger Agreement was executed before opening of trading on November 18.

The Dicerna Board consisted of the following directors at the time of approval: Douglas M. Fambrough III, Ph.D. (President and CEO; also a director); J. Kevin Buchi; Stephen Doberstein, Ph.D.; Martin Freed, M.D.; Patrick Gray; Stephen J. Hoffman, M.D., Ph.D.; Adam M. Koppel, M.D., Ph.D.; Marc Kozin; and Cynthia Smith. All resigned at the Effective Time on December 28, 2021.

No board committee (e.g., special committee) was separately disclosed in the SC 14D9 as conducting the negotiations; the full Dicerna Board was described as the decision-making body throughout.

## Rationale

The SC 14D9 states the Dicerna Board considered the following reasons in unanimously approving the Merger Agreement:

The Board viewed the $38.25 per share consideration as representing attractive value, noting it represented approximately an 80% premium to the closing price on November 17, 2021 and approximately a 78% premium to the 30-day VWAP. The Board considered Dicerna's current and historical financial condition, competitive position, business prospects, and the execution risks and uncertainties associated with development, regulatory approval, and commercialization of its product portfolio, including risks of delays in planned marketing applications for nedosiran. The Board stated that the Offer Price in its judgment represented the best value reasonably available to holders of Shares.

The Board considered the process: the Offer was the result of arm's-length negotiations, and Dicerna's advisors secured increases in Novo's proposed acquisition price from $32.50 to $38.25 (and improvements to Merger Agreement terms) through negotiations. Two additional counterparties were contacted; one passed without making an offer, and Company A's highest indicated interest was $33.00 per share, which the Board viewed as insufficient.

The Board considered the all-cash structure, which it viewed as providing immediate liquidity and certainty of value relative to the risks of Dicerna's standalone strategy. It also considered the speed and likelihood of consummation, noting the absence of a financing condition, the limited antitrust profile (only HSR review required), and Novo's stated commitment to close before year-end. The Board noted Novo's business reputation, capabilities, and financial condition. The Merger Agreement's provision for full acceleration of all employee equity awards was viewed positively by the Board in terms of employee retention during the pending period and post-close.

The Board also considered potentially negative factors: the preclusion of stockholder participation in Dicerna's future value creation; restrictions on soliciting alternative proposals; the $100 million termination fee; interim operating covenants; risks that the Minimum Condition or other conditions might not be satisfied; potential employee attrition; the potential tax treatment of transaction proceeds; and potential conflicts created by executive officers' and directors' financial interests in the transaction.

The Board received fairness opinions from both Centerview (Annex I to the SC 14D9) and SVB Leerink (Annex II), each confirming that $38.25 per share was fair from a financial point of view.

The SC 14D9 states that Novo's pre-existing relationship with Dicerna through the 2019 Collaboration Agreement and equity stake provided a foundation for Novo's strategic interest and due diligence. The filing describes Novo's initial approach as arising from a desire to expand the collaboration around GalXC Plus; Dicerna's rejection of that expansion led Novo to propose an acquisition. Management and Board deliberations addressed the Company's standalone prospects and risk profile as context for evaluating Novo's proposals.

## Buyout Rumors

Source: OpenRouter web search (Gemini 3 Flash Preview). Pre-deal rumors only.

The acquisition of Dicerna Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (DRNA) by Novo Nordisk was announced on **November 18, 2021**. Prior to this definitive agreement, there were no widely reported specific rumors of an imminent buyout or a specific price per share from a named acquirer. 

Instead, speculation regarding Dicerna as a takeover target was primarily driven by its extensive list of high-profile partnerships and the broader "renaissance" of the RNA interference (RNAi) sector.

The following is a chronological list of the speculation and partnership-driven rumors that appeared before the deal:

*   **October 24, 2018**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *Fierce Biotech* / *BioPharma Dive*
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Alexion Pharmaceuticals
    *   **Rumored Price:** Not reported (Partnership terms: $22M upfront, $15M equity)
    *   **Details:** Following a collaboration deal for two RNAi molecules, analysts began highlighting Dicerna as a key player in the "pipeline rebuild" for rare disease companies, leading to general speculation that its platform (GalXC) made it an attractive long-term target for its partners.

*   **October 29, 2018**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *BioSpace* / *BioPharma Dive*
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Eli Lilly
    *   **Rumored Price:** Not reported (Partnership terms: $100M upfront, $100M equity)
    *   **Details:** After Eli Lilly signed a massive $3.7 billion potential value collaboration and took a significant equity stake, market speculation increased that Lilly was "sealing the deal" for future control of the technology. Analysts noted that the equity investment often serves as a precursor to a full acquisition in the biotech sector.

*   **October 31, 2019**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *Roche Press Release* / *Market Speculation*
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Roche
    *   **Rumored Price:** Not reported (Partnership terms: $200M upfront)
    *   **Details:** Dicerna entered a $1.47 billion collaboration with Roche to develop treatments for chronic hepatitis B. This third major partnership with a "Big Pharma" player led to recurring mentions of Dicerna in "Top Takeover Targets" lists by biotech analysts, though no specific bid was rumored.

*   **November 18, 2019**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *Various Analyst Notes (e.g., Cowen, SVB Leerink)*
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Novo Nordisk
    *   **Rumored Price:** Not reported (Partnership terms: $175M upfront)
    *   **Details:** Upon the announcement of a research collaboration with Novo Nordisk to explore 30+ liver targets, analysts noted that Novo Nordisk was "late to the RNAi game" and might eventually need to own the platform to compete with Alnylam and Arrowhead.

*   **August 16, 2021**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *Seeking Alpha* / *Twitter (Biotech Traders)*
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** General Speculation (Novo Nordisk or Eli Lilly)
    *   **Rumored Price:** Not reported
    *   **Details:** Following a significant drop in share price due to mixed clinical data for its drug *nedosiran*, speculation intensified that Dicerna had become a "bargain" for its existing partners. Traders and analysts suggested the company was now "vulnerable" to a low-premium takeover by a partner already familiar with its data.

**Note:** On November 18, 2021, the confirmed deal was announced at **$38.25 per share** in cash, representing an 80% premium over the previous day's closing price. No specific leak of this price or the timing of the Novo Nordisk bid appeared in major financial press immediately prior to the official press release.
