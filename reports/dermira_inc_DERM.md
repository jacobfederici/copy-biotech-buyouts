## Summary

Dermira, Inc. was acquired by Eli Lilly and Company for $18.75 per share in an all-cash tender offer valued at approximately $1.1 billion in aggregate. The transaction focused on Dermira's late-stage biologic lebrikizumab for atopic dermatitis and its commercial product QBREXZA for hyperhidrosis. The deal was completed via a Section 251(h) merger following the successful tender of approximately 74.8% of Dermira's outstanding shares.

## Timeline

- 2019-08-29: Dermira contacts Lilly regarding potential lebrikizumab sublicense.
- 2019-09-10: Dermira and Lilly enter into a Mutual Non-Disclosure Agreement.
- 2019-11-25: Lilly submits a non-binding proposal to acquire Dermira for $13.00 per share in cash.
- 2019-12-02: Dermira Board meets to discuss Lilly proposal and authorizes outreach to 10 other potential buyers.
- 2019-12-16: Dermira informs Lilly the $13.00 price is unacceptable but allows further due diligence.
- 2019-12-27: Lilly increases its offer to $16.75 per share in cash.
- 2019-12-30: Dermira counters at $18.75 per share.
- 2019-12-31: Lilly agrees to the $18.75 per share price.
- 2020-01-10: Merger Agreement is executed and publicly announced.
- 2020-01-22: Lilly commences the tender offer.
- 2020-02-19: Tender offer expires with 74.8% of shares tendered.
- 2020-02-20: Merger completed; Dermira becomes a wholly owned subsidiary of Lilly.

## Interaction History

Negotiations began following Dermira's outreach for a lebrikizumab partnership. On November 25, 2019, Lilly's SVP Patrik Jonsson contacted Dermira CEO Thomas Wiggans with an unsolicited $13.00/share bid. Dermira's board, advised by Citi and SVB Leerink, conducted a broad market check, contacting 12 pharmaceutical companies (Companies A-L). While Company A proposed a licensing deal, no other party submitted an acquisition bid. On December 27, Lilly raised its bid to $16.75. Dermira countered at $18.75 on December 30, citing recent stock price appreciation and the value of the pipeline. Lilly accepted the $18.75 price on December 31, 2019. Final merger terms, including a $40 million termination fee and 'tail' insurance, were negotiated through early January 2020.

## Rationale

The Dermira Board determined the deal was superior to remaining independent due to the substantial capital required ($1.125 billion estimated) to fund lebrikizumab's Phase 3 trials and commercial launch. Lilly sought to bolster its immunology pipeline with a late-stage asset (lebrikizumab) that had demonstrated positive Phase 2b results.

## Buyout Rumors

Source: OpenRouter web search (Gemini 3 Flash Preview). Pre-deal rumors only.

The following is a chronological timeline of acquisition rumors and speculation regarding Dermira, Inc. (DERM) prior to the official announcement of its acquisition by Eli Lilly and Company on January 10, 2020.

*   **December 2019**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** SVB Leerink (Analyst Note)
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Eli Lilly and Company
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Not specified
    *   **Details:** Analyst Pasha Sarraf identified Eli Lilly as a "top candidate" to acquire Dermira. The speculation was based on Lilly’s need to bolster its dermatology pipeline, specifically to compete with Sanofi and Regeneron’s Dupixent, following safety concerns and challenges surrounding Lilly’s own JAK inhibitor, baricitinib.

*   **Late December 2019 – Early January 2020**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** Various Market Commentators / Stock Market Activity
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Unspecified (General Takeover Speculation)
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Not specified; however, the stock price rose from approximately $8 to $18 in the weeks leading up to the deal.
    *   **Details:** Significant upward movement in Dermira’s share price and increased trading volume led to widespread market speculation that the company was a "takeout target." Analysts noted that the timing—just before the annual J.P. Morgan Healthcare Conference—was a typical window for such acquisitions to be announced.

*   **January 9, 2020**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** Market Rumors / Options Activity
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Eli Lilly and Company
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Not specified
    *   **Details:** On the day immediately preceding the announcement, speculation intensified as Dermira's stock closed at $18.34, nearly reaching the eventual acquisition price of $18.75. Market observers pointed to the company's Phase 3 asset, lebrikizumab, as the primary driver for a near-term acquisition.
