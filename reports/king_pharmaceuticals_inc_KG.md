## Summary

Pfizer Inc. acquired King Pharmaceuticals, Inc. in an all-cash transaction for $14.25 per share, valuing the deal at approximately $3.6 billion. The acquisition was structured as a tender offer followed by a short-form merger. The King Board of Directors unanimously recommended that shareholders tender their shares, citing the significant premium and certainty of value compared to the risks of remaining a standalone company. The tender offer was successfully completed on January 31, 2011, with Pfizer acquiring approximately 92.5% of King's outstanding shares, leading to a change in control and the subsequent completion of the merger.

## Interaction History

On June 3, 2010, Pfizer's CEO, Jeffrey B. Kindler, initiated contact with King's CEO, Brian A. Markison, to request a meeting. They met on June 9, 2010, to discuss a potential transaction, with Mr. Kindler suggesting a confidentiality agreement and due diligence would be necessary to discuss price. On June 11, Mr. Markison informed Mr. Kindler that King's Board was not interested. Despite this, Mr. Kindler pursued the matter in further calls on June 18 and June 24. During this time, Pfizer's financial advisor, J.P. Morgan, suggested a price of approximately $11 per share to King's advisor, Credit Suisse, which was deemed too low. On June 25, J.P. Morgan orally proposed a non-binding range of $12 to $14 per share, which Mr. Markison indicated the Board would likely reject. On June 28, J.P. Morgan increased the low end of the range to $13, proposing $13 to $14 per share. Following this, on June 29, King's Board authorized management to enter a confidentiality agreement and allow a five-day period of high-level diligence. On July 9, 2010, Pfizer's CFO, Frank D’Amelio, formally communicated the $13 to $14 per share non-binding offer, and the companies executed a confidentiality agreement. Pfizer conducted preliminary due diligence from August 2 to August 6, with a follow-up meeting on August 12. On August 16, Mr. D’Amelio made a non-binding offer of $13.25 per share, which Mr. Markison stated he believed the Board would not support. This offer was confirmed in writing on August 31. On September 9, Mr. Markison formally communicated the Board's rejection of the $13.25 offer. Between September 9 and September 16, King contacted four other parties to gauge interest, but no proposals were received. On September 20, Mr. D’Amelio indicated Pfizer might raise its offer to $13.75, which Mr. Markison again said would likely be unacceptable. On September 21, Mr. D’Amelio made a 'best and final' non-binding offer of $14.25 per share. On September 24, King's Board authorized full due diligence and the negotiation of a definitive agreement based on this offer. A draft merger agreement was delivered by Pfizer's counsel on September 29, with King's counsel providing comments on October 1. The parties negotiated the final terms between October 1 and October 11. On October 11, 2010, King's Board received a fairness opinion from Credit Suisse and unanimously approved the Merger Agreement, which was executed the same day. The tender offer commenced on October 22, 2010, and was successfully completed on January 31, 2011, with Pfizer acquiring approximately 92.5% of King's shares, resulting in a change of control. The transaction was set to be finalized via a short-form merger on or about February 28, 2011.

## Rationale

In recommending the transaction, King's Board of Directors considered several factors. The Board believed the offer provided a certain and compelling cash value for shareholders, which was more favorable than the potential value from other strategic alternatives, including remaining an independent company. This certainty was contrasted with the risks and uncertainties associated with King's standalone business, including the completion of clinical trials, obtaining regulatory approvals, commercializing key product candidates, protecting intellectual property, and facing competition from generic products. The Board noted the significant premium the $14.25 per share offer represented over recent trading prices (40.4% over the last trading day). The course of negotiations led the Board to believe this was the highest price Pfizer was willing to pay. The Board also considered the high likelihood of completion, given the absence of a financing condition and Pfizer's experience with acquisitions, and the favorable fairness opinion from its financial advisor, Credit Suisse. The tender offer structure was seen as a way to deliver cash to shareholders promptly.

(8-K, 0000950123-11-009303)

## Decision Notes

The transaction was a cash tender offer followed by a short-form merger, as the tender offer resulted in Pfizer owning over 90% of King's shares. The final price of $14.25 per share was the culmination of a negotiation process that started with informal discussions around $11 per share and included a formal offer of $13.25 per share, which was rejected. The shares outstanding figure of 249,958,811 is as of October 19, 2010, per the SC 14D9 filing. The deal value is calculated based on this share count and the per-share price. No CVRs were involved.

(Note: This deal was later terminated in early 2005 following opposition from activist investor Carl Icahn).

    *   **Reporting Outlet:** The Deal / New York Post
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Novartis, GlaxoSmithKline, or Teva Pharmaceutical Industries.
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Not specified.
    *   **Details:** Following the collapse of the Mylan merger, reports circulated that King was "back on the block." Speculation suggested that European pharmaceutical giants or generic manufacturers were eyeing King’s primary care portfolio and its cardiovascular drug, Altace.

*   **August 2006**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** Fly on the Wall / Briefing.com
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Unnamed "Large Pharma" suitor.
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** $20.00–$22.00 per share.
    *   **Details:** Market chatter increased regarding a potential takeover bid as King’s stock price showed unusual volume. Analysts speculated that a larger company would want King’s specialty sales force.

*   **May 2007**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** Daily Mail (UK) / ThisIsMoney.co.uk
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** AstraZeneca or Wyeth.
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Speculated at a "significant premium" to the then-trading price of ~$18.00.
    *   **Details:** Rumors surfaced in the UK press that AstraZeneca was considering a bid for King Pharmaceuticals to bolster its pain management and neuroscience pipeline.

*   **September 2008**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** CNBC / Bloomberg
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Pfizer or Johnson & Johnson.
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Not specified.
    *   **Details:** During King’s own hostile pursuit of Alpharma Inc., market speculators suggested that King itself had become a "bite-sized" target for Pfizer, which was looking to diversify its revenue ahead of the Lipitor patent expiration.

*   **June 2010**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** Wall Street Journal (Heard on the Street) / Barron's
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Pfizer.
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** $12.00–$15.00 per share.
    *   **Details:** Reports highlighted King as a prime acquisition target due to its leadership in "abuse-resistant" opioid technology (Embeda). Analysts noted that Pfizer’s existing partnership with King on the drug Remoxy made a full acquisition a logical strategic move.

*   **October 11, 2010 (The day before the announcement)**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** Bloomberg / Reuters (citing "people familiar with the matter")
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Pfizer.
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Approximately $3.6 billion ($14.25 per share).
    *   **Details:** Late-stage reports emerged that Pfizer was in advanced talks to acquire King. The reports correctly identified the cash nature of the deal and the approximate valuation just hours before the official press release on the morning of October 12.
