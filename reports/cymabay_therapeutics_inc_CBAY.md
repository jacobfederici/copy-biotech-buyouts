## Summary

CymaBay Therapeutics was acquired by Gilead Sciences for $32.50 per share in an all-cash tender offer valued at approximately $4.3 billion. The transaction focused on CymaBay's lead asset, seladelpar, a Phase 3 investigational treatment for primary biliary cholangitis (PBC). The deal was completed via a Section 251(h) merger following the successful tender of approximately 77.3% of outstanding shares.

## Interaction History

Negotiations began in earnest following CymaBay's positive Phase 3 data in September 2023. Gilead's initial November 2023 offer of $23.00 was rejected by the board. CymaBay's management, led by CEO Sujal Shah and advised by Centerview and Lazard, engaged with 22 potential partners, including 'Company A', 'Company B', 'Company C', and 'Company D'. Company C withdrew in January 2024, and others cited valuation concerns. Gilead increased its bid to $31.00 plus a $2.50 CVR on January 25, 2024. The CymaBay board pushed for more upfront cash and a higher price. On January 30, Gilead moved to $32.50 all-cash, stating it was their 'best and final' offer. CymaBay attempted to negotiate for $33.00 on January 31, but Gilead refused. Board member Janet Dorling recused herself from all deliberations due to her relationship with Gilead. Regulatory clearance was obtained under the HSR Act, and the deal closed on March 22, 2024, after 77.3% of shares were tendered.

## Rationale

The CymaBay board determined the deal was superior to standalone operation, noting the risks of building an independent commercial infrastructure and the uncertainty of market acceptance for seladelpar. Gilead sought to strengthen its liver disease portfolio, leveraging its existing commercial scale to accelerate the global launch of seladelpar.

The deal included a $151.6 million termination fee.
