## Summary

Alkaloida Chemical Company Exclusive Group Ltd., a subsidiary of Sun Pharmaceutical Industries Ltd., launched a hostile tender offer to acquire all outstanding ordinary shares of Taro Pharmaceutical Industries Ltd. for $7.75 per share in cash. This offer followed Taro's termination of a May 2007 merger agreement with Sun. The Taro Board of Directors unanimously recommended that shareholders reject the offer, characterizing it as a 'sham' and 'low-ball' proposal designed to force the exercise of options held by the controlling Levitt and Moros families at an undervalued price following a significant operational turnaround at the company.

## Interaction History

Negotiations began in 2006 via The Blackstone Group due to Taro's liquidity crisis, leading to a May 2007 merger agreement at $7.75/share. Shareholder opposition, led by Templeton, prevented a vote. In early 2008, Sun acquired a block of shares from Brandes at $10.25/share and proposed amending the merger to that price, conditioned on eliminating Israeli 'disinterested majority' vote protections. Taro's board, advised by Merrill Lynch, found $10.25 inadequate given a 'dramatic financial turnaround' in 2007. Sun Chairman Dilip Shanghvi met with the Taro board on May 14, 2008, issuing an ultimatum. Taro terminated the merger on May 28, 2008. Sun contested the termination in New York court and launched a $7.75 tender offer on June 30, 2008, which Taro's board labeled a 'sham' intended to trigger 2007 option agreements with the controlling families (Drs. Barrie Levitt and Daniel Moros) at an outdated valuation.

## Rationale

The Taro Board recommended rejection because the $7.75 price was significantly below the current market price and the $10.25 price Sun previously offered. The Board argued the offer failed to reflect Taro's 2007 financial turnaround (reporting $21.1 million in net income) and was 'coercive' and 'illegal' under Israeli 'special tender offer' rules. Sun's motive was characterized as an attempt to seize control by forcing the controlling families to honor an option agreement at an obsolete price.
