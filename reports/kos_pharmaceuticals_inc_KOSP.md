## Summary

Abbott Laboratories acquired Kos Pharmaceuticals for $78.00 per share in cash, representing a total transaction value of approximately $3.7 billion net of cash. The acquisition was structured as a tender offer followed by a second-step merger, aimed at expanding Abbott's presence in the lipid management market and strengthening its late-stage pipeline in cardiovascular and respiratory diseases.

## Interaction History

Negotiations began in April 2006 following an unsolicited bid from an unnamed company at $65/share, which was rejected. In September, Greenhill contacted nine potential acquirors. Three parties conducted due diligence. In mid-October, Abbott and 'Party B' submitted initial ranges. On November 1, Abbott bid $72.00 (merger) and Party B bid $71.00 (tender offer). On November 3, both bidders increased to $74.00. The Kos Board initially preferred Party B due to better contract terms and speed. However, on November 5, Abbott met with Kos advisors in New York and increased its bid to $78.00 per share while accepting Kos's preferred contract forms. Party B declined to match the $78.00 offer. The Kos Board, advised by Greenhill and legal counsel Cravath, Swaine & Moore and Holland & Knight, unanimously approved the Abbott transaction on November 5, 2006.

## Rationale

The acquisition is intended to expand Abbott's presence in the $20 billion lipid management market, specifically adding HDL-raising therapies (Niaspan) to Abbott's existing TriCor franchise. It also provides Abbott with late-stage pipeline products in respiratory (Flutiform) and metabolic diseases, while increasing R&D spending capacity.

## Evidence

- On December 12, 2006, Abbott and Parthenon successfully completed a tender offer... representing approximately 91.4% of the outstanding shares (8-K, 2006-12-13).

No pre-deal rumors exist for the acquisition of Kos Pharmaceuticals Inc. (KOSP).

The company was acquired by Abbott Laboratories in a deal announced on **November 6, 2006**, for $78 per share in cash (approximately $3.7 billion). Financial news reports at the time of the announcement, including those from *Bloomberg* and *The New York Times*, noted that the deal came as a surprise to the market. While Kos Pharmaceuticals had been a subject of general sector speculation due to its strong position in the lipid-management (cholesterol) market, there were no specific, documented reports of an impending buyout or named suitors in the days or weeks immediately preceding the definitive agreement.
