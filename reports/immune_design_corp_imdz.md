## Summary

Immune Design Corp. (Nasdaq: IMDZ), a Seattle-based clinical-stage cancer immunotherapy company, entered into a merger agreement with Merck Sharp & Dohme Corp. on February 20, 2019, under which Merck's wholly owned subsidiary Cascade Merger Sub Inc. commenced a cash tender offer on March 5, 2019 to acquire all outstanding shares of Immune Design at $5.85 per share. The offer represented a 312% premium to the February 20, 2019 closing price of $1.42 per share and a 294% premium to the prior 20-trading day VWAP of $1.49, reflecting the severe depression of IMDZ's stock following the October 2018 discontinuation of its lead program CMB305 in synovial sarcoma. Merck had previously been in discussions with Immune Design regarding a license to Immune Design's GLAAS platform when discussions escalated to an acquisition proposal in October 2018. Following outreach to four strategic counterparties, all of which declined, and arm's-length negotiation on price that moved the offer from an initial structure of $200M plus CVRs to a firm $5.85 per share all-cash offer, the board unanimously approved the transaction. As of the April 2, 2019 expiration, 41,970,607 shares (approximately 86.75% of outstanding shares) were validly tendered. Merck completed the merger on April 2, 2019 pursuant to Section 251(h) of the DGCL without a stockholder vote, with Immune Design surviving as a wholly owned subsidiary of Merck.

## Interaction History

**Pre-acquisition licensing discussions (early September–October 2018).** For several months prior to October 2018, Business Development representatives of both Merck and Immune Design had been in discussions about a potential license from Immune Design to Merck with respect to the GLAAS platform. In early September 2018, Merck proposed license terms; Immune Design provided a counter-proposal. The parties continued to correspond through the middle of October 2018. The SC 14D9 (accession 0001193125-19-063377) does not identify by name the Merck or Immune Design personnel involved in the licensing discussions.

**First acquisition approach (October 2018).** On October 18, 2018, following Immune Design's October 11 announcement of the CMB305 discontinuation and workforce reduction, Dr. Carlos Paya (President and CEO) and a senior executive of Merck exchanged emails to arrange a meeting at the European Society for Medical Oncology (ESMO) conference in Munich. At a business dinner on October 21, 2018, the Merck senior executive indicated to Dr. Paya that Merck had an interest in acquiring Immune Design. Dr. Paya responded that Immune Design was not for sale, especially at its current trading price. The Merck representative did not provide a specific price. The identity of the senior executive is not disclosed in the SC 14D9.

**Board forms Transaction Committee (October 26, 2018).** The board met telephonically with management and Cooley LLP. Noting the acquisition interest expressed by Merck and the need to evaluate strategic alternatives, the board authorized a Transaction Committee consisting of: Dr. Carlos Paya (President and CEO, the only non-independent member), Franklin Berger (board member), William Ringo (board member), and Lewis Coleman (board member), each selected based on their independence (other than Dr. Paya), experience with strategic transactions, existing board roles, and willingness to serve. The SC 14D9 notes the committee's formation was for convenience, not to address conflicts of interest. The Transaction Committee was delegated authority to evaluate and make recommendations on indications of interest, engage financial advisors, and advise management on strategic alternatives negotiations.

**First formal Merck acquisition proposal (November 10–12, 2018).** On November 10, 2018, a senior member of Merck's Business Development team called Dr. Paya and walked him through a non-binding verbal acquisition offer. On November 12, 2018, Merck submitted the November 12 Offer: acquire Immune Design for $200.0 million in cash plus two CVRs of $85 million each, contingent on achieving regulatory approvals for products containing GLA or the ZVex vector. The offer was based on Merck's perceived value of Immune Design's research programs. Merck also requested preliminary due diligence access. The Transaction Committee met that same day with management and Cooley, discussed the offer, and decided to postpone further review until a financial advisor was formally engaged.

**Financial advisor selection (November 15, 2018).** The Transaction Committee reviewed qualifications, expertise, fee proposals, and conflicts disclosures from Lazard Frères & Co. LLC ("Lazard"), Financial Advisor 1, and Financial Advisor 2 (the names of the two other advisors are not disclosed in the SC 14D9). The Transaction Committee authorized engagement of Lazard based on its experience and industry sector expertise.

**Transaction Committee reviews November 12 Offer and initiates market check (November 18, 2018).** Lazard discussed factors including timing, certainty, and potential alternative bidders based on Immune Design's existing collaboration relationships and potential market interest in its key assets. The Transaction Committee directed management to push for more upfront consideration and directed Lazard to contact four strategic counterparties. The four were selected based on potential strategic fit, financial resources, and ability to timely and successfully complete an acquisition. Their identities are not disclosed in the SC 14D9.

**Response to November 12 Offer — push for upfront consideration (November 19, 2018).** Dr. Paya communicated to a senior Merck representative that, as a precondition to beginning due diligence, Merck must provide an offer on a per-share basis with the CVR value moved into upfront cash. Later that day, Merck submitted the November 19 Offer: acquire Immune Design for $225.0 million in cash at closing with no CVRs, again based on Merck's perceived value of Immune Design's research programs. On the same day, Lazard contacted all four strategic parties. On November 20, Lazard contacted Strategic Party A, Strategic Party B, and Strategic Party C; provided Strategic Party C with a non-confidential process overview and a draft confidentiality agreement. On November 28, Strategic Party A declined. On November 29, Strategic Party B declined. Strategic Party C received follow-up contacts on November 20, November 28, and December 7, 2018; in each instance, Strategic Party C indicated it was not interested. Strategic Party D was contacted on November 26 and December 7, 2018; in each instance, Strategic Party D did not indicate interest in a strategic transaction.

**November 26 Offer — estimated $5.85 per share, subject to assumptions (November 26, 2018).** Merck submitted a non-binding letter of intent to acquire Immune Design at an estimated price-per-share of $5.85 based on the enterprise value of Immune Design and certain financial and other operating assumptions, subject to completion of confirmatory diligence. The Transaction Committee met on November 27 and directed Lazard to continue to negotiate with Merck and indicate that due diligence materials would be made available, while not agreeing to the price.

**Confidentiality agreement and data room access (December 4, 2018).** Merck executed a new confidentiality agreement containing an employee non-solicitation restriction and a standstill provision with a fall-away clause (standstill becomes inoperative if the Company enters into a definitive agreement for a sale). Immune Design provided Merck access to an electronic data room for preliminary due diligence. Merck conducted due diligence calls with management on December 11 and 12.

**Board reviews financial projections (December 12, 2018).** The full board met with management, Lazard, and Cooley. Management presented long-range financial projections, including development timelines, probability of success, revenue forecasts, and potential partnership/licensing arrangements. Lazard noted none of the four counterparties contacted had shown interest in engaging further. The board, after reviewing new competitive landscape data from the 2018 American Society of Hematology Meeting, directed management to revise projections to reflect a lower probability of success for G100. On December 20, the board reviewed the revised projections and authorized Lazard to use them in future fairness analyses.

**Merck's final-offer ultimatum (January 8–9, 2019).** On January 8, 2019, both a senior Merck executive via Lazard and a separate senior Merck executive via Dr. Paya communicated the same message: Merck was not willing to entertain any increase in price from the November 26 Offer and would terminate discussions if the Company tried to seek a higher price. On January 9, another senior Merck executive met with Dr. Paya and reiterated this position. The identity of these Merck executives is not disclosed in the SC 14D9.

**Board solicits a firm per-share price (January 11, 2019).** At a full board meeting, Dr. Paya reviewed the interactions with Merck on January 8 and 9. Lazard discussed the strategic process, the lack of engagement from other parties, and the need to get a definitive per-share number (because the November 26 Offer's estimated price was subject to assumptions that made it difficult for the board to assess). The board directed Lazard to solicit a firm per-share price from Merck not subject to outstanding assumptions. Lazard subsequently contacted Merck, which requested specific capitalization and cash information. On January 12, Lazard provided Merck and Credit Suisse (Merck's financial advisor) a summary of Immune Design's current capitalization, expected cash balance, and transaction-related expense assumptions.

**Negotiation from $5.80 to $5.85 per share (January 15–17, 2019).** On January 15, Credit Suisse communicated to Lazard an offer of $5.80 per share. Following discussion, and at the Company's direction, Lazard communicated to Merck on January 16 that $5.85 per share — consistent with the November 26 Offer estimate — would likely be viewed much more favorably by the board. On January 17, Merck provided the January 17 Offer: $5.85 per share, subject only to completion of confirmatory diligence.

**Transaction Committee recommends to proceed; board approves confirmatory diligence (January 17–18, 2019).** The Transaction Committee met on January 17. Lazard provided an overview of the valuation discussion sequence. Cooley reviewed fiduciary duties. The Transaction Committee — noting the lack of viable third-party alternatives given that none of the four counterparties had engaged — recommended to the board to proceed with confirmatory diligence and negotiate definitive documents with Merck. On January 18, the board approved this course of action.

**Confirmatory diligence and document negotiation (January 19 – February 20, 2019).** Management conducted due diligence calls and responded to written diligence requests from Merck over a roughly four-week period. On January 29 and February 1, Merck conducted site visits to two of Immune Design's vendors related to GLA manufacturing. On January 23, Cooley sent a draft merger agreement to Gibson, Dunn & Crutcher LLP (Gibson), Merck's outside legal counsel, which included a proposed termination fee equal to 2.5% of equity value. On January 29, Cooley sent draft disclosure schedules to Gibson. On February 4, Gibson returned a revised merger agreement with a termination fee of 3.75% of equity value, plus comments to disclosure schedules. From February 5 to February 20, the parties negotiated and finalized the open issues, settling on a termination fee of 3.5% of equity value ($10.5 million).

**Final board approval and execution (February 20, 2019).** The board met telephonically with management, Lazard, and Cooley. Lazard reviewed its financial analyses. Members asked questions. Lazard orally rendered its fairness opinion that $5.85 per share was fair from a financial point of view to the holders of Shares (other than Excluded Holders). The written opinion was subsequently confirmed in writing as Annex I to the SC 14D9. Cooley then reviewed the board's fiduciary duties and the principal terms of the merger agreement, including: (a) the fiduciary duty exception permitting negotiation of an unsolicited superior proposal, (b) the 3.5% termination fee ($10.5 million) payable if the Company terminated to accept an alternative superior proposal, (c) the conditions to consummation of the Offer and Merger, and (d) employee compensation matters. The board unanimously approved and executed the Merger Agreement that afternoon. The joint press release was issued on the morning of February 21, 2019.

**Tender offer, tender outcome, and merger completion (March 5 – April 2, 2019).** Cascade Merger Sub commenced the tender offer on March 5, 2019. The Company filed the SC 14D9 recommending acceptance on March 5, 2019. The offer expired at 12:00 midnight Eastern Time on April 2, 2019. As reported in the April 2, 2019 8-K (accession 0001193125-19-095238), 41,970,607 Shares (approximately 86.75%) were validly tendered and 379,162 additional Shares were tendered by notice of guaranteed delivery. Cascade Merger Sub accepted all validly tendered Shares. Immediately following acceptance, the Merger was completed pursuant to Section 251(h) of the DGCL, with Immune Design surviving as a wholly owned subsidiary of Merck. All remaining Shares (other than appraisal shares and shares held by Merck/Cascade) were converted into the right to receive $5.85 per share.

## Rationale

The SC 14D9 (accession 0001193125-19-063377) sets out the following factors that the board considered in recommending acceptance of the Offer. The board assessed Immune Design's prospects for substantially increasing stockholder value as a standalone company, noting risks including enrollment, completion, and technical success risks for G100 clinical trials; risks related to the launch and manufacturing of G100; competition in follicular lymphoma from existing drugs and drugs in development; risks relating to reimbursement and product pricing; the belief that Immune Design would require additional capital to execute its strategy before achieving profitability; and execution risks associated with transforming a clinical-stage cancer immunotherapy company into a profitable commercialized entity. The board also assessed the need to raise additional equity financing and the difficulty of the financing environment given the depressed stock price and limited near-term catalysts.

The board considered the results of the strategic process, including the outreach to four strategic counterparties, none of which engaged regarding a possible transaction. The board stated its belief that the Offer Price of $5.85 per share was more certain and favorable to stockholders than the potential value from continuing as a standalone public company or a sale of certain assets. The board cited the 312% premium to the February 20, 2019 closing price of $1.42 and the 294% premium to the prior 20-trading-day VWAP of $1.49. The board noted that the stock had traded below $2.10 since October 2018 and had not traded above $5.00 since October 2017. The board also noted that the Merger Agreement contained a fiduciary-out provision permitting the Company to negotiate and accept an unsolicited superior proposal subject to a $10.5 million termination fee, and that the transaction was structured as a cash tender offer without a financing condition, reducing execution risk. The board believed that Merck, as a leading pharmaceutical company with a commitment to research and development, was an appropriate acquirer for Immune Design's pipeline assets, including G100 and the GLAAS platform, which Merck had independently been evaluating for a potential license for several months before the acquisition discussions.

Lazard's written fairness opinion, dated February 20, 2019, was based on a sum-of-the-parts DCF analysis of probability-adjusted projections for G100 in 4L follicular lymphoma, 2L follicular lymphoma, 2L CTCL, 2L marginal zone lymphoma, and HSV/RSV/HPV collaboration revenue, using two financial projection scenarios prepared by management — Case 1 (standalone product run-off) and Case 2 (going concern). The full opinion is included as Annex I to the SC 14D9.
