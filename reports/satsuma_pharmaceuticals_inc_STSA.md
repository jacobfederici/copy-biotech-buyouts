## Summary

Satsuma Pharmaceuticals entered into a definitive merger agreement with its co-founder and major shareholder, Shin Nippon Biomedical Laboratories (SNBL), following disappointing Phase 3 clinical results for its lead migraine candidate, STS101. The transaction was structured as a tender offer for $0.91 per share in cash plus a contingent value right worth up to $5.77 per share based on future proceeds from the STS101 program. The deal closed on June 8, 2023, making Satsuma a wholly-owned subsidiary of SNBL.

## Timeline

- 2022-09-13: Satsuma Board directs management to engage Lazard on an informal basis to evaluate strategic options.
- 2022-11-14: Satsuma announces topline results from SUMMIT Phase 3 trial; STS101 failed to achieve statistical significance on co-primary endpoints.
- 2022-11-14: Company publicly announces it will explore strategic alternatives and minimize cash expenditures.
- 2022-12-07: SNBL representative Ken Takanashi indicates SNBL may be willing to pursue a transaction to prevent the STS101 program from being abandoned.
- 2023-02-16: SNBL submits non-binding proposal to acquire Satsuma for net cash plus $1.66 million and a CVR.
- 2023-02-27: Satsuma Board directs management to counter SNBL's proposal with a fixed price based on net cash plus a $7 million premium.
- 2023-03-08: SNBL submits revised proposal: net cash plus $2 million and a CVR with milestones up to $4.50 per share.
- 2023-03-12: Satsuma counters with net cash plus $2.5 million and a CVR with milestones up to $5.77 per share.
- 2023-03-14: SNBL accepts Satsuma's terms with minor adjustments to CVR threshold calculations.
- 2023-04-16: Satsuma Board approves the Merger Agreement; parties execute the agreement.
- 2023-05-05: Purchaser commences tender offer.
- 2023-06-05: Tender offer expires with ~66% of shares tendered.
- 2023-06-08: Merger completed via Section 251(h) of the DGCL.

## Interaction History

Following the SUMMIT trial failure in November 2022, Lazard contacted 29 potential counterparties. While 11 parties signed NDAs, none submitted actionable proposals except for SNBL and 'Party A'. Party A suggested a stock-for-stock merger at a 110% market cap valuation but refused to include a CVR or fixed cash price. SNBL, already an 8.6% shareholder and the licensor of the delivery technology, initially preferred a third-party buyer but stepped in to preserve the STS101 program. Negotiations between John Kollins (Satsuma CEO) and Ken Takanashi (SNBL EVP and Satsuma Director) focused on the 'premium' over net cash and the CVR structure. The final deal settled on $0.91 per share (representing a $2.5M premium over projected net cash) and a CVR reflecting a tiered sharing of 'Aggregate Cumulative Net Proceeds' from the STS101 program, ranging from $0.15 to $5.77 per share. The Board met multiple times in executive session without Mr. Takanashi to evaluate the fairness of the offer against a potential liquidation, which management estimated would yield only $0.65 to $0.79 per share.

## Rationale

The Satsuma Board determined that the company lacked the capital to independently commercialize STS101 following the SUMMIT trial failure. Management expressed substantial doubt about the company's ability to continue as a going concern. The merger was viewed as superior to a liquidation, which was estimated to return significantly less value to shareholders ($0.65-$0.79) and would involve higher administrative costs and longer timelines. SNBL's motive was to maintain the viability of the STS101 program, which utilizes SNBL's proprietary nasal drug delivery technology.

- On June 8, 2023, following consummation of the Offer, Purchaser merged with and into the Company, with the Company surviving the merger as the Surviving Corporation. (0001193125-23-163082)
- The Company Board... determined that the Merger Agreement and the transactions contemplated thereby, including the Offer and the Merger, are fair to, advisable and in the best interests of the Company and its stockholders. (0001193125-23-136402)

## Decision Notes

The deal value of ~$27.7M cited in the 8-K reflects the actual cash paid for shares not already owned by the buyer group. The CVR milestones are tiered based on 'Aggregate Cumulative Net Proceeds' received by Parent from the sale or license of the STS101 program, with payments starting at $25M in proceeds ($0.15/CVR) and maxing out at $500M in proceeds ($5.77/CVR).
