## Summary

Inspire Pharmaceuticals, Inc. entered into a definitive merger agreement with Merck & Co., Inc. on April 5, 2011, following a competitive bidding process. Under the agreement, Merck's subsidiary, Monarch Transaction Corp., launched a cash tender offer to acquire all outstanding shares of Inspire for $5.00 per share. The transaction followed the disappointing Phase 3 clinical trial results for Inspire's lead candidate, denufosol, which led the board to explore strategic alternatives. The tender offer successfully concluded on May 12, 2011, with approximately 75.4% of shares tendered. Merck subsequently exercised a top-up option to reach the 90% threshold required for a short-form merger, which was completed on May 16, 2011.

## Interaction History

Following the failure of the Denufosol Phase 3 trial in early January 2011, Inspire's board authorized management to explore strategic alternatives. Goldman Sachs contacted multiple potential buyers. Company 4 initially offered $5.00 per share in late January but withdrew in early February. Company 3 emerged as a strong contender, offering a range of $5.00-$5.25, but later lowered its firm offer to $4.40 on March 24. Merck entered the process in late February, initially offering $4.85. Throughout March, Inspire negotiated with both Merck and Company 3. Company 3's proposal was viewed as less certain due to significant financing requirements and a higher minimum tender condition. On April 4, Merck increased its offer to $5.00, while Company 3 increased its offer to $4.90. The Board determined Merck's offer was superior in price, certainty, and timing. The deal was signed on April 5, 2011. Regulatory clearance was obtained following HSR filings on April 12, 2011.

## Rationale

The Board recommended the transaction due to the failure of the Denufosol Phase 3 trial, which significantly impacted the company's independent prospects. The $5.00 cash price provided immediate liquidity and a 25.6% premium over the pre-announcement closing price. The Board also considered the risks of remaining independent, including generic competition for Elestat and the lack of resources to compete in the pharmaceutical business development landscape.

## Buyout Rumors

Source: OpenRouter web search (Gemini 3 Flash Preview). Pre-deal rumors only.

No pre-deal rumors or public takeover speculation for Inspire Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (ISPH) were reported in major financial or pharmaceutical news outlets prior to the official acquisition announcement.

The acquisition of Inspire Pharmaceuticals by Merck & Co. was announced on **April 5, 2011**, at a price of **$5.00 per share** (a 26% premium). While subsequent reporting by outlets such as *Drug Discovery News* (May 4, 2011) noted that Inspire had "entertained bids from two other suitors" before accepting Merck's offer, these competitive bids and the sale process itself were conducted privately and did not leak as rumors to the press before the definitive agreement was signed.

The lack of pre-deal rumors is attributed to the company's specific circumstances in early 2011:
*   **Clinical Failure:** In January 2011, Inspire's lead drug candidate for cystic fibrosis (denufosol) failed a Phase 3 trial, causing the stock to plummet 58% in a single day.
*   **Strategic Shift:** Following this failure, the company immediately announced a restructuring and a shift to focus on its existing ophthalmology portfolio.
*   **Private Process:** The "extensive analysis of various strategic options" mentioned by CEO Adrian Adams upon the deal's announcement was handled internally and with private advisors, successfully avoiding public speculation until the Merck deal was finalized.
