## Summary

Celgene Corporation acquired Receptos, Inc., a San Diego-based biopharmaceutical company focused on the discovery and development of novel drugs targeting the immunology and inflammatory pathways, for $232.00 per share in cash. The Merger Agreement was executed on July 14, 2015, following a process that began in August 2013 with an unsolicited proposal from a third-party pharmaceutical company and included two separate engagement periods with Celgene and a competing offer from a second bidder. The tender offer commenced July 28, 2015, expired August 24, 2015 with approximately 83% of shares tendered, and the merger was consummated on August 27, 2015 under DGCL Section 251(h) without a stockholder vote. Receptos's shares were delisted from the NASDAQ Global Market effective August 28, 2015.

## Interaction History

The transaction emerged from a process that began in August 2013 with an unsolicited approach from Party A, a publicly traded pharmaceutical company. Following receipt of that proposal on August 14, 2013, when the closing price was $17.03, the Company engaged Centerview Partners LLC on August 23, 2013 as financial advisor and Latham & Watkins LLP as legal advisor. At the direction of the Company Board, management and Centerview contacted 11 additional publicly traded pharmaceutical and biotechnology companies, including Celgene, to gauge interest in a strategic transaction. The Company entered into a non-disclosure agreement with Celgene on August 28, 2013.

From August through October 2013, both Party A and Celgene conducted due diligence and held discussions with the Company. Celgene indicated during this period that it would be willing to consider a transaction with a significant contingent component. Party A ultimately withdrew from discussions without specifying a reason in the SC 14D9. On October 7, 2013, Celgene informed the Company it was not going to further pursue a transaction at that time; the closing price that day was $29.97.

Over the following 13 months, the Company's stock advanced sharply as its ozanimod program produced a series of positive clinical data disclosures. On December 5, 2013, the Phase 2 RADIANCE interim analysis was announced, which coincided with a rise in closing price from $22.70 to $27.64. On June 10, 2014, the Phase 2 RADIANCE primary endpoint achievement was announced; the closing price rose from $29.20 to $39.94. On October 28, 2014, the TOUCHSTONE Phase 2 primary endpoint success in ulcerative colitis was announced; the closing price rose from $67.74 to $95.76.

In November 2014, Hasnain was separately contacted by representatives of both Celgene and Party B, each expressing interest in strategic transactions including a potential acquisition. From November 2014 through April 2015, the Company held discussions with and sought proposals from 13 publicly traded pharmaceutical companies. In late November 2014, the Company completed a public offering of 4,140,000 shares at $100.00 per share. On November 10, 2014, the Company Board discussed strategic alternatives and determined to retain strategic flexibility by completing the capital raise rather than pursuing a sale or partnership at that time.

On February 17, 2015, Celgene conveyed an oral proposal at approximately a 65% premium to the Company's then-current trading price, implying approximately $191.12 per share based on the February 13, 2015 closing price of $115.83. On February 25, 2015, Celgene delivered a written, non-binding indication of interest at $193.00 per share in cash, representing approximately 60% premium to the February 24, 2015 closing price of $120.59. At direction of the Board, the Company invited Celgene to further due diligence and delivered an initial draft of the Merger Agreement. On February 26, 2015, Party B delivered a written, non-binding proposal at $175.00 to $185.00 per share. At Board direction, Hasnain informed Party B it would need to significantly increase its offer before receiving confidential information.

On February 27, 2015, the Company Board held a telephonic meeting with management, Centerview, and Latham & Watkins. Centerview reviewed the financial aspects of both proposals and the identities of additional parties contacted. Latham & Watkins reviewed fiduciary duties. The Board concluded both proposals provided a sufficient basis for further negotiation but directed management to seek higher prices from each party. The Company entered into a new engagement letter with Centerview effective February 27, 2015.

On March 2, 2015, Party B revised its proposal to up to $225.00 per share, and the Company invited Party B into due diligence and delivered a draft merger agreement. Latham & Watkins and Proskauer Rose LLP (Celgene's outside counsel) and counsel for Party B exchanged drafts during this period. On March 6, 2015, the parties amended the respective non-disclosure agreements to include customary standstill obligations.

The Company Board held three meetings on March 6, 16, and 23, 2015 to receive updates on discussions and due diligence with both Celgene and Party B. At the March 23 meeting, the Board directed management to request final acquisition proposals from both parties by April 2, 2015. Celgene conducted a face-to-face due diligence session on March 23, 2015.

On March 27, 2015, Party B withdrew its proposal, leaving Celgene as the sole interested acquiror. The closing price that day was $147.43. On April 1, 2015, media reports of a potential sale caused intraday volatility, with the stock reaching $202.96 before closing at $173.44. That same day, Celgene informed the Company it needed more time. Between mid-April and the first week of May, Celgene's representatives had a number of discussions with Hasnain to provide timeline updates.

On April 7, 2015, Party C delivered a term sheet for a worldwide commercialization collaboration for ozanimod, proposing an upfront cash payment, contingent milestone payments, and an equal split of commercialization expenses and profits. On April 16, 2015, Party A delivered a similar term sheet, including a provision under which certain of the Company's strategic rights under the collaboration would terminate if the Company were acquired by a third-party strategic competitor of Party A.

On April 22, 2015, the Company Board reviewed both collaboration term sheets and decided not to pursue either Party A's or Party C's proposals on their proposed terms, concluding they did not appropriately reflect the Company's value, would not match standalone value potential, and would potentially limit strategic flexibility including the potential for a future sale. The Board determined to continue preparing for independent commercialization while remaining open to strategic transaction offers.

On May 1, 2015, Company and Celgene representatives had a call on due diligence matters. At the end of the first week of May, Celgene suspended discussions again. On May 22, 2015, the Company sent letters to Celgene and Party B requesting return or destruction of confidential information.

On June 10, 2015, media outlets again reported that multiple companies were considering bids. The closing price that day rose from an opening of $160.09 to close at $180.52.

On June 18, 2015, Party B representatives met with Hasnain and expressed renewed interest but indicated their likely maximum was approximately $200.00 per share plus unspecified contingent consideration — below their earlier offer of $225.00 per share. On the same day, Celgene CEO Robert Hugin contacted Hasnain to express renewed interest in acquisition discussions. The June 17 closing price was $177.48.

On June 23, 2015, the Company Board held a telephonic meeting; Hasnain updated the Board on the renewed communications. On June 24, Celgene orally indicated $207.00 per share; Hasnain responded that Celgene would need to increase the price. The June 23 closing price was $189.65. On June 25 and 26, Hasnain and Celgene representatives continued discussions. On June 26, Celgene delivered a written, non-binding proposal at $230.00 per share and returned a revised Merger Agreement draft. The June 25 closing price was $183.54.

On June 27, 2015, the Company Board held a telephonic meeting with management, Centerview, and Latham & Watkins to review Celgene's proposal. The Board discussed whether to conduct a broader pre-signing market check, weighing the potential for a higher price or better terms against the risk that Celgene could withdraw its bid, the workforce disruption and operational risk of delay, and the timing and execution risk of a broader process. The Board acknowledged that prior media speculation in April and June 2015 had not attracted additional acquiror contacts, and concluded that the risks of a broader market check outweighed its potential advantages. The Board directed management to negotiate for a higher price.

On June 28, Hasnain informed Celgene a higher price was required. On June 30, following additional discussions, Celgene orally increased its offer to $232.00 per share.

On July 1, 2015, the Company Board held a meeting to review the $232.00 offer and Celgene's initial comments to the Merger Agreement. Key terms under discussion included the Board's ability to change its recommendation, the parties' obligations to consummate, and termination fees. The Board instructed Latham & Watkins to negotiate more favorable terms. Latham & Watkins sent a revised draft to Proskauer on July 2.

During the week of July 5, face-to-face due diligence sessions were held on July 9 and 10. Latham & Watkins and Proskauer negotiated remaining Merger Agreement provisions. The Company Board held a telephonic meeting on the afternoon of July 10 to receive a status update on negotiations and a financial review from Centerview. Latham & Watkins sent a further revised Merger Agreement to Proskauer that evening.

Between July 12 and July 14, the parties resolved all open issues: the Company Termination Fee was set at $230.0 million (approximately 2.95% of aggregate equity value), the Parent Termination Fee was set at $400.0 million (approximately 5.1% of aggregate equity value), and the Termination Loan was set at up to $350.0 million if requested by the Company in the event the Merger Agreement were terminated for failure to obtain regulatory approvals.

On the afternoon of July 14, 2015, the Company Board held a special telephonic meeting. Latham & Watkins reviewed fiduciary duties. Management and Latham & Watkins reviewed the negotiated terms. Centerview rendered its oral fairness opinion (confirmed in writing that day) that the $232.00 per share cash consideration was fair, from a financial point of view, to the holders of Company Shares. The Company Board unanimously approved the Merger Agreement and recommended that stockholders tender their shares. The compensation committee had separately recommended approval of the excise tax gross-up arrangements for certain officers and the Chairman. The Merger Agreement was executed and delivered on the afternoon of July 14, 2015, and a joint press release was issued after the market close.

On July 14, 2015, Faheem Hasnain, President and CEO, and William H. Rastetter, Ph.D., Chairman of the Board, entered into a Tender and Support Agreement in their capacities as stockholders. As of July 28, 2015, Hasnain beneficially owned 384,409 shares and Rastetter beneficially owned 332,234 shares (exclusive of stock options and RSU awards).

The Support Agreement terminates if the Merger Agreement is terminated or the Company Board changes its recommendation, which would have permitted both Hasnain and Rastetter to support a competing superior proposal.

No financial advisor conflict disclosures were reported in the SC 14D9 with respect to Centerview; the filing stated that in the past two years Centerview had not provided investment banking or other services to the Company, Parent, or Purchaser for which it had received compensation.

The SC 14D9 does not disclose the identities of Party A, Party B, or Party C by name.

## Rationale

The SC 14D9 identifies the following material factors and benefits that the Company Board cited in its unanimous determination to approve the Merger Agreement and recommend that stockholders accept the Offer and tender their shares:

The Board cited the Offer Price's premium to market price, noting it represented approximately 17.7% above the July 13, 2015 closing price and approximately 44.6% above the June 9, 2015 closing price (the trading day before media speculation about a sale). The Board also cited the certainty of value that the all-cash consideration provided, compared to the risks and uncertainties of continuing as a standalone company.

With respect to the Company's standalone prospects, the Board identified the following risks: FDA approval risk for ozanimod and RPC4046; uncertainty of future clinical trial outcomes; the need for substantial additional capital (and associated dilution) to fund clinical programs and commercialization; execution risks of transforming from a development-stage company into a profitable commercial pharmaceutical company; competitive market conditions; patent risk; and going-concern risks associated with continued net losses.

The Board considered potential strategic alternatives, including continuing as an independent company, and concluded that none of the alternatives was reasonably likely to create greater value for stockholders, taking into account execution risk and market risk. The Board also considered the risk that Celgene could withdraw its proposal if the Company delayed in proceeding.

The Board cited the negotiations with Celgene and the terms of the Merger Agreement, including the Board's view that $232.00 represented the highest price per share that Celgene was willing to pay, as supported by the progression of negotiations from $193.00 (February 25, 2015) to $230.00 (June 26, 2015) to $232.00 (June 30, 2015). The Board also cited: the fiduciary termination right and ability to respond to unsolicited acquisition proposals; the Parent Termination Fee of $400.0 million and Termination Loan of up to $350.0 million; the extension rights for the tender offer; the absence of a financing condition; and the anticipated timing of completion.

The Board cited the fairness opinion of Centerview Partners LLC rendered on July 14, 2015, to the effect that the $232.00 per share consideration was fair, from a financial point of view, to holders of Common Stock (other than excluded shares).
