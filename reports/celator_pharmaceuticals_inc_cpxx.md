## Summary

Celator Pharmaceuticals Inc, a pre-commercial biopharmaceutical company developing the drug VYXEOS (CPX-351), a liposomal formulation of cytarabine and daunorubicin for acute myeloid leukemia, was acquired by Irish pharmaceutical company Jazz Pharmaceuticals plc in an all-cash transaction announced on May 27, 2016, and completed on July 12, 2016. The transaction valued Celator at $30.25 per share in cash, representing significant premiums over the trading price before Phase 3 clinical trial results were disclosed on March 14-15, 2016. The deal was structured as a tender offer followed by a merger under Delaware General Corporation Law Section 251(h), allowing the acquirer to effect the merger without requiring stockholder approval.

## Interaction History

The history of Celator's engagement with Jazz Pharmaceuticals and other potential bidders reflects a disciplined process structured by the company's Board to maximize stockholder value following positive clinical trial results.

Nicole Vitullo, a member of the Board, initially introduced Scott Jackson to a Jazz representative in November 2014. Jackson and Derek Miller (Chief Business Officer) met with Jazz representatives at an industry conference in San Francisco on December 6, 2014, where representatives of each party provided oral presentations regarding their respective businesses. Following this initial meeting, the companies executed a mutual confidentiality agreement on December 22, 2014. Miller and other Celator representatives met Jazz representatives again on January 12, 2015 at a San Francisco industry conference, where they discussed commercial assumptions and opportunities for VYXEOS.

Throughout 2015, Celator's strategy focused on pursuing a business development path intended to find partnership arrangements to commercialize VYXEOS outside the United States. The company held routine management meetings with Jazz and other pharmaceutical companies to discuss potential strategic transactions, with due diligence in financial, commercial, clinical, legal and regulatory matters. In March and April 2015, both Jazz and another company (referred to as Party A) submitted non-binding indications of interest to acquire Celator for $3.00 to $4.00 per share upfront plus $4.00 to $6.75 per share in contingent value rights for milestone achievements relating to VYXEOS development and commercialization. In response to these initial indications, the Board formed a Transaction Committee on March 17, 2015 consisting of five directors: Nicole Vitullo, Scott Jackson, Michael Dougherty, Richard Kollender and Scott Morenstein.

During spring 2015, the Board and Transaction Committee held several telephonic meetings with management and advisors (MTS Health Partners and Duane Morris) to discuss the indications from Jazz and Party A. These discussions did not advance beyond an early stage and did not progress to negotiating definitive agreements. The company continued to pursue its standalone business plan, completing a public offering on October 28, 2014 that raised approximately $13.6 million to fund the Company's operational plan.

On June 24, 2015, Celator publicly announced final induction response rate results from its Phase 3 trial comparing VYXEOS to standard-of-care cytarabine-daunorubicin in patients with untreated high-risk (secondary) acute myeloid leukemia. The stock traded at $1.68 per share at the close on March 14, 2016 (the announcement date). Following this significant milestone, from June 2015 through March 14, 2016, Celator continued to focus on finding potential partnership arrangements to commercialize VYXEOS outside the United States, with the company entering into a significant number of confidentiality agreements and conducting routine dialogue with other companies and representatives.

The engagement dynamics shifted materially on March 14, 2016, when Celator announced positive results from its Phase 3 trial. The trial met its primary endpoint, demonstrating a statistically significant improvement in overall survival compared to standard-of-care therapy. The stock price jumped to $8.94 per share by March 15, 2016, reflecting market enthusiasm for the clinical data. Following this announcement, Celator received a significant number of unsolicited inquiries from parties that were already under confidentiality agreements as well as additional interested parties. The company entered into 14 additional confidentiality agreements and made relevant non-public information available. In total, 41 parties under confidentiality agreements expressed interest in discussing opportunities for VYXEOS.

On March 24, 2016, Party B's Chief Executive Officer called Michael Dougherty and Scott Jackson expressing interest in VYXEOS. Later that same month, on March 29, 2016, Celator completed a public offering of 4.6 million shares at $9.50 per share, raising capital for regulatory, manufacturing, commercial and clinical activities related to VYXEOS and general corporate purposes.

On April 4, 2016, a Jazz representative contacted Derek Miller to congratulate him on the positive trial results and express Jazz's desire to re-engage in discussions regarding a strategic transaction with Celator. This re-engagement initiated the events that would ultimately lead to the transaction. On April 18, 2016, Derek Miller and other Celator representatives met with Jazz representatives at an industry conference in New Orleans to discuss a draft agenda for an upcoming in-person meeting scheduled for April 26, 2016, covering medical, commercial, clinical and regulatory issues.

The in-person meeting on April 26, 2016 was held at the offices of Duane Morris (Celator's corporate counsel) in Philadelphia. Derek Miller and other Celator representatives met with Jazz representatives for presentations and due diligence discussions covering a range of topics with specific focus on medical, commercial, clinical and regulatory issues.

On April 28, 2016, Jazz CEO Bruce Cozadd called Michael Dougherty and Scott Jackson. Following this call, the company received a written non-binding indication of interest from Jazz to acquire Celator for $20.00 per share in cash, subject to due diligence and other customary conditions. Jazz requested a response by May 3, 2016 and indicated preparedness to promptly finalize due diligence and negotiate definitive agreements on an accelerated timeline. The stock closed at $14.84 per share on April 28, 2016.

That same day, the Transaction Committee held a telephonic meeting with a majority of the Board, management and representatives of MTS, Duane Morris and Kirkland & Ellis (which had been retained as legal advisor in May 2015). The Committee determined that Jazz's indication was credible and warranted further exploration. The Committee and other Board members present then discussed the pros and cons of exploring Jazz's indication versus exploring interest from other potential bidders. After discussion, the Transaction Committee and Board members present directed MTS to contact Jazz to indicate the company was considering their proposal while the Board evaluated other indications of interest. The Committee and Board also directed MTS to engage with approximately 35 other pharmaceutical and biotechnology companies to explore their interest in a strategic transaction with Celator.

Between April 28 and May 2, 2016, MTS representatives contacted each of approximately 35 Potential Bidders to gauge their interest in pursuing a strategic transaction. MTS informed Jazz that the company would not respond to their May 3 deadline given the Board's intention to engage in a competitive process with other Potential Bidders. Jazz was also informed the company would soon provide further information regarding the anticipated timeline.

On May 2, 2016, the Board held a telephonic meeting with management and advisors. Kirkland & Ellis reviewed the Board's fiduciary duties when considering strategic transactions. MTS reviewed a preliminary financial analysis based on company projections and Jazz's indication of interest. MTS also described ongoing contacts with Potential Bidders. The Board discussed potential conflicts of interest and the importance of addressing such issues. The Board weighed the potential benefits of a May 20, 2016 submission deadline (including potential for higher-value transactions and more favorable terms from bidders needing time to formulate proposals) against risks of delaying engagement with Jazz (including possibility that Jazz might withdraw). After discussion, the Board determined that discussions should not be limited to acquisition but should also include partnerships to commercialize VYXEOS outside the United States. The Board directed that a letter be sent to Potential Bidders with a May 20, 2016 submission deadline for proposals.

Beginning later on May 2, 2016, MTS distributed a letter to each Potential Bidder requesting proposals for a "Qualified Transaction" by May 20, 2016. The letter indicated that Celator's proposed form of merger agreement would follow and that acquisition proposals would need to include a markup of the merger agreement.

During May 2-20, 2016, the company held management meetings with representatives of Potential Bidders for due diligence. On May 11, 2016, Derek Miller and other Celator representatives met with Party D representatives at dinner, where Party D requested information relating to VYXEOS. On May 17, 2016, Scott Jackson, Michael Dougherty and other Celator representatives held a telephonic conference with Party B representatives to discuss organizational structure and clinical topics.

On May 11, 2016, the Board held a telephonic meeting to receive an update on MTS's outreach to Potential Bidders. MTS provided a presentation listing each Potential Bidder, their level of interest, type of Qualified Transaction likely to be pursued, capacity to engage timely, and outstanding due diligence items. Following discussion, the Board confirmed that the range of Potential Bidders contacted was appropriate. Kirkland & Ellis provided a summary of Celator's proposed form of merger agreement. Following discussion, the Board instructed MTS to provide the draft merger agreement to Potential Bidders.

On May 20, 2016, Celator received six non-binding indications of interest. Four of these (from Jazz, Party B, Party C and Party D) contemplated an acquisition of Celator. Two (from companies referred to as Party E and Party F) contemplated partnership arrangements to commercialize VYXEOS outside the United States. Jazz submitted a proposal to acquire Celator for $20.00 per share in cash. Party B proposed $21.00 per share in cash. Party C proposed $18.00 per share in cash. Party D proposed $21.00 per share in cash. With respect to partnerships, Party E proposed $10 million upfront plus milestone payments up to $80 million on net sales milestones and 5% royalty. Party F proposed 30 million euros upfront plus milestone payments up to 80 million euros and 10-20% royalty. The stock closed at $16.90 per share on May 20, 2016.

On May 22, 2016, the Board held a telephonic meeting with management and advisors. Management reviewed financial matters and company projections. MTS reviewed preliminary financial analysis of the company and the indications received. Following discussion on the risks and benefits of continuing as a standalone company (including execution risks in building commercial infrastructure for the first commercial product), the Board determined that a sale would be more attractive than a partnership to maximize stockholder value. The Board focused on principal merger agreement issues including the definition of "material adverse effect" and deal protection provisions. The Board believed the process was robust and competitive, with potential to obtain higher value and more favorable terms. The Board directed MTS to inform each of Jazz, Party B, Party C and Party D that the process was highly competitive and they could increase their per-share indications by May 25, 2016.

Following the May 22 meeting, MTS distributed a letter to each acquisition bidder encouraging them to resubmit proposals with improved terms. MTS requested revised proposals by May 25, 2016. Kirkland & Ellis provided a revised draft merger agreement to each bidder based on the most favorable markup received on May 20. Representatives of MTS informed Party E and Party F that Celator would only be considering acquisition proposals going forward. Both indicated they would not be able to submit revised competitive acquisition proposals in the required timeframe.

On May 25, 2016, Party C informed MTS it was no longer interested in pursuing a transaction, as it did not believe it could be competitive from an economic standpoint.

Also on May 25, 2016, Celator received revised indications from Jazz, Party B and Party D. Jazz submitted a revised non-binding indication of interest to acquire Celator for $23.00 per share in cash, subject to due diligence and customary conditions, accompanied by a markup of the merger agreement. Party B proposed $23.00 per share in cash plus $2.00 per share in contingent value rights payable upon FDA approval of VYXEOS, subject to due diligence and customary conditions. Party D proposed $24.50 per share in cash, subject to due diligence and exclusivity through June 7, 2016.

On May 26, 2016, the Board held a telephonic meeting with management and advisors to discuss the May 25 indications. MTS reviewed preliminary financial analysis of the revised indications. The Board discussed principal issues including the definition of "material adverse effect" and "no-shop" provisions. The Board felt strongly that Party D's request for exclusivity should be rejected at that time. The Board and management discussed the effect of these provisions on certainty of closing. Management updated the Board on due diligence status with Jazz, Party B and Party D. Representatives of MTS also updated the Board on communications with two other large-cap publicly traded companies that had been substantially involved in due diligence. These two potential bidders had previously indicated they intended to submit proposals but subsequently indicated they could not submit competitive indications given the valuations already offered by other bidders. Following discussion, the Board believed the process remained very competitive with potential to obtain even higher value and more favorable merger terms. The Board directed MTS to inform Jazz, Party B and Party D that they would have the opportunity for "best and final" proposals by May 27, 2016. The stock closed at $17.50 per share on May 26, 2016.

On May 26, 2016, at the Board's direction, MTS contacted Jazz, Party B and Party D to discuss the ongoing process, highlighting its competitive nature and requesting "best and final" proposals by May 27, 2016. MTS distributed a letter to each party requesting revised proposals by May 27, 2016. During that day, Kirkland & Ellis received calls from each party's counsel to discuss issues identified in their markups of the merger agreement.

Between the evening of May 26 and the morning of May 27, 2016, Celator received revised indications from each party. Jazz submitted a revised indication to acquire Celator for $30.25 per share in cash, accompanied by a markup of the merger agreement. This represented a significant jump from Jazz's previous best proposal of $23.00 per share. Party B submitted a revised indication of $25.50 per share in cash. Party D indicated its proposal would remain at $24.50 per share in cash. Each party again indicated it would seek support agreements from certain stockholders as a condition to signing.

On the morning of May 27, 2016, the Board held a telephonic meeting with management and advisors. MTS reviewed preliminary financial analysis and the revised indications from all three remaining bidders. MTS indicated that each party understood the competitive nature of the process and the request for best and final proposals. Kirkland & Ellis indicated that if the Board decided to proceed, a merger agreement with Jazz could be executed within a day or two. After discussion, the Board directed MTS to communicate to Jazz that it had been selected as the winning bidder in the process and discuss the goal of executing a definitive agreement in a timely manner.

Following the Board meeting, MTS contacted Jazz on May 27, 2016 to inform them of the Board's decision and discuss outstanding items and timeline to execute the merger agreement. Kirkland & Ellis contacted Jazz's counsel (Cooley LLP) to discuss the same matters.

On the day of May 27, 2016, representatives of Kirkland & Ellis and Cooley worked to finalize the merger agreement and Celator's disclosure letter. Members of management, Kirkland & Ellis, Duane Morris and Morrison & Foerster LLP (intellectual property counsel) participated on a call with Jazz representatives and Cooley to answer Jazz's outstanding confirmatory due diligence questions and questions relating to Celator's disclosure letter.

On the afternoon of May 27, 2016, the Board held a telephonic meeting to consider approval of the proposed merger agreement with Jazz and Plex Merger Sub. At this meeting, management and Kirkland & Ellis reviewed with the Board the outcome of negotiations with Jazz and the terms of the proposed merger agreement compared to Celator's proposed form dated May 11, 2016. The Board asked questions and discussion ensued. MTS reviewed its financial analysis of the $30.25 per share consideration and rendered an oral opinion (subsequently confirmed in writing that same date) to the effect that, as of May 27, 2016 and based on the assumptions, qualifications and limitations contained in the opinion, the $30.25 per share consideration was fair, from a financial point of view, to holders of Celator Common Stock (other than Jazz, Plex Merger Sub and their respective affiliates).

After discussions with financial and legal advisors and management, and in light of the reasons considered (including the process undertaken, the premium to market, the cash certainty, the risks of remaining independent, and the fairness opinion), the Board unanimously determined that the tender offer, the Merger and the other transactions contemplated by the Merger Agreement were fair to and in the best interest of Celator and its stockholders. The Board approved and declared advisable the Merger and authorized execution, delivery and performance of the Merger Agreement. The Board also approved an amendment to the bylaws establishing the Delaware Court of Chancery as the exclusive forum for the adjudication of certain types of legal actions brought by stockholders (derivative actions and other intracorporal claims).

On the evening of May 27, 2016, the parties executed the Merger Agreement. Jazz and certain Celator stockholders and officers executed support and tender agreements committing to tender their shares in the offer.

Prior to the opening of U.S. stock markets on May 31, 2016, Jazz and Celator issued a joint press release announcing the execution of the Merger Agreement.

On June 10, 2016, Plex Merger Sub commenced the tender offer at $30.25 per share. The offer was scheduled to expire 20 business days later, on July 11, 2016.

The tender offer deadline was July 11, 2016 at 11:59 p.m. New York time. As of the expiration, American Stock Transfer & Trust Company (the depositary) reported that 36,516,173 shares had been validly tendered (not validly withdrawn), representing approximately 81.13% of outstanding shares. Additionally, guaranteed delivery notices had been delivered for 2,016,237 additional shares, representing approximately 4.48% of outstanding shares. The minimum tender condition had been satisfied. Purchaser accepted for payment all shares validly tendered prior to expiration, with payment to be made promptly according to the offer terms.

On July 12, 2016, the Merger was completed. Plex Merger Sub was merged with and into Celator, with Celator as the surviving corporation. Celator became an indirect wholly-owned subsidiary of Jazz. Each outstanding share (other than shares held by stockholders who properly demanded appraisal under Delaware law Section 262) was converted into the right to receive $30.25 in cash.

In connection with the Merger completion, Celator repaid in full all outstanding loans under the Loan and Security Agreement with Hercules Technology Growth Capital, Inc. (dated May 9, 2014, as amended April 25, 2016) together with all interest and other amounts due, and terminated all commitments under the loan agreement except for certain indemnification obligations.

On July 12, 2016, Celator requested that NASDAQ halt trading of the shares, suspend listing and delist the shares from NASDAQ. The Company requested NASDAQ file with the SEC a Form 25 for delisting and deregistration under Section 12(b) of the Securities Exchange Act. The Company also indicated its intention to file a Form 15 with the SEC for deregistration under Section 12(g) and suspension of reporting obligations under Sections 13 and 15(d) of the Exchange Act.

## Rationale

The Board's rationale for the transaction was grounded in the strategic opportunity created by positive Phase 3 clinical results, the risks and execution challenges of remaining independent, and the attractive valuation achieved through a competitive bidding process.

The Board evaluated the company's prospects and risks as a standalone entity, including the uncertainties and execution risks associated with: (i) completing regulatory approval of VYXEOS following the positive Phase 3 results announced March 14, 2016; (ii) successfully manufacturing VYXEOS at commercial scale; (iii) building a commercial infrastructure and launching the company's first commercial product; (iv) managing risks associated with the competitive oncology market and pricing/reimbursement environment; (v) depending on partnerships for VYXEOS commercialization outside the United States; (vi) meeting future funding requirements; and (vii) navigating uncertain capital markets. The Phase 3 data, while positive, represented an inflection point but not a guarantee of regulatory success, commercialization success, or financial return adequate to compensate shareholders for continued execution risk.

The Board considered that a cash tender offer and merger provided certainty of value ($30.25 per share, all cash) compared to the uncertain value creation potential of a standalone pursuit of VYXEOS development and commercialization. The Board noted that the $30.25 offer price represented a 73% premium to the May 26 closing price ($17.50), a 101% premium to the 30-day volume-weighted average price preceding May 26, a 123% premium to the volume-weighted average since March 15 (post-Phase 3 announcement), and a 604% premium to the one-year volume-weighted average preceding May 26.

The Board evaluated strategic alternatives to the Jazz acquisition, including the possibility of remaining independent to pursue standalone commercialization or entering a partnership arrangement to commercialize VYXEOS outside the United States. Party E proposed $10 million upfront plus milestone payments up to $80 million and 5% royalty; Party F proposed 30 million euros upfront plus milestone payments up to 80 million euros and 10-20% royalty on net sales. The Board determined that these partnership proposals would expose the company to substantial execution risk for revenues potentially far below the $1.3 billion market capitalization at offer price, and concluded that a sale offered superior value certainty to shareholders compared to remaining independent or entering partnerships.

Jazz's strategic interest in the transaction centered on the addition of VYXEOS to its portfolio of established oncology products and the potential for commercial acceleration through Jazz's established sales, marketing and commercial infrastructure. Jazz's due diligence focused on the Phase 3 clinical data, manufacturing scale-up potential, regulatory pathway to FDA approval, commercial assumptions, and market opportunity in AML. The Board believed Jazz's existing oncology franchise and commercial capabilities positioned the company to maximize the probability and value of successful VYXEOS commercialization.

The competitive process undertaken by the Board—structured to maximize stockholder value by enlisting 35+ potential bidders, conducting four rounds of bidding over May 20-27, and driving per-share offers from $20.00 (Jazz initial bid April 28) to $30.25 (Jazz best final bid May 27)—demonstrated the highest value reasonably obtainable. Jazz's willingness to escalate its bid substantially from $23.00 (May 25) to $30.25 (May 27) in response to the Board's direction that it submit a best and final proposal indicated the company had reached the highest price Jazz was prepared to pay for the transaction.

The Board further noted the favorable Merger Agreement terms protecting stockholder interests, including: (i) a definition of "material adverse effect" that allocated to Jazz the risks of regulatory, clinical or manufacturing changes to VYXEOS; (ii) a termination fee of $45.8 million (approximately 3% of deal equity value), which the Board determined was reasonable and unlikely to deter competing bids; (iii) the Board's right to participate in discussions or negotiations with third parties proposing superior proposals that met the definition of "Superior Company Proposal"; (iv) the Board's right to change its recommendation under specified circumstances; (v) no stockholder vote requirement under Section 251(h) of the Delaware General Corporation Law; and (vi) the structure as an all-cash tender offer followed by merger, allowing stockholders to receive payment in a relatively short timeframe.
