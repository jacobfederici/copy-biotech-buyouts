## Summary

On October 17, 2011, Anadys Pharmaceuticals, Inc. announced it had entered into a definitive merger agreement to be acquired by Hoffmann-La Roche Inc. through a tender offer of $3.70 per share in cash. The acquisition was driven by Roche's interest in Anadys' Phase IIb hepatitis C direct-acting antiviral, setrobuvir. The transaction closed on November 23, 2011.

## Interaction History

In March 2010, the board of directors of Anadys met with investment banking firms, including Lazard Frères & Co. LLC, to assist in exploring potential strategic opportunities, including a sale, in anticipation of 12-week clinical data from a Phase IIa trial of setrobuvir. Anadys engaged Lazard in May 2010. Following directives from the board, Lazard contacted 15 pharmaceutical companies, including Roche. Twelve of these companies were already under confidentiality agreements, and seven proceeded with scientific due diligence. On August 25, 2010, Anadys and Roche held a teleconference where Anadys provided a clinical update on setrobuvir. However, based on feedback, the board determined that the contacted parties were not interested in pursuing a strategic transaction at that time and required more clinical data on setrobuvir. As a result, the board decided to raise capital, pricing a $25 million offering at $1.80 per share on October 15, 2010, and pursue a Phase IIb trial for setrobuvir.

Following the offering, Anadys and Lazard maintained dialogue with at least five parties, including Roche, who had indicated potential interest pending further data. Between October 2010 and August 2011, Anadys held multiple meetings and discussions with these parties to provide clinical updates and discuss possible combination studies. Representatives of Anadys met with Roche several times at industry conferences, including on October 31, 2010, January 12, 2011, February 24, 2011, and March 31, 2011. 

On May 27, 2011, Dr. Worland (CEO of Anadys) and Kevin Eastwood (SVP of Business Development) met with a Roche representative, who indicated Roche's interest in adding a complementary direct-acting antiviral to its portfolio. On June 14, 2011, Roche confirmed its interest in exploring opportunities and conducting due diligence on setrobuvir. In July 2011, Roche commenced technical due diligence. On August 26, 2011, Roche submitted a non-binding proposal to acquire Anadys for $3.00 per share.

The board reviewed the $3.00 proposal on August 27 and September 2, 2011, determining that it did not represent full value, and instructed Lazard to contact other potentially interested parties while negotiating with Roche. On September 15, 2011, Lazard sent bid request letters to Roche and two other parties (Party B and Party C). Party B and Party C ultimately declined to submit bids, citing the need for drug-drug interaction studies or a lack of interest. 

On October 4, 2011, Anadys provided Roche with unblinded 12-week data from the Phase IIb study of setrobuvir. On October 6, 2011, Roche submitted a revised non-binding proposal of $3.70 per share. On October 7, the board authorized Lazard to counter at $4.50 per share. On October 8, Lazard conveyed the counterproposal, but Roche responded the next day that $3.70 per share was its final offer. On October 10, Anadys informed Roche it was willing to proceed at $3.70 per share on a non-exclusive basis to finalize the merger agreement. Following final negotiations and the receipt of a fairness opinion from Lazard, the Anadys board approved the merger agreement on October 16, 2011. The transaction was announced on October 17, 2011.

## Rationale

The board considered the estimated $850 million in capital required to fund the clinical development of setrobuvir and ANA773 through marketing approval and commercial launch, and the substantial time and investment still needed to achieve marketing approval. The board also recognized that the development of setrobuvir might necessarily be in combination with other direct-acting antivirals, making it more valuable as part of a larger portfolio of direct-acting antiviral drugs. Evaluating the potential for creating value as a stand-alone company, the board factored in the significant dilution to existing stockholders that would be required to raise the necessary funds, ultimately determining that the $3.70 per share cash offer provided better and more certain value.
