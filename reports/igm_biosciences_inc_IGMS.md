## Summary

IGM Biosciences entered into a definitive agreement to be acquired by Concentra Biosciences, LLC, an affiliate of Tang Capital Partners, for $1.247 per share in cash plus a contingent value right. The transaction followed a strategic pivot in January 2025 after imvotamab data failed to meet competitive benchmarks, leading the board to conclude that a financial buyer transaction offered greater certainty and value than a liquidation or a reverse merger with strategic partners.

## Timeline

- 2024-09-27: IGM commits to a strategic pivot to prioritize autoimmune diseases.
- 2025-01-07: IGM Board determines imvotamab data is insufficient for competitive profile; decides to terminate development and explore strategic alternatives.

- 2025-08-14: Merger completed via DGCL Section 251(h).

## Interaction History

Following the failure of imvotamab data in early 2025, IGM management, led by CEO Mary Beth Harler, evaluated over 130 opportunities including mergers and asset acquisitions. Between January and May 2025, IGM engaged with several strategic parties (Parties A through F). Party A proposed a merger of equals but negotiations stalled over board control and leadership. Other parties (C, D, E, F) proposed reverse mergers with valuations the Special Committee found unattractive. On May 21, 2025, the Special Committee shifted focus to financial buyers. IGM contacted Parties G, H, I, and Parent (Concentra). Party I required third-party financing, which was viewed as a risk. Party G offered $78 million but required a longer timeline. Parent initially offered $75.5 million ($1.204/share) on June 6. After feedback that the offer was insufficient, Parent increased its bid to $77 million on June 13 and finally to $78 million ($1.246/share, later adjusted to $1.247 based on share count) on June 18. The Board selected Parent due to its track record of closing quickly, which preserved more 'Closing Net Cash' for the CVR. Houlihan Lokey provided a fairness opinion on July 1, 2025, and the deal was signed.

## Rationale

The IGM Board determined that the transaction provided the best value reasonably obtainable. Key factors included the immediate liquidity of the cash payment, the potential upside from the CVR, and the high certainty of closing compared to a standalone liquidation or a risky reverse merger. The board noted that a statutory liquidation would likely result in lower distributions ($1.16 - $1.24 per share) due to wind-down costs and the need for a holdback reserve.

## Decision Notes

The deal was completed via a tender offer followed by a merger under Section 251(h) of the DGCL. The CVR includes two components: a cash-sweep of net cash exceeding $82M at closing and a 1-year tail on 80% of proceeds from the sale of the IgM platform assets. Shares outstanding include both voting and non-voting common stock as both were eligible for the same consideration.
