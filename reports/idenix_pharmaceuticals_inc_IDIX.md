## Summary

Merck & Co., Inc., through its subsidiary Imperial Blue Corporation, acquired Idenix Pharmaceuticals for $24.50 per share in an all-cash tender offer valued at approximately $3.85 billion on a fully diluted basis. The acquisition was designed to bolster Merck's Hepatitis C (HCV) portfolio, specifically acquiring Idenix's promising nucleotide prodrug candidates. The transaction was completed via a second-step merger under Section 251(h) of the DGCL following the successful tender of approximately 82.5% of outstanding shares.

## Interaction History

Negotiations began in late 2013 with Merck's interest in IDX-21437. In early 2014, Idenix received unsolicited interest from Party A and Party B. Idenix CEO Ronald Renaud and Merck's Iain Dukes (SVP Business Development) maintained contact throughout the spring. After receiving a $9.78/share bid from Merck on May 9, the Idenix Board, advised by Centerview and Sullivan & Cromwell, initiated a competitive bidding process. Party B withdrew on May 30. On June 3, Merck bid $18.00 and Party A bid $14.00. Following a request for 'best and final' offers, Party A moved to $18.50 and then $19.75. Merck significantly increased its bid to $24.50 on June 8. The Board determined Merck's offer was financially superior. A key component was the Support Agreement from The Baupost Group (14.9% holder), which Merck required as a condition of the deal.

## Rationale

The Idenix Board recommended the deal due to the significant 239% premium over the unaffected stock price, the certainty of cash consideration, and the risks associated with remaining an independent clinical-stage company. Specifically, the Board noted the high execution risk in commercializing HCV therapies and the ongoing, complex patent litigation with Gilead Sciences regarding Sovaldi.

## Evidence

- At a meeting held on June 8, 2014, the Board unanimously: (i) approved and declared advisable the Merger Agreement and the Offer... (ii) determined that the Merger Agreement and such transactions are fair to and in the best interests of the Company and its stockholders... (0001193125-14-244635)
- On August 5, 2014, as a result of its acceptance of, and payment for, the Shares tendered in the Offer, Merger Sub acquired a sufficient number of Shares to complete the merger... without a vote of the stockholders... pursuant to Section 251(h) (0001193125-14-295760)

## Decision Notes

The deal value is calculated based on 151,379,334 shares outstanding as of June 18, 2014, multiplied by the $24.50 offer price. The fully diluted value of ~$3.85B accounts for the 8.2M outstanding options mentioned in Item 1. The transaction utilized the then-new Section 251(h) of the DGCL to bypass a shareholder meeting.

## Buyout Rumors

Source: OpenRouter web search (Gemini 3 Flash Preview). Pre-deal rumors only.

Before the confirmed announcement of its acquisition by Merck & Co. on June 9, 2014, Idenix Pharmaceuticals (IDIX) was a frequent subject of takeover speculation, primarily driven by the high-stakes race to develop oral Hepatitis C (HCV) treatments.

The following is a chronological timeline of rumors and speculation regarding a potential buyout of Idenix:

*   **Early 2012**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *FierceBiotech* (referencing broader market sentiment)
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Unspecified (General "Big Pharma" interest)
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Not specified
    *   **Details:** Idenix was reported to be one of the "most hotly rumored takeover targets" in the biotechnology sector at the start of 2012. This speculation was fueled by the "HCV M&A craze" following Gilead’s $11 billion acquisition of Pharmasset in late 2011.

*   **August 2012**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *TheStreet* (Adam Feuerstein) / *FierceBiotech*
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Unspecified
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Not specified
    *   **Details:** Speculation reached a peak but was abruptly "quashed" when the FDA placed a clinical hold on Idenix’s lead drug candidate, IDX184, due to safety concerns (cardiac toxicity) seen in a similar Bristol-Myers Squibb program. Reports noted that this regulatory setback effectively killed the "takeover buzz" for the remainder of the year.

*   **November 6, 2012**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *FierceBiotech*
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Unspecified
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Not specified
    *   **Details:** Reports suggested that if Idenix could provide safety data to satisfy the FDA and resolve the clinical hold on IDX184, "takeover rumors" were expected to "reignite" immediately, as the company remained one of the few remaining independent players with a "nuc" (nucleotide inhibitor) for Hepatitis C.

*   **April – May 2014**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *Bloomberg* / *CNBC* (reported retrospectively on June 9, 2014, regarding the weeks leading up to the deal)
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** **Johnson & Johnson (J&J)** and **AbbVie**
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Competitive bidding process
    *   **Details:** Following positive Phase I/II data for Idenix’s new candidate, IDX21437, in April 2014, the company reportedly became the center of a quiet but intense bidding war. While not public at the time, it was later confirmed by Merck’s R&D chief, Roger Perlmutter, that Idenix had been approached by another company (later identified by CNBC as J&J or AbbVie), which triggered a formal sale process.

*   **June 6, 2014 (The Friday before the Monday announcement)**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *Various market observers/StockTwits*
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** **Merck** or **Gilead**
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Not specified
    *   **Details:** Unusual options activity and a slight uptick in volume led to renewed social media and floor speculation that a deal in the HCV space was imminent, though no specific price was leaked before the official Monday morning announcement.

**Note on the Final Deal:** On June 9, 2014, Merck & Co. officially announced it would acquire Idenix for **$24.50 per share** in cash, a **239% premium** over the previous Friday's closing price. Post-announcement reporting by *CNBC* and *FierceBiotech* confirmed that **Johnson & Johnson** and **AbbVie** were the unsuccessful bidders that had driven the price to that level.
