## Summary

Auspex Pharmaceuticals entered into a definitive agreement to be acquired by Teva Pharmaceutical Industries for $101.00 per share in an all-cash tender offer valued at approximately $3.5 billion. The transaction focused on Auspex's portfolio of deuterated medicines for movement disorders, particularly SD-809 for Huntington's disease chorea. The deal was completed via a Section 251(h) merger following the successful tender of approximately 78% of outstanding shares.

## Interaction History

Negotiations began in earnest following positive Phase 3 data for SD-809 in late 2014. Auspex, advised by J.P. Morgan, ran a competitive process involving Teva and five other pharmaceutical companies (Companies A, B, C, D, and E). Company D was a persistent bidder, offering a mix of stock, cash, and CVRs valued up to $3.0 billion. Company B conducted extensive diligence but ultimately declined to bid due to strategic fit concerns. Teva initially proposed a range of $85-$95 per share on February 24, 2015. After Auspex's board indicated they would not sell for less than the high end of that range, Teva increased its offer to $100 and finally $101 per share on March 27, 2015. The board favored Teva's all-cash offer over Company D's complex structure and tax-free spin-off proposal. The Merger Agreement was signed on March 29, 2015, featuring a 3% termination fee ($104 million).

## Rationale

The Auspex board determined that the $101.00 cash price provided immediate and certain value that outweighed the risks of remaining a standalone company. These risks included the high cost of building a commercial infrastructure, the uncertainty of FDA approval, and the potential need for dilutive future financing. Teva sought the acquisition to strengthen its CNS portfolio and leverage its existing commercial scale in movement disorders.
