## Summary

Endo Pharmaceuticals Holdings Inc. acquired Indevus Pharmaceuticals, Inc. for $4.50 per share in cash plus contingent value rights worth up to $3.00 per share based on regulatory and sales milestones for NEBIDO and octreotide. The transaction was structured as a tender offer followed by a short-form merger, significantly expanding Endo's portfolio in endocrinology and urology.

## Timeline

- 2008-09-19: Initial meeting between Indevus CEO Glenn Cooper and Endo CEO David Holveck to discuss strategic combination.
- 2008-09-30: Indevus and Endo enter into a mutual non-disclosure agreement.
- 2008-10-24: Endo submits non-binding proposal to acquire Indevus for $4.50 cash plus $2.00 CVR.
- 2008-10-26: Indevus Board rejects the $6.50 total offer as inadequate but authorizes 30-day due diligence period.
- 2008-12-12: Endo increases proposal to $4.50 cash plus up to $3.00 CVR ($7.50 total).
- 2009-01-05: Merger Agreement signed and transaction announced.
- 2009-01-07: Tender offer formally commences.
- 2009-02-04: Merger Agreement amended to reduce termination fee from $20M to $18M as part of a litigation settlement.
- 2009-02-20: Initial tender offer period expires with 77.97% of shares tendered.
- 2009-02-23: Endo accepts tendered shares and commences subsequent offering period.
- 2009-03-23: Merger completed; Indevus becomes a wholly-owned subsidiary of Endo and changes name to Endo Pharmaceuticals Solutions Inc.

## Interaction History

Negotiations began in September 2008 when Morgan Stanley, acting for Endo, contacted Indevus CEO Glenn Cooper. Following a confidentiality agreement on Sept 30, management teams met on Oct 1. Endo's initial Oct 24 proposal of $4.50 cash plus $2.00 CVR was rejected by the Indevus Board on Oct 26 as inadequate. Indevus granted a 30-day diligence period to encourage a higher bid. On Dec 12, Endo raised the offer to $4.50 cash plus $3.00 in CVRs. The Indevus Board reviewed stand-alone valuations, debt obligations (specifically $72M in notes due July 2009), and financing risks. On Dec 17, the Board requested an escrow for NEBIDO payments to mitigate credit risk. Final negotiations occurred through late December, shifting the structure from a one-step merger to a tender offer to accelerate closing. The deal was unanimously approved on Jan 5, 2009.

## Rationale

The Indevus Board determined the offer was superior to remaining independent due to significant capital requirements to fund the NEBIDO launch and the July 2009 maturity of $72 million in convertible promissory notes. The deal provided immediate cash at a 45% premium while allowing shareholders to retain upside through CVRs. Endo sought to diversify its portfolio with specialty urology and endocrinology products.

## Evidence

- The Company Board, during a meeting held on January 5, 2009, by unanimous vote determined that the Offer and the Merger are in the best interests of the Company and its stockholders (SC 14D9).
- On March 23, 2009, the merger of Purchaser with and into the Company was consummated (8-K, 2009-03-23).

## Buyout Rumors

Source: OpenRouter web search (Gemini 3 Flash Preview). Pre-deal rumors only.

No pre-deal rumors exist. 

While Indevus Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (formerly traded under the symbol IDEV) was the subject of significant market activity and insider trading investigations leading up to its acquisition by Endo Pharmaceuticals, there were no publicly reported buyout rumors, specific acquirer names, or rumored price premiums published by reporting outlets prior to the official deal announcement on January 5, 2009.

The acquisition was characterized by the following timeline of events:

*   **June 2008:** Indevus received a "complete response letter" from the FDA regarding its drug Nebido, which caused the stock to drop 69%. During this period, internal executives (such as the VP of Corporate Communications) engaged in insider trading based on the non-public FDA news, but no public rumors of a takeover by Endo or any other party surfaced at this time.
*   **Late 2008:** According to later SEC filings and legal proceedings, the company was privately exploring strategic alternatives, but these discussions remained confidential and did not leak to the press as specific rumors.
*   **January 5, 2009:** Endo Pharmaceuticals and Indevus Pharmaceuticals officially announced a definitive merger agreement. Endo agreed to acquire Indevus for $4.50 per share in cash, plus up to $3.00 per share in contingent cash rights (totaling approximately $637 million). This was the first public report of the transaction.
