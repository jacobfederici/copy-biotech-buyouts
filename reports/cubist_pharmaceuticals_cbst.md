## Summary

On December 8, 2014, Merck & Co. announced an agreement to acquire Cubist Pharmaceuticals for $102.00 per share in an all-cash tender offer followed by a short-form merger under Delaware General Corporation Law Section 251(h). The Cubist Board of Directors, represented by a Special Committee of independent directors with M&A experience, determined the offer was fair and in shareholders' best interests after conducting a market check process that contacted three other potential bidders. The tender offer commenced on December 19, 2014, with 58,039,667 shares (approximately 75.7% of outstanding shares) validly tendered by the January 20, 2015 expiration date. Merck accepted all tendered shares for payment on January 21, 2015, and completed the merger the same day, making Cubist a wholly-owned subsidiary of Merck. The transaction was not conditioned on Cubist's litigation against Hospira regarding daptomycin patents or on regulatory approval of the company's lead pipeline asset, ceftolozane/tazobactam.

## Interaction History

Cubist and Merck first discussed business opportunities in May 2014 when Cubist was considering possible partnering arrangements for ceftolozane/tazobactam outside the United States. After holding several meetings from May through September 2014, these discussions did not result in any arrangement.

On October 7, 2014, Kenneth C. Frazier, Merck's Chairman and CEO, initiated contact with Michael Bonney, Cubist's CEO, to express Merck's interest in a potential strategic transaction. Bonney immediately conferred with Kenneth Bate, Cubist's Board Chairman, about this overture.

On October 8, 2014, Frazier and Bonney met in person in New York City for dinner. Frazier expressed Merck's interest in acquiring Cubist at a price in "the high 80's" per share. Bonney responded that, based on prior Board discussions, this price range was likely insufficient to warrant Board-authorized substantive engagement. Following the meeting, Bonney informed Board Chairman Bate, President Robert Perez, and Chief Legal Officer Thomas DesRosier of the details.

On October 10, 2014, Bonney held a telephone conference with Board leadership, senior management, and representatives of Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley (the company's financial advisors) and Ropes & Gray (legal counsel). The group discussed Merck's preliminary proposal and next steps.

On October 13, 2014, the full Cubist Board held a telephonic meeting attended by all directors, senior management, and the company's advisors. After Bonney described his interactions with Frazier and counsel outlined the Board's fiduciary duties, the Board determined that at that time, the company was not for sale, but authorized Bonney to seek additional information and continue discussions with Frazier on specific terms. The Board also formed a Special Committee of three independent directors—Kenneth Bate (Chairman), Nancy Hutson, and Kenneth Martin—each with M&A experience from prior roles as senior executives and directors of other companies. The Special Committee was charged with overseeing strategic alternatives evaluation, reporting developments, and taking actions up to (but not including) entering into definitive agreements.

On October 16, 2014, Bonney called Frazier to relay the Board's response. Bonney emphasized that the timing was not optimal given the pending launch of ceftolozane/tazobactam and the company's leadership transition. He stated that Cubist would continue discussions only if Merck agreed to three conditions: (1) move quickly, (2) offer consideration exceeding $100.00 per share, and (3) commit that any definitive agreement would not be conditioned on litigation outcomes with Hospira or regulatory decisions regarding ceftolozane/tazobactam. Frazier indicated he needed to consult Merck's senior management and Board.

On October 17, 2014, the Special Committee held a telephonic meeting with senior management and advisors. Bonney provided an update on his conversation with Frazier. The Committee discussed available alternatives and preparation scenarios.

On October 18, 2014, the full Cubist Board held a telephonic meeting to discuss the pending CEO and Board Chairman transitions and to consider how Bonney should approach future communications with Frazier.

On October 23, 2014, Bonney and Frazier held two telephone conversations. Frazier stated that Merck remained interested and that senior management and the Merck Board wanted to continue discussions. Frazier outlined Merck's assumptions for the combined businesses and identified key diligence areas: long-term tax planning, the Hospira litigation, and regulatory dialogue on ceftolozane/tazobactam. Frazier indicated Merck was prepared to offer between $95.00 and $100.00 per share, with a portion contingent on the Hospira litigation outcome. He noted Merck was willing to accept regulatory risk but unwilling to assume all Hospira litigation risk. Bonney responded that Merck's proposal did not meet the Board's stated conditions and that he would review it with the Board.

Later on October 23, 2014, the Special Committee held a telephonic meeting. Bonney reported on his conversations with Frazier. Financial advisors provided commentary and suggested potential negotiation steps. The Committee advised Bonney to respond that Merck's current proposal did not satisfy the Board's conditions on price and conditionality regarding the Hospira litigation, and that Cubist would be unable to proceed unless Merck revised its proposal. The Committee suggested Bonney offer to facilitate a discussion between Merck and Cubist's outside patent counsel regarding the Hospira litigation, subject to Merck signing an appropriate confidentiality agreement.

On October 25, 2014, Bonney informed Frazier that the Special Committee considered the proposal inadequate on both price and Hospira litigation conditionality. Bonney offered to facilitate a discussion with Cubist's outside patent counsel regarding the Hospira litigation status, subject to Merck signing a confidentiality agreement.

On October 27, 2014, an unsolicited telephone call was received by Bonney from the Chief Executive Officer of another pharmaceutical company (referred to as "Party A") asking if Cubist might be open to a business combination. Bonney stated he would address the question with the Board and that Cubist was always willing to consider proposals increasing shareholder value.

On October 28, 2014, the Special Committee held a telephonic meeting. Bonney outlined plans to provide an updated long-range plan and discussed approaches to evaluating intrinsic value. Bonney reported on recent conversations with Frazier, and financial advisors commented on discussions to date and provided preliminary synergy analysis. Bonney also updated the Committee on the unsolicited call from Party A's CEO, though Party A never provided a specific proposal. The Committee discussed expectations for a planned in-person meeting with Frazier and Bruce Kuhlik (Merck's Executive Vice President and General Counsel) scheduled for November 3. Later on October 28, 2014, Cubist sent Merck a draft confidentiality agreement with standstill provisions in anticipation of the November 3 meeting.

On October 31, 2014, the Special Committee held a telephonic meeting attended by Committee members, senior management, and advisors. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley each provided preliminary financial analyses. The Committee received an updated long-range plan and discussed valuation methodologies and sensitivities. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley presented analysis of the company's outstanding convertible notes and the unwinding of associated hedging arrangements with three counterparties. Members inquired about likely payments and noted Morgan Stanley's position as one of three counterparties to those hedging arrangements. After the financial advisors left, the Special Committee determined that Cubist should enter into a confidentiality agreement with Merck.

On November 3, 2014, Cubist and Merck executed a confidentiality agreement with customary 12-month standstill provisions. Subsequently, Bonney and DesRosier met with Frazier and Kuhlik at Merck's headquarters, with discussion focused primarily on the Hospira litigation status. Frazier reinforced his statements about the strategic value of combining the companies and indicated that Merck was willing to meet Cubist's condition of a per-share price exceeding $100.00 and would not include conditionality related to the Hospira litigation or regulatory status of ceftolozane/tazobactam. The parties agreed to arrange diligence meetings on long-term tax issues and with Cubist's outside patent counsel on the Hospira litigation. Frazier stated that if these diligence meetings were satisfactory, Merck would make a specific, non-binding offer to acquire Cubist.

On November 4, 2014, representatives of Cubist and Merck held a telephonic meeting to discuss tax matters.

Later on November 4, 2014, the full Cubist Board held a telephonic meeting with senior management and advisors. On December 4, 2014, the Cubist Board held a meeting with senior management and advisors. Bonney summarized the process, including outreach to and responses from potentially interested parties. He described recent negotiations with Merck and efforts to increase the proposed price. The Board discussed the process and value perspectives from Party B and Party C, including their desire to acquire Cubist but unwillingness to offer equal to or greater than Merck's price. Ropes & Gray outlined the Board's fiduciary duties in considering a sale and outlined alternative courses of action. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley described and the Board discussed their respective financial analyses. The Board asked questions regarding differences between analyses. Each financial advisor described investment banking relationships and interests previously discussed with the Board. Members discussed with Morgan Stanley the expected economics of unwinding convertible note derivatives and noted the two Morgan Stanley Board members who are also Merck Board members. Goldman Sachs estimated the total net payment if the merger closed on February 1, 2015, subject to market changes. Goldman Sachs noted the estimate depended on volatility assumptions. Morgan Stanley noted the exact payment also depended on volatility factors but believed Goldman Sachs' analysis was reasonable and anticipated volatility inputs within the range Goldman Sachs indicated. The financial advisors left. The Special Committee discussed potential conflicts and determined Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley could present unbiased advice. Ropes & Gray summarized key merger agreement terms and discussed open items and completion timing. The Board discussed alternative courses of action (remaining independent) and potential risks and benefits of each. The Board also discussed adopting an exclusive forum bylaw and implications for executive and employee compensation. At the meeting's conclusion, the Board instructed senior management and legal advisors to inform Merck that the Board was willing to pursue Merck's $102.00 per-share acquisition subject to continued merger agreement negotiation, with the Board positioned to approve on December 7.

On December 7, 2014, the Cubist Board held a telephonic meeting attended by all Board members, senior management, and advisors. Bonney updated the Board on interactions with Merck following the December 4 meeting. Ropes & Gray summarized proposed merger agreement terms, focusing on changes from the December 4 draft. The Board noted the process had not yielded an alternative proposal near Merck's price and that $102.00 per share represented greater value than the Board and management believed achievable over a reasonable period by continuing independent execution. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley updated their analyses reflecting intervening changes. At the conclusion of their presentations, Goldman Sachs delivered an oral fairness opinion (confirmed in writing on December 8) that the $102.00 per share in cash was fair from a financial point of view to holders (other than Merck and affiliates) of Cubist common stock pursuant to the Merger Agreement. Morgan Stanley updated its analysis and rendered an oral opinion (subsequently confirmed in writing) that the $102.00 per share in cash was fair from a financial point of view to Cubist shareholders pursuant to the Merger Agreement. Thereafter, the Board unanimously (i) determined the Merger Agreement, Offer, Merger, and associated transactions' terms were fair to the company and stockholders and in their best interests, (ii) approved, declared advisable, and adopted the Merger Agreement, (iii) determined to recommend that Cubist stockholders tender shares into the Offer, and (iv) approved an amendment to bylaws implementing the forum selection bylaw provision.

On December 8, 2014, Cubist and Merck executed the Merger Agreement. Before NASDAQ opening, the parties issued a joint press release announcing the proposed merger. On the same day, the United States District Court for the District of Delaware issued a ruling in the Hospira litigation, which included a decision adverse to Cubist regarding certain of its patents.

From December 8 through December 19, 2014, Merck and Cubist conducted additional diligence, finalized documentation, and obtained necessary approvals.

On December 19, 2014, the tender offer commenced, with the Offer to Purchase and Letter of Transmittal dated December 19, 2014.

On January 20, 2015, the tender offer expired at 11:59 PM Eastern time. Computershare Trust Company, the depositary, advised that 58,039,667 shares had been validly tendered and not withdrawn, representing approximately 75.7% of outstanding shares on a fully diluted basis.

On January 21, 2015, Merck's subsidiary (Purchaser) accepted all validly tendered shares and made payment. Following acceptance and payment, Purchaser and Cubist completed the second-step merger under Delaware General Corporation Law Section 251(h) without a stockholder vote. At the Effective Time, the company became a wholly-owned subsidiary of Merck. The company notified NASDAQ of the merger's consummation and requested delisting; trading was suspended as of the close of trading on January 21, 2015. The company intended to file a Form 15 to suspend reporting obligations.

## Rationale

The Cubist Board determined that the $102.00 per share all-cash offer from Merck represented fair value and was in the best interests of the company and its shareholders for several reasons stated in the SC 14D9 filing:

**Business risks and execution challenges**: The Board considered that Cubist shareholders would remain subject to substantial risks and uncertainties if the company remained independent, including litigation risk related to Cubist's Cubicin (daptomycin) patent portfolio and threatened generic competition, regulatory risk related to the approval and commercial launch of the company's lead pipeline asset ceftolozane/tazobactam (Zerbaxa), and execution risk in clinical trials and product development. The Board also considered the company's pending leadership transition, with CEO Michael Bonney retiring on December 31, 2014, and President Robert Perez transitioning to CEO on January 1, 2015.

**Process and market check results**: The Board conducted a comprehensive market check process that identified and contacted three large-cap pharmaceutical companies with interest in the acute care segment and financial capacity to compete with Merck's offer. Party B (identified as having significant interest) ultimately withdrew, stating it could not achieve synergies and could not match or exceed the $100.00 per share floor. Party C indicated a preliminary range of $95-$110 per share but ultimately could not offer above the low-90s per share and was not seriously competitive. Party D declined to bid, stating Cubist was not a strategic fit. As a result, the process yielded no superior proposals.

**Valuation analysis**: Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley each rendered fairness opinions that the $102.00 per share consideration was fair from a financial point of view. Goldman Sachs' sum-of-the-parts analysis using product-level free cash flow projections produced a valuation range of $75.64 to $81.43 per share, while including comparable company multiples and precedent transaction analysis resulted in other valuation ranges. The Board noted the offer represented a 37.2% premium to the closing price on December 5, 2014 ($74.36), a 39.1% premium to the 30-day volume-weighted average price ($73.35), and a 24.2% premium to the 52-week high ($82.12).

**Deal certainty and favorable terms**: The Board emphasized that the offer was all-cash with no financing condition, reducing uncertainty for shareholders. Critically, the Board obtained Merck's agreement to exclude from the definition of "material adverse effect" any regulatory developments regarding ceftolozane/tazobactam approval and any outcome in the Hospira patent litigation with Hospira. This addressed the company's stated concerns about regulatory and litigation conditionality. The Board also negotiated a 3.5% termination fee, which it determined was not likely to be a significant deterrent to a superior offer. The Section 251(h) short-form merger structure permitted completion without a stockholder vote once Merck obtained more than 50% of outstanding shares in the tender offer, providing faster certainty to shareholders while preserving appraisal rights for dissenting shareholders.

**Strategic value of combination**: Frazier stated in negotiations that Merck perceived significant strategic value in combining Cubist's commercial infrastructure, Cubicin franchise, and pipeline assets (particularly ceftolozane/tazobactam) with Merck's broader portfolio and capabilities. Merck's willingness to pay $102.00 per share in all cash, above Cubist's internal valuation assumptions, demonstrated the buyer's assessment of strategic value. The Board believed the offer represented the most attractive alternative available to shareholders given the company's operational risks, leadership transition, and market dynamics.

The Board also considered risks and uncertainties of remaining independent, including the possibility of business and stock price decline if the transaction failed to close and the opportunity cost of foregone independent participation in future earnings and growth.

## Decision Notes

**Acceptance and completion**: This packet is **accepted** and marked status **completed**. Cubist Pharmaceuticals was acquired by Merck & Co. through an all-cash tender offer followed by a short-form merger under Delaware Section 251(h), with no stockholder vote required following successful tender of 75.7% of outstanding shares. The transaction closed on January 21, 2015, as evidenced by the Form 8-K filed by Cubist on January 21, 2015 (accession 0001104659-15-003691), Item 2.01 (Completion of Acquisition).

**Board recommendation and process**: The Cubist Board unanimously approved the Merger Agreement and recommended the tender offer on December 7, 2014, after a comprehensive market check process that identified and contacted three potential bidders. Two bidders (Parties B and C) determined they could not match the $102.00 per share offer or justify the acquisition on strategic and financial grounds. One bidder (Party D) declined. The Board obtained fairness opinions from both Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley.

**Offer terms and valuation**: The per-share consideration was $102.00 all cash with no CVRs, no financing conditions, and no closing conditions tied to the Hospira patent litigation or ceftolozane/tazobactam regulatory approval. Based on 76,421,535 shares outstanding as of December 4, 2014 (per SC 14D9 Item 1(b)), the implied equity value was approximately $7.79 billion. The offer represented a 37.2% premium to the December 5, 2014 closing price of $74.36 per share.

**Pipeline**: Cubist's portfolio comprised approved/commercial products (Cubicin/daptomycin, Zerbaxa pending approval, Sivextro, Dificid, Entereg) and late-stage pipeline assets (Surotomycin in Phase 3, Bevenopran out-licensing assumed post-2017). Ceftolozane/tazobactam (Zerbaxa) was the strategic focus of negotiations, with Merck specifically agreeing to exclude regulatory approval risk from closing conditions. The Hospira patent litigation regarding daptomycin was also a key negotiation point; Merck agreed to exclude litigation outcomes from the MAE definition and closing conditions.

**Shares outstanding and market cap**: Shares outstanding were 76,421,535 as of December 4, 2014 (SEC Company Facts). Historical market cap data from the EODHD market-cap API was unavailable (403 error); market cap for the weekly price table was calculated as closing price × shares outstanding. The earliest available shares observation in the work packet (shares.json file) is from July 23, 2010 (58,986,463 shares). For weeks prior to August 4, 2014 (when the latest pre-announcement shares observation is available), earlier dated shares observations from the json file were used. This limitation means market cap calculations for the 2012-2013 period reflect shares outstanding as of those historical observation dates, not the precise share count on each weekly date. The stated deal shares (76,421,535) increased from approximately 59 million shares in 2010-2012 due to equity issuances and option exercises over the years prior to acquisition.

**Announcement and close timeline**: Announced December 8, 2014; tender offer commenced December 19, 2014; tender offer expired January 20, 2015 at 11:59 PM ET; merger completed January 21, 2015 (same-day effective time following Purchaser's acceptance and payment for validly tendered shares). No shareholder vote occurred due to Section 251(h) DGCL provision permitting short-form merger upon acquisition of shares exceeding 50% of outstanding shares (tendered shares represented 75.7% on fully diluted basis).

**Major price inflection**: Stock price jumped 29.5% from $74.36 (December 5 close) to $96.31 (announcement week closing on December 12). Price stabilized at approximately $100-$101 per share during the tender offer period (December 19, 2014—January 20, 2015), slightly below the $102.00 offer price, consistent with typical tender offer dynamics.

**Regulatory and litigation risks**: On December 8, 2014 (same day as Merger Agreement execution and announcement), the United States District Court for the District of Delaware issued an adverse ruling in the Hospira patent litigation regarding daptomycin (Cubicin). This did not affect deal completion; the Merger Agreement specifically excluded litigation outcomes from closing conditions and the MAE definition, as negotiated by Cubist.

**Financial advisor conflicts**: Morgan Stanley served as a counterparty to 35% of Cubist's convertible note hedging arrangements, which would be unwound in the transaction. Goldman Sachs estimated the total net payment for unwinding, with Morgan Stanley as a counterparty. Additionally, two members of Morgan Stanley's Board deemed independent by Morgan Stanley's Board were also members of the Merck Board. The Cubist Board reviewed these conflicts and determined that both Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley could provide unbiased advice despite these relationships and financial incentives.

**Status note**: This acquisition was completed, not pending, terminated, asset-only, or unclear. The SEC filings (SC 14D9 and Form 8-K) clearly evidence completion of both the tender offer and the merger, with Cubist becoming a wholly-owned subsidiary of Merck as of January 21, 2015, Effective Time.
