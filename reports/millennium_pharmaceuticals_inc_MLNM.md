## Summary

Millennium Pharmaceuticals, Inc. entered into a definitive merger agreement with Takeda Pharmaceutical Company Limited on April 10, 2008, under which Takeda's subsidiary Mahogany Acquisition Corp. launched a tender offer for all outstanding shares of Millennium at $25.00 per share in cash. The transaction was structured as a two-step acquisition, with the tender offer followed by a short-form merger. The deal closed on May 14, 2008, after Takeda acquired approximately 90.3% of Millennium's shares, making Millennium a wholly-owned subsidiary of Takeda.

## Interaction History

Negotiations began as a collaboration dialogue in July 2007. By January 2008, Takeda expressed interest in a full acquisition. Takeda's initial offer of $23.00 on January 31 was followed by a period of limited due diligence. On March 18, Takeda increased its offer to $24.00. Millennium's CEO, Deborah Dunsire, acting on Board instructions, informed Takeda that $25.00 was required for acceptance. Takeda agreed to the $25.00 price on March 20, provided Millennium entered into an exclusivity agreement and agreed to a break-up fee. Millennium successfully negotiated the break-up fee down from 3.5% to 2.8% of equity value. Final negotiations occurred between April 1 and April 9, 2008, focusing on the merger agreement terms and employee retention arrangements requested by Takeda. Goldman Sachs provided a fairness opinion on April 9, and the board approved the deal that evening.

## Rationale

The Millennium Board determined the merger was more favorable than remaining independent, citing the 52.9% premium over the closing price, the certainty of cash value, and Takeda's ability to fund the deal. Takeda sought to establish a global oncology platform with Millennium as a stand-alone business unit foundation.

## Evidence

- On May 14, 2008, TPC and Millennium announced the completion of the Offer... Merger Sub was merged with and into Millennium pursuant to the terms of the Merger Agreement (8-K, 0001104659-08-033222).

## Decision Notes

The deal value is computed based on 326,704,024 shares outstanding as of April 7, 2008, multiplied by the $25.00 offer price. The transaction was completed via a short-form merger following a successful tender offer that secured 90.3% of shares.

## Buyout Rumors

Source: OpenRouter web search (Gemini 3 Flash Preview). Pre-deal rumors only.

The acquisition of Millennium Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (MLNM) by Takeda Pharmaceutical Company was announced on **April 10, 2008**. While the deal itself was described by analysts as a "surprise" in terms of the specific buyer and timing, Millennium had been a perennial subject of takeover speculation for several years due to the success of its blood-cancer drug, Velcade.

The following is a chronological timeline of reported rumors and speculation appearing before the confirmed deal:

*   **October 2004**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *BioCentury* / *SG Cowen* (Analyst Commentary)
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** General "Big Pharma" or "Large-cap Biotech"
    *   **Rumored Price:** Not specified
    *   **Details:** Following the submission of an expanded label for Velcade, analysts noted that Millennium was a prime candidate for "Big Pharma" looking for liquidity and stability. The company's partnership with Johnson & Johnson on Velcade led to persistent early speculation that J&J was the most logical suitor.

*   **Late 2006 – Early 2007**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** Various Wall Street analysts (e.g., *Leerink Swann*, *Prudential Equity Group*)
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** **Johnson & Johnson (J&J)**, **Sanofi-Aventis**, or **Novartis**
    *   **Rumored Price:** Speculated premiums of 30–50% over the then-trading price (approx. $12–$15 range)
    *   **Details:** Speculation intensified as Velcade sales grew. Analysts frequently cited Millennium as a "bolt-on" acquisition target for companies needing to bolster their oncology pipelines. J&J was the most frequently named buyer because it already co-promoted Velcade outside the U.S.

*   **December 2007 – January 2008**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *Reuters*, *Forbes*, and *Bloomberg* (Market "Chatter")
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** **Japanese Pharmaceutical Companies** (specifically **Eisai** or **Astellas**)
    *   **Rumored Price:** Not specified
    *   **Details:** Following Eisai’s $3.9 billion acquisition of MGI Pharma in December 2007, market speculation shifted toward other Japanese firms looking to expand in the U.S. oncology market. Millennium was repeatedly identified by "industry watchers" as the next logical target in this trend, though Takeda was rarely named specifically in these rumors until the deal was finalized.

*   **March 2008**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *The Boston Globe* / *MarketWatch*
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** **Unspecified "Strategic Buyer"**
    *   **Rumored Price:** Not specified
    *   **Details:** In the weeks leading up to the deal, Millennium's stock price saw increased volatility and "takeover chatter" in trading circles. Analysts noted that the company's transition to profitability (its first since 1998) made it an increasingly attractive and "de-risked" target for a major pharmaceutical company.

*   **April 9, 2008 (The day before the announcement)**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *Bloomberg* / *Tokyo Stock Exchange* (Indirect)
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** **Takeda Pharmaceutical Co.**
    *   **Rumored Price:** Not specified
    *   **Details:** Just prior to the official announcement, Takeda's shares fell 2.5% in Tokyo trading amid rumors that the company was preparing a massive, multi-billion dollar overseas acquisition to offset the upcoming patent expiration of its diabetes drug, Actos. While Millennium was not always named in every report that morning, the "war chest" Takeda had built (approx. $10 billion) was widely linked to a potential U.S. biotech deal.
