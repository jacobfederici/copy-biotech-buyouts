## Summary

Sanofi, through its indirect wholly owned subsidiary Thunder Acquisition Corp., acquired Synthorx, Inc. for $68.00 per share in cash, totaling approximately $2.48 billion. The acquisition was structured as a tender offer followed by a merger, with the Synthorx board unanimously recommending the offer to its stockholders.

- 2019-12-09: The execution of the Merger Agreement was publicly announced at 1:00 a.m. Eastern Time. A Centerview representative contacted Company A, acknowledged receipt of its December 8, 2019 proposal, and informed them of the announcement of the Merger Agreement.
- 2019-12-23: Purchaser commenced the tender offer to acquire all outstanding shares of Synthorx common stock for $68.00 per share in cash.
- 2020-01-23: The tender offer expired. Purchaser accepted for payment 27,443,994 Shares, representing approximately 83.7% of outstanding Shares. The merger of Purchaser with and into Synthorx was completed, with Synthorx continuing as an indirect wholly owned subsidiary of Sanofi. Synthorx notified NASDAQ of the consummation of the Merger and requested delisting of its shares.

## Interaction History

Synthorx's board and senior management pursued a strategy of seeking partnering opportunities to fund clinical development and enhance stockholder value, regularly engaging with potential partners and reporting to the board. Initial discussions with Sanofi and other pharmaceutical companies, including Company A, Company B, and Company C, began in October 2018 and continued through 2019, often at industry conferences and through direct meetings. Confidentiality agreements were established with Sanofi (May 2019, amended October 2019), Company A (June 2019), and Company C (September 2019). Sanofi submitted a non-binding partnering term sheet in September 2019, which Synthorx deemed inadequate. Company B also presented partnering terms in September 2019, which Synthorx rejected as inadequate in October 2019, leading to Company B terminating discussions on October 17, 2019. Sanofi submitted an unsolicited, non-binding offer to acquire Synthorx for $36.00 per share on November 25, 2019, requesting a 21-day exclusivity period and indicating it was evaluating alternative transactions. The Synthorx Board, advised by Cooley LLP and Centerview Partners LLC, deemed the offer inadequate and authorized Centerview to communicate this to Sanofi, and to contact Company A, Company B, and Company C to solicit higher offers. Company B declined to pursue a transaction. Company C entered into a confidentiality agreement and received a management presentation but later indicated it would not pursue a transaction. Company A expressed interest and, after receiving a management presentation and dataroom access, verbally communicated an expected offer of $44.00 per share or an alternative of $41.50 plus a CVR of up to $6.00 per share. Sanofi then submitted a revised non-binding offer of $41.00 per share on December 2, 2019, with a tight deadline. The Board decided to solicit best and final non-binding offers for exclusivity from Sanofi and Company A. On December 3, 2019, Company A offered $47.75 per share, and Sanofi offered $50.50 per share. The Board approved moving forward with Sanofi and entered into an exclusivity agreement until December 10, 2019. During this exclusivity period, Company A submitted an unsolicited offer of $62.00 per share on December 5, 2019. The Board, after consulting with Cooley, notified Sanofi of this offer as required by the exclusivity agreement. On December 6, 2019, Sanofi submitted a revised offer of $68.00 per share, with a deadline for execution that day. Centerview rendered an oral fairness opinion to the Board, which then unanimously approved the Merger Agreement. The Merger Agreement was executed on December 7, 2019. On December 8, 2019, Company A submitted another unsolicited offer of $70.00 per share. The Board, after consulting with Cooley and Centerview, determined this could reasonably lead to a Superior Offer and notified Sanofi, but did not engage with Company A given the impending public announcement of the Sanofi deal. The transaction was publicly announced on December 9, 2019.

## Rationale

The Synthorx Board's rationale for recommending the offer included the immediate value and liquidity provided by the all-cash offer, the significant premium over historical market prices, and the belief that a competitive process had secured the best available value for stockholders. The Board also considered the risks and uncertainties of Synthorx operating as a standalone company, including high development costs, clinical trial risks, and the need for non-dilutive financing. The financial analyses by Centerview Partners and the fairness opinion supported the decision. The tender offer structure was seen as beneficial for its prompt completion and reduction of uncertainty. The Board also considered the terms of the Merger Agreement, including Sanofi's ability to fund the offer, the high likelihood of consummation, and the ability to consider superior offers under certain conditions.
