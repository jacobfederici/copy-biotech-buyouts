## Summary

Bayer AG acquired Schering AG through a voluntary public tender offer followed by a domination and profit and loss transfer agreement. The transaction was initiated as a friendly alternative to an unsolicited EUR 77.00 per share bid from Merck KGaA. Bayer initially offered EUR 86.00 per share, which was increased to EUR 89.00 after Bayer purchased a 21.8% stake from Merck KGaA. Following the completion of the tender offer, which resulted in 92.4% ownership, the parties entered into a Domination Agreement. Under German law, this triggered a Mandatory Offer for remaining shares at EUR 89.36 (the three-month average stock price). The deal consolidated the companies' pharmaceutical operations into a new entity, Bayer Schering Pharma AG.

## Interaction History

Negotiations began on March 13, 2006, following an unsolicited bid from Merck KGaA. Schering's CEO Dr. Hubertus Erlen and Bayer's CEO Werner Wenning led the discussions. Initial price friction occurred on March 21, when Schering rejected EUR 84.00. Bayer countered with a choice between EUR 87.00 (with a 1% break-up fee) or EUR 86.00 (without a fee). Schering's board chose the lower price to avoid the break-up fee. The deal turned competitive when Merck KGaA began buying shares in the market, eventually forcing Bayer to buy out Merck's 21.8% stake at EUR 89.00 per share on June 14, 2006. This purchase legally required Bayer to raise its tender offer price to EUR 89.00. Post-tender, the integration was formalized via a Domination Agreement, which required a valuation by KPMG and a court-appointed auditor (Warth & Klein). The final mandatory compensation was set at EUR 89.36 to match the three-month average stock price as determined by BaFin.

## Rationale

The Schering board supported the Bayer offer as a 'friendly transaction' that provided a superior valuation compared to the Merck KGaA bid. Bayer sought the acquisition to combine pharmaceutical activities, realize growth synergies, and establish a global champion in specialized therapeutic fields. The Domination Agreement was utilized to allow Bayer to give instructions to Schering management and integrate cash pooling and operational functions.

## Buyout Rumors

Source: OpenRouter web search (Gemini 3 Flash Preview). Pre-deal rumors only.

The following is a chronological timeline of acquisition rumors and takeover speculation regarding Schering AG that appeared before the confirmed deal announcements in March 2006.

*   **Early 2000s (Recurring)**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** Various (referenced in *PharmaTimes*)
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Bayer AG
    *   **Rumored Price:** Not specified
    *   **Details:** Industry analysts and media outlets repeatedly suggested that Bayer and Schering AG should join forces to gain the critical mass necessary to compete with global pharmaceutical giants.

*   **March 10, 2006**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *Financial Times*, *Reuters*, and various German media
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Merck KGaA
    *   **Rumored Price:** Approximately €77 per share
    *   **Details:** Reports emerged that Merck KGaA was preparing a hostile takeover bid for Schering AG. The speculation was fueled by unusual trading activity and Schering's vulnerability as a mid-sized independent player in a consolidating market.

*   **March 12, 2006**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *The Wall Street Journal*, *Bloomberg*
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Merck KGaA
    *   **Rumored Price:** €77 per share (approx. €14.9 billion total)
    *   **Details:** Speculation intensified that a formal offer from Merck KGaA was imminent. Reports indicated that Schering’s management was already preparing a defense strategy against a "low-ball" offer. (Note: Merck KGaA officially announced its hostile bid later this day).

*   **March 13–22, 2006**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *Handelsblatt*, *Berliner Zeitung*, *The New York Times*
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Novartis AG, Sanofi-Aventis, or Pfizer
    *   **Rumored Price:** Speculated to be above €80 per share
    *   **Details:** Following Merck KGaA's official hostile bid, intense speculation centered on a "White Knight" emerging to rescue Schering. Analysts specifically highlighted Novartis as the most likely suitor due to its cash reserves and strategic fit.

*   **March 22, 2006**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *Manager Magazin*, *Reuters*
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Bayer AG
    *   **Rumored Price:** Speculated to be in the mid-€80s per share
    *   **Details:** Rumors shifted away from Swiss firms toward a domestic "German solution." Reports surfaced that Bayer AG was in secret, advanced negotiations with Schering’s board to launch a friendly counter-bid to block Merck KGaA.

*   **March 23, 2006 (Pre-Announcement)**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *CNBC*, *AFX News*
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Bayer AG
    *   **Rumored Price:** €86 per share
    *   **Details:** Immediate speculation on the morning of March 23 suggested that Bayer had finalized a deal with Schering's management and would announce a formal offer of €86 per share before the market close. (Note: Bayer officially confirmed the €16.3 billion offer later that day).
