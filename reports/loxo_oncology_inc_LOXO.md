## Summary

Loxo Oncology entered into a definitive agreement to be acquired by Eli Lilly and Company for $235.00 per share in cash, representing a total equity value of approximately $8.0 billion. The transaction was structured as a tender offer followed by a second-step merger under Section 251(h) of the DGCL, aimed at expanding Lilly's oncology portfolio with Loxo's pipeline of highly selective medicines for patients with genomically defined cancers.

## Timeline

- 2018-04-16: Dr. Joshua Bilenker (Loxo CEO) meets with Dr. Levi Garraway (Lilly) at AACR meeting for updates.
- 2018-06-13: Loxo Board reviews long-term financial forecasts and strategic landscape with Goldman Sachs.

- 2018-12-20: Lilly executives meet Loxo management in Stamford and submit a written proposal to acquire Loxo for $230.00 per share in cash.

- 2018-12-30: Lilly submits an increased 'best and final' offer of $235.00 per share in cash.
- 2018-12-30: Loxo Board determines to proceed with Lilly at $235.00 without a broad market check to avoid leak risks and secure the premium.
- 2019-01-05: Loxo Board unanimously approves the Merger Agreement; Goldman Sachs renders fairness opinion.
- 2019-01-07: Joint press release announcing the transaction.
- 2019-01-17: Purchaser commences the tender offer.
- 2019-02-14: Tender offer expires with 84.6% of shares tendered.
- 2019-02-15: Merger completed; Loxo becomes a wholly-owned subsidiary of Lilly.

## Interaction History

Negotiations began in earnest on December 20, 2018, when Lilly's Darren Carroll and Dr. Levi Garraway proposed $230.00 per share. Loxo's Board, advised by Goldman Sachs and Fenwick & West, countered that the price was inadequate but opened a data room for diligence. On December 30, 2018, Lilly's CEO David Ricks informed Dr. Bilenker that Lilly was increasing its offer to $235.00, which was characterized as their 'best and final' offer. The Loxo Board deliberated on whether to contact other parties but noted that previous licensing discussions for LOXO-292 had not yielded superior value and feared a leak would jeopardize the Lilly deal. Between January 2 and January 5, 2019, the parties negotiated the merger agreement terms, specifically the termination fee, which was eventually settled at $265 million (3.3% of equity value). The deal was signed on January 5, 2019.

## Rationale

The Loxo Board determined the deal was superior to remaining independent due to the significant cash premium (68% over the last trading day) and the risks associated with standalone drug development, regulatory approval, and commercialization. Lilly sought the acquisition to broaden its oncology portfolio with a pipeline of precision medicines and global commercial capabilities.

## Evidence

- On January 5, 2019, the Board, among other things, unanimously (1) determined that the Offer, the Merger and the other Transactions are advisable, fair to and in the best interests of Loxo Oncology and its stockholders (0001193125-19-011650).
- Purchaser has accepted for payment all Shares that were validly tendered and not properly withdrawn pursuant to the Offer... Lilly completed its acquisition of Loxo Oncology by consummating the merger (0001193125-19-042505).

## Decision Notes

The deal value of $8.0 billion is the stated aggregate cash consideration in the 8-K. Shares outstanding (30,787,728) were sourced from the 14D-9 as of Jan 16, 2019. The transaction included the acceleration of all unvested options.

## Buyout Rumors

Source: OpenRouter web search (Gemini 3 Flash Preview). Pre-deal rumors only.

Based on a review of financial news and market reporting prior to the official announcement on January 7, 2019, there were **no specific pre-deal rumors** or leaked reports of an acquisition of Loxo Oncology, Inc. (LOXO) by Eli Lilly or any other buyer.

The deal was widely characterized by financial analysts and media outlets as a "surprise" announcement that occurred on the first day of the J.P. Morgan Healthcare Conference. Subsequent regulatory filings (Schedule 14D-9) revealed that the negotiations were conducted with extreme speed and secrecy over a period of approximately 18 days during the December 2018 holiday season to ensure the deal could be unveiled at the conference.

While there were no specific rumors of a Loxo acquisition, the following context regarding the company's M&A environment existed prior to the deal:

*   **General Sector Speculation:** Following Bristol-Myers Squibb’s $74 billion acquisition of Celgene on January 3, 2019 (just four days before the Loxo announcement), market speculation intensified regarding which oncology-focused biotech companies might be next. Loxo was often cited by analysts as a *potential* candidate for future M&A due to its recent FDA approval for Vitrakvi, but no specific buyer or price was rumored before the Lilly announcement.
*   **Licensing Discussions (Not Buyout):** According to SEC filings released after the deal, Loxo had been in active discussions with more than 15 potential partners starting in April 2018. However, these discussions were specifically for **licensing** its RET inhibitor (LOXO-292), not for a company-wide acquisition. These talks remained confidential and did not result in public buyout rumors.
*   **Bayer Partnership:** In November 2017, Loxo entered a major collaboration with Bayer for its TRK program. At the time, some analysts speculated this could eventually lead to a closer relationship, but no specific takeover rumors materialized from this partnership prior to the 2019 Lilly deal.
