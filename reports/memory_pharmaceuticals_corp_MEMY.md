## Summary

Memory Pharmaceuticals Corp. entered into a definitive merger agreement with Hoffmann-La Roche Inc. (Roche) on November 25, 2008, following a period of severe financial distress and potential delisting from NASDAQ. Roche, an existing collaboration partner, acquired all outstanding shares of Memory for $0.61 per share in cash through a tender offer. The transaction provided immediate liquidity to stockholders and secured the future development of Memory's nicotinic alpha-7 agonist program, which was already licensed to Roche. The deal closed on January 5, 2009, after approximately 89% of shares were tendered and a top-up option was exercised to reach the 90% threshold for a short-form merger.

## Interaction History

Memory Pharmaceuticals had a long-standing relationship with Roche dating back to 2002 and 2003 collaborations. In mid-2008, facing NASDAQ delisting and a cash crunch, Memory explored a PIPE financing with MPM Asset Management. During these efforts, Roche expressed interest in a broader relationship. On October 21, 2008, Roche proposed an acquisition at a $40-$50 million valuation. Memory's Board, advised by Lazard and Covington & Burling, pursued Roche while keeping the MPM financing as a backup. Negotiations involved Memory's CEO Vaughn Kailian and Roche executives. Memory attempted to include a Contingent Value Right (CVR) to increase the price, but Roche refused. Roche initially offered $0.55 per share on November 12. Memory countered with a $70 million valuation, which Roche rejected. On November 17, the Board determined the MPM financing was too dilutive and provided insufficient runway, leading them to authorize a $0.61 per share counteroffer ($50 million valuation). Roche accepted this on November 21. Regulatory clearance was obtained via a joint CFIUS filing on November 25, 2008.

## Rationale

The Board recommended the deal due to Memory's precarious financial condition, including a lack of funding beyond six months and imminent NASDAQ delisting. The Roche offer provided a significant premium (319% over the closing price) and immediate liquidity in a volatile market. The Board also noted that Roche's existing rights to Memory's lead program acted as a deterrent to other potential buyers, making Roche the most logical acquirer.

## Evidence

- On November 24, 2008, the Board approved resolutions: (1) determining that the merger agreement and the transactions contemplated thereby are fair to and in the best interests of Memory’s stockholders... (3) resolving to recommend acceptance of the Offer (SC 14D9).
- On January 5, 2009, Memory Pharmaceuticals Corp... completed its merger (the 'Merger') with 900 North Point Acquisition Corporation... a wholly-owned subsidiary of Hoffmann-La Roche Inc. (8-K, 2009-01-06).
