## Summary

Cellular Dynamics International (CDI) entered into a definitive merger agreement with FUJIFILM Holdings Corporation on March 30, 2015, under which FUJIFILM acquired CDI via a cash tender offer for $16.50 per share. The transaction valued CDI at approximately $260.9 million based on outstanding shares. The deal followed CDI's evaluation of capital-raising alternatives due to its lack of profitability and upcoming debt obligations. The merger was completed on May 1, 2015, through a short-form merger following the successful tender of approximately 90.3% of CDI's shares.

## Interaction History

Negotiations began in mid-2014 as CDI sought capital to refinance debt and fund R&D. Robert Palay (CEO) met with FUJIFILM and other parties (Parties A-H). FUJIFILM initially proposed a joint venture and minority investment in October 2014. By November, FUJIFILM shifted toward a majority or total acquisition. CDI's board, advised by J.P. Morgan and Sidley Austin, pushed for a higher valuation, rejecting initial ranges of $13.00-$14.50. In January 2015, FUJIFILM raised the offer to $15.50. CDI management explored other bidders, but Party F did not respond, and Party H's interest was at a much lower premium ($6.81-$7.34). In March 2015, CDI requested $17.50 and a bridge loan to pay off its existing credit facility with Sixth Floor Investors. FUJIFILM ultimately agreed to $16.50 per share and a $12.1 million bridge loan, provided CDI dropped 'go-shop' demands. The board approved the final terms on March 29, 2015.

## Rationale

The CDI Board recommended the deal because the $16.50 price represented a 108% premium over the prior closing price and provided immediate liquidity. CDI faced significant financial pressure, including a lack of profitability, high R&D burn, and the need to refinance debt that Sixth Floor Investors refused to extend. The board determined that a standalone strategy carried high execution risk and potential for significant shareholder dilution through necessary future equity raises.
