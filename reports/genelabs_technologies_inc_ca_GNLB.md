## Summary

Genelabs Technologies, Inc. entered into a definitive merger agreement with GlaxoSmithKline plc (GSK) and its subsidiary SmithKline Beecham Corporation, under which GSK acquired Genelabs for $1.30 per share in cash. The transaction was structured as a tender offer followed by a short-form merger, aimed at integrating Genelabs' infectious disease and hepatitis C virus (HCV) technology into GSK's pharmaceutical portfolio.

## Timeline

- 2008-02-15: Genelabs enters into a confidentiality agreement with Company A regarding a potential business combination.
- 2008-03-26: Company A submits a non-binding offer to acquire Genelabs at $1.03 per share in stock.
- 2008-04-02: Company A increases its offer to $1.34 per share in stock.
- 2008-05-01: Genelabs engages Cowen and Company, LLC as its financial advisor.
- 2008-06-23: Company A informs Genelabs it is no longer interested in an acquisition.
- 2008-09-19: Dr. Moncef Slaoui of GSK contacts Dr. Irene Chow to express interest in an outright acquisition rather than a license.
- 2008-09-23: GSK submits a non-binding proposal to acquire Genelabs for $1.25 per share in cash.
- 2008-10-10: GSK increases its proposal to $1.30 per share; Genelabs enters into an exclusivity agreement with GSK through October 31.
- 2008-10-29: Genelabs Board unanimously approves the Merger Agreement at $1.30 per share.
- 2008-11-12: Purchaser formally commences the tender offer.
- 2009-01-07: Tender offer expires; Purchaser exercises Top-Up Option and completes the merger.

## Interaction History

Negotiations began in early 2008 with Company A, which eventually withdrew. GSK, which had a pre-existing license agreement with Genelabs dating back to 1992 for Hepatitis E vaccines, initially approached Genelabs in February 2008 for an HCV license. By September 2008, GSK shifted its focus to an acquisition. Dr. Moncef Slaoui (GSK) negotiated directly with Dr. Irene Chow (Genelabs). GSK's initial offer of $1.25 per share was raised to $1.30 on October 10, 2008, following a meeting between Slaoui and Chow. During the exclusivity period, Genelabs' financial advisor Cowen contacted 21 potential bidders, but no superior proposals emerged. Company D expressed interest in a stock-for-stock merger but lacked specific terms. The board met multiple times in October to evaluate the GSK offer against stand-alone prospects and other strategic alternatives, ultimately approving the $1.30 cash offer as the best reasonably obtainable value.

## Rationale

The Genelabs board recommended the deal due to the company's precarious financial position, including low cash reserves and the difficulty of raising capital in the 2008 economic climate. The board noted that the company's pipeline was at an early stage with no near-term clinical milestones to drive valuation. The $1.30 price represented a 465% premium over the closing price on the day prior to the announcement. GSK sought to acquire Genelabs to bolster its infectious disease portfolio and secure full control of the HCV technology platform.

## Evidence

- At a meeting of the Company’s Board of Directors held on October 29, 2008, the Company Board unanimously: (1) determined that the Offer and the Merger are fair to, and in the best interests of, the Company and its shareholders... and resolved to recommend that the Company’s shareholders tender their Shares (SC 14D9, Item 4).
- On January 7, 2009, Gemstone Acquisition Corporation... was merged with and into Genelabs Technologies, Inc. (8-K, 2009-01-09).

## Decision Notes

The deal value is computed based on the 43,879,917 shares outstanding as of November 5, 2008, multiplied by the $1.30 per share offer price. The transaction was completed via a short-form merger following the exercise of a Top-Up Option that brought the buyer's ownership above the 90% threshold required by California law.

## Buyout Rumors

Source: OpenRouter web search (Gemini 3 Flash Preview). Pre-deal rumors only.

The acquisition of Genelabs Technologies, Inc. (GNLB) by GlaxoSmithKline (GSK) was announced on October 30, 2008. Based on historical records and reporting from that period, there were no specific, documented buyout rumors or takeover speculation in the public domain prior to the official deal announcement.

Industry reports following the announcement characterized the deal as a "surprise" to both the company and the market. Specifically:

*   **October 30, 2008:** GlaxoSmithKline and Genelabs Technologies announced a definitive agreement for GSK to acquire Genelabs for **$1.30 per share** in cash (approximately $57 million).
*   **Reporting on the Lack of Rumors:** *Drug Discovery News* (December 9, 2008) reported that the acquisition offer was "as much a surprise to Genelabs as it was to financial analysts and the pharmaceutical community." The report noted that while the two companies had been in discussions regarding a potential research partnership for Hepatitis C (HCV), GSK "at the last moment" decided to forgo the collaboration and instead make an offer to acquire the company outright.
*   **Market Reaction:** On the day of the announcement, Genelabs' stock price rose from a previous close of **$0.23** to **$1.22**, a gain of over 430%, further indicating that the premium and the deal itself had not been priced in by speculative activity or rumors.

No pre-deal rumors exist.
