## Summary

InSite Vision entered into a definitive merger agreement with Ranbaxy Inc., a subsidiary of Sun Pharmaceutical Industries Ltd., following a competitive bidding process involving QLT Inc. Sun Pharma acquired InSite for $0.35 per share in cash via a tender offer, representing a significant premium over a previous stock-for-stock agreement with QLT. The transaction provided InSite with necessary liquidity to fund its operations and the BromSite NDA filing through a secured line of credit provided by the buyer.

## Interaction History

InSite Vision faced severe financial distress starting in 2012, leading to a formal strategic review in late 2013 advised by Guggenheim Securities. After contacting 36 companies, InSite initially signed with QLT Inc. in June 2015. However, Sun Pharma, led by Jerry St. Peter (Head of Ophthalmic Business), re-engaged in August 2015 citing a new strategic focus on U.S. branded ophthalmics. Sun initially offered $0.25 cash, which InSite's board (including Chairman Timothy McInerney and CEO Timothy Ruane) deemed a potential superior proposal. QLT matched this value with an amended stock-for-stock deal on August 26. On September 3, Sun increased its bid to $0.35 cash. QLT countered with a complex collar-based proposal worth approximately $0.355-$0.365 but demanded a significantly higher termination fee ($4.5M-$5M) and required a QLT shareholder vote, increasing closing risk. On September 11, the InSite board gave QLT a deadline to reduce the fee and provide a reciprocal fee if their shareholders rejected the deal. QLT declined. Consequently, InSite terminated the QLT deal, paid a $2.667M break fee (funded by Sun), and signed with Sun Pharma on September 15, 2015.

## Rationale

The InSite Board recommended the deal because the company lacked sufficient cash to operate beyond July 2015 and had failed to secure alternative financing. The $0.35 cash price provided immediate liquidity and a 150% premium over the pre-announcement price. Sun Pharma also provided a $11.9M secured line of credit to fund operations and the QLT termination fee, which was critical for InSite's survival as a going concern.
