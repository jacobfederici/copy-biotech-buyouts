## Summary

Merck Sharp & Dohme Corp. acquired ArQule, Inc., a Burlington, Massachusetts-based pharmaceutical company, for $20.00 per share in cash through a tender offer and subsequent merger. The acquisition was announced on December 9, 2019, following a nine-week exploration period that began when Merck expressed interest in a co-development and profit-sharing partnership for ArQule's clinical-stage program ARQ 531 (a Menin inhibitor in development for hematologic malignancies). After Merck indicated interest in broader strategic control, the parties expanded discussions to a full acquisition of ArQule, reached agreement on December 6, 2019, and completed the transaction on January 16, 2020. The tender offer achieved 75.3% subscription as of January 15, 2020, satisfying the minimum condition requirement. The merger was effected without stockholder vote under Delaware General Corporation Law Section 251(h), with ArQule continuing as a wholly-owned subsidiary of Merck.

## Interaction History

**June 2019 - Initial Partnership Discussions**: Following an introduction from an ArQule director, Merck approached ArQule regarding a potential co-development and profit-sharing partnership for ARQ 531, ArQule's lead clinical program—a Menin inhibitor in early development for relapsed or refractory B-cell lymphoid malignancies. This inquiry came within the context of ArQule's publicly stated openness to exploring such partnerships if available on attractive terms, which the company had confirmed on investor calls during 2018-2019.

**Summer 2019 - Confidentiality and Due Diligence**: ArQule and Merck executed a confidentiality agreement allowing Merck to conduct preliminary due diligence on clinical, safety, and regulatory data related to ARQ 531. Merck was one of nine parties that had expressed interest in a co-development and profit-sharing partnership for ARQ 531 during 2018-2019, and one of six parties (including Merck) to whom ArQule provided due diligence data room access.

Of the six parties with data access, one was actively negotiating a definitive co-development agreement with ArQule throughout 2018 before withdrawing at the JP Morgan Healthcare Conference in January 2019 due to its own pending transaction. Another party proposed co-development terms in early 2019 that the Board found unattractive given ARQ 531's perceived strategic and commercial potential; that party did not continue meaningful engagement after ArQule's counterproposal. At the time of Merck's November 29 proposal, no other party was actively pursuing a co-development and profit-sharing transaction with ArQule.

**November 19-20, 2019 - Shift to Acquisition Discussions**: On November 19, 2019, the ArQule Board discussed status of Merck discussions during a Board meeting. The following day (November 20), Merck representatives indicated to Peter Lawrence, ArQule President and COO, that Merck was interested in a broader scope transaction affording Merck greater control of ARQ 531 than a co-development partnership would provide. Lawrence indicated such a transaction would require Board discussion. Hours later, Merck representatives reiterated this interest directly to CEO Paolo Pucci and Lawrence, emphasizing the interest in fuller corporate control.

**November 25, 2019 - Expanded Due Diligence**: Following Board discussions of Merck's request to explore a broader transaction, ArQule and Merck amended their confidentiality agreement to expand information coverage beyond ARQ 531 to include ArQule's broader clinical pipeline and business data. The amended agreement included a standstill provision, preventing Merck from initiating unsolicited proposals or accumulating shares without ArQule consent.

**November 29, 2019 - Initial Offer**: Merck submitted a formal proposal to acquire ArQule at $18.00 per share in cash.

**November 30, 2019 - Board Response**: The ArQule Board met to evaluate Merck's November 29 proposal. Board members discussed with management and financial advisor Centerview Partners the desirability of pursuing an acquisition transaction, strategic considerations including the need for management to prepare financial projections, the possibility of obtaining an improved proposal from Merck, and the risks of contacting other potential counterparties. The Board authorized Centerview to communicate to Merck the need to substantially improve its offer and instructed management to prepare projections for subsequent Board review. Significantly, the Board did not grant Merck exclusivity at this time, though it did note the risk of leaks and the potential adverse impact on Merck's interest if other parties were contacted during the compressed timeline Merck had proposed for announcement (before December 9, 2019).

**December 1, 2019 - Negotiation of Final Terms**: Centerview delivered a counteroffer of $20.00 per share in cash. Merck initially proposed $19.00 per share. Centerview reiterated that the Board had authorized engagement in further due diligence with a goal of completing negotiations within one week and announcing by December 9 only if Merck increased its offer to $20.00 per share. Merck confirmed its willingness to increase its offer to $20.00 per share if the parties worked toward announcing by December 9, 2019.

**December 4-6, 2019 - Final Negotiations and Board Approval**: During December 4-6, ArQule's Compensation Committee and representatives of management, counsel (Skadden, Arps, Slate, Meagher & Flom LLP), and compensation consultants discussed compensation proposals designed to incentivize and align management and employee interests with stockholder interests in completing the transaction. Topics included treatment of 2019 annual bonuses, potential severance and retention programs, and related tax consequences. Merck and ArQule representatives negotiated merger-related compensation and benefits items based on input and authorization from the Compensation Committee.

**December 6, 2019 - Unanimous Board Determination and Deal Execution**: The ArQule Board held a meeting at which it unanimously:
- Determined that the Merger Agreement and the proposed transactions were fair to and in the best interest of ArQule and its stockholders;
- Declared it advisable for ArQule to enter into the Merger Agreement;
- Approved execution, delivery, and performance of the Merger Agreement and consummation of the transactions, including the tender offer and Merger;
- Resolved that the Merger would be effected under Section 251(h) of the DGCL (requiring no stockholder vote);
- Resolved to recommend that stockholders accept the Offer and tender their Shares pursuant to the Offer;
- Authorized the CEO, CFO, and COO to execute and deliver the Merger Agreement.

The Board's determination was supported by the following factors (not in order of relative importance): (1) the structure of the transaction (tender offer followed by Section 251(h) merger) enabled stockholders to receive the Offer Price in a relatively short timeframe; (2) likelihood of Offer completion based on support agreements from directors and officers representing approximately 8% of outstanding shares and the fact that Merck had no financing contingency; (3) likelihood that favorable financial results from ARQ 531 Phase 1 study at the ASH Conference (December 7-10) could occur after announcement, which under the Merger Agreement would not prevent closing; (4) availability of a "fiduciary out" provision allowing the Board to change recommendation and accept a Superior Proposal; (5) extensive prior outreach to other potential counterparties during 2018-2019 had not resulted in any other party actively pursuing a transaction with ArQule, and Merck's price and terms compared favorably to past proposals; (6) risks and uncertainties of ArQule as a standalone company, including the need for additional future financing for development and commercialization of product candidates and resulting dilution to stockholders; (7) the fact that Merck was no longer interested in pursuing a co-development and profit-sharing partnership and was only interested in a full acquisition; (8) ability to improve the financial terms relative to Merck's initial November 29 proposal; and (9) risks that a broader outreach to other counterparties could delay a transaction and disrupt the opportunity with Merck.

Same day (December 6), following the Board meeting, ArQule, Merck, and Argon Merger Sub executed the Merger Agreement. ArQule's directors and executive officers executed tender and support agreements with Merck and Merger Sub, under which they agreed to tender all their Shares in the Offer and vote against any competing Acquisition Proposal, other merger, or action that would prevent completion of the transaction. As of December 11, 2019, the Tendering Stockholders (including all directors and executive officers) beneficially owned approximately 12.6% of outstanding Shares including shares subject to stock options and warrants (approximately 8% when calculated without such options and warrants).

**December 9, 2019 - Public Announcement**: ArQule and Merck jointly announced execution of the Merger Agreement and the forthcoming tender offer. The announcement came before the opening of trading on Nasdaq, allowing the market to price in the transaction. ArQule filed a Form 8-K with the SEC disclosing the Merger Agreement. On the same day, ArQule released final results of its Phase 1, dose escalation study of ARQ 531 in patients with relapsed or refractory B-cell lymphoid malignancies at the ASH Conference. The timing—announcement in the morning before market open, followed by positive clinical data release later in the day—was coordinated to maximize impact.

**December 17, 2019 - Tender Offer Commencement**: Merck and Argon Merger Sub commenced the tender offer at $20.00 per Share in cash. ArQule filed the Schedule 14D-9 (this document), unanimously recommending that stockholders accept the Offer and tender their Shares. The filing incorporated the Merger Agreement, amendment, form of support agreement, and other transaction documents. ArQule's Board reiterated the fairness determination and the reasons supporting the transaction recommendation.

**December 17, 2019 - Technical Amendment**: ArQule, Merck, and Argon Merger Sub entered into the First Amendment to the Merger Agreement to correct scrivener's errors in Annex I regarding conditions to consummation of the tender offer. The amendment clarified which conditions could not be waived without ArQule's consent and limited the scope of antitrust and judgment/law conditions to jurisdictions where Merck or its affiliates operate or own assets.

**January 15, 2020 - Offer Expiration**: The tender offer expired at 11:59 p.m. New York time. American Stock Transfer & Trust Company, as depository, reported that 103,394,298 Shares had been validly tendered and not validly withdrawn—approximately 75.3% of the Minimum Condition denominator (consisting of all Shares outstanding plus all Shares that would be issued upon vesting, conversion, settlement, or exercise of all then-outstanding warrants, options, benefit plans, obligations, or securities convertible into Shares). The Minimum Condition was satisfied. All conditions of the Offer and the Merger Agreement had been satisfied or waived by Merck. Merger Sub accepted all validly tendered Shares for payment.

**January 16, 2020 - Merger Completion**: The Merger was consummated. Merck completed its acquisition of ArQule by effecting the Merger without a stockholder meeting under Section 251(h) of the DGCL. At the Effective Time, ArQule's certificate of incorporation and bylaws were amended and restated to reflect the change in control. The directors of Merger Sub—Rita Karachun, Caroline Litchfield, and Jon Filderman—became the directors and officers of the Surviving Corporation. All nine members of ArQule's former Board of Directors (Patrick J. Zenner, Timothy C. Barabe, Susan L. Kelley, Ronald M. Lindsay, Michael D. Loberg, William G. Messenger, Ran Nussbaum, and Paolo Pucci) resigned and ceased to be directors. ArQule's common stock was suspended from trading on Nasdaq effective before market open on January 16, 2020, and ArQule filed a Form 25 Notification requesting delisting and deregistration. ArQule intended to file a Form 15 certification requesting suspension of reporting obligations under Sections 13 and 15(d) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934. The Surviving Corporation repaid in full all outstanding obligations under its Loan and Security Agreement with Oxford Finance LLC.

## Rationale

**Merck's Strategic Interest**: Merck's acquisition of ArQule was driven primarily by the clinical potential and early success of ARQ 531, a Menin inhibitor in Phase 1 development for relapsed or refractory B-cell lymphoid malignancies. The SC 14D9 indicates that Merck initially approached ArQule to discuss a co-development and profit-sharing partnership for ARQ 531 following an introduction from an ArQule director in June 2019. Merck recognized the strategic and commercial potential of ARQ 531 and conducted due diligence on the program's clinical, safety, and regulatory profile during the summer and fall of 2019 under confidentiality agreements. As negotiations progressed and Merck indicated desire for greater control (November 20, 2019), the focus shifted from partnership to full acquisition of ArQule, allowing Merck to secure complete ownership of the ARQ 531 program and ArQule's pipeline of oncology assets (ARQ 092, ARQ 751, and derazantinib with partnered rights).

**ArQule's Strategic Rationale and Board Determination**: ArQule's Board determined the transaction fair and in the best interest of stockholders and the company based on several factors articulated in the SC 14D9. First, the Board considered ArQule's financial prospects and development risks as a standalone company. ArQule was a clinical-stage pharmaceutical company dependent on external funding to advance its pipeline toward regulatory approval and commercialization. The company faced significant risks inherent in drug development, including regulatory uncertainty, clinical trial outcomes, competitive landscape, and manufacturing and commercialization risks. Second, the Board weighed ArQule's capital needs and path to profitability. Development and commercialization of product candidates require substantial ongoing investment and financing. ArQule would require additional capital raises (likely dilutive to existing shareholders) to fund the long-term development of its pipeline. Merck's acquisition provided an exit at $20.00 per share in cash—a significant premium to the pre-announcement trading price of approximately $7.88-$9.67 per share in the weeks prior to November 29, 2019.

Third, the Board assessed the likelihood and timeline of obtaining partnership or acquisition offers from other parties. During 2018-2019, ArQule had engaged with nine interested parties regarding co-development and profit-sharing partnerships for ARQ 531, and eleven parties expressed interest in other product candidates. However, only six parties requested data room access for ARQ 531, and only one party engaged in substantive negotiations (reaching near-agreement in 2018 before withdrawing at JP Morgan Healthcare Conference in January 2019). Another party proposed terms in early 2019 that the Board found unattractive. Critically, by the time of Merck's November 29 proposal, no other party was actively pursuing a transaction with ArQule. Merck's offer to acquire the entire company at $20.00 per share was substantially superior to the partnership and licensing opportunities that had been available and rejected. The Board authorized management to seek improvements from Merck (resulting in negotiation from Merck's initial $18.00 proposal up to $20.00) but concluded that contacting other potential counterparties during the compressed timeline and amid risks of information leaks would likely delay or derail the Merck opportunity without securing a superior offer.

Fourth, the Board considered the likelihood of Merck completing the transaction. Merck had no financing condition—the tender offer obligation was not dependent on Merck securing funds or regulatory approvals (beyond HSR antitrust clearance and foreign regulatory requirements in Merck's operating jurisdictions, which the parties expected to clear routinely). Directors and officers of ArQule, representing approximately 8% of outstanding shares, had agreed to tender their Shares in the Offer pursuant to support agreements, significantly increasing the probability of achieving the Minimum Condition (>50% of fully diluted shares) and deal completion. The structure (tender offer followed by Section 251(h) merger) was designed to enable quick completion without requiring an additional stockholder vote.

Fifth, the Board considered and valued the fiduciary out provisions in the Merger Agreement. The agreement permitted the Board to change its recommendation and accept a Superior Proposal (a competing acquisition offering better financial or other terms) if such proposal was made and the Board, after consultation with financial and legal advisors, determined it was superior. This flexibility reduced the risk of being locked into an unfavorable deal if a better offer emerged.

**Board's Rationale for Recommendation**: Based on these considerations, the ArQule Board unanimously determined the Merger Agreement and proposed transactions fair to, and in the best interest of, ArQule and its stockholders. The Board resolved to recommend that stockholders accept the Offer and tender their Shares. The Board's recommendation was based not on any single factor but on the totality of the information presented, including the relative risks and uncertainties of continuing as a standalone company, the capital intensity of pharmaceutical development, the prior unsuccessful attempts to obtain superior partnership or acquisition terms from other parties, the substantial premium represented by Merck's $20.00 per-share offer relative to pre-proposal trading prices, and the likelihood of deal completion given Merck's financial strength and the stockholder support agreements already in place.

**Strategic Fit for Merck**: From Merck's perspective, the acquisition of ArQule secured ownership of ARQ 531 and complementary oncology assets at a time when ARQ 531's Phase 1 data was about to be presented at the ASH Conference (December 7-10, 2019). Merck's rapid execution—from initial partnership discussions in June to deal signing by December 6 and announcement on December 9—reflected competitive time pressure (Merck noted in internal discussions that other parties might also be monitoring ARQ 531's clinical progress and that securing the asset before positive clinical data were disclosed might prevent competing offers). The transaction allowed Merck to access a promising early-stage oncology pipeline and dedicate Merck's resources, infrastructure, and development expertise to accelerating the programs toward potential regulatory approval and commercialization.

## Decision Notes

**Acquisition Status**: This transaction qualifies as a completed acquisition. The Merger Agreement was executed December 6, 2019, the tender offer commenced December 17, 2019 at $20.00 per share in cash, achieved a 75.3% subscription rate by the January 15, 2020 expiration date (satisfying the >50% Minimum Condition), and the Merger was consummated effective January 16, 2020 under Delaware Section 251(h) without a stockholder vote. Completion evidence is provided by the Form 8-K filed January 16, 2020 (accession 0001104659-20-004500), which reports the Merger effective date, the suspension of Nasdaq trading, and the delisting of ArQule's common stock.

**Data Completeness**: SEC filing sources are complete. The SC 14D9 filed December 17, 2019 (accession 0001104659-19-073484) provides comprehensive disclosure of the interaction history between ArQule and Merck, the negotiation of financial terms, board deliberations, the fairness opinion from Centerview Partners, and the terms of the transaction. The Form 8-K filed January 16, 2020 (accession 0001104659-20-004500) confirms completion. The Merger Agreement and First Amendment are incorporated into these filings. Additional 8-K filings from December 9, 2019 (accession 0001104659-19-070788) and December 17, 2019 (accession 0001104659-19-073280) document the initial announcement and technical amendment, respectively.

**Stock Price and Market Data**: Weekly stock prices and trading volumes were successfully retrieved via EODHD market data API for ARQL covering the full requested period (December 9, 2017 through January 16, 2020). News items related to the transaction were successfully retrieved (5 major news items from announcement through close). Historical market cap data from EODHD returned HTTP 403 errors, so market cap figures in the Stock Price Over Time table are calculated mechanically as closing price multiplied by shares outstanding from the latest available SEC filing as of each date. This produces lagged and potentially understated market cap estimates, particularly for weeks prior to December 2019. The shares outstanding figure from the SC 14D9 (120,873,271 as of December 11, 2019) should be treated as the authoritative count for calculating the transaction's equity value.

**Pipeline Classification**: All pipeline assets were classified based on SEC filing language in the SC 14D9 and its incorporated exhibits. ARQ 531 is identified as a Menin inhibitor in Phase 1 dose escalation for relapsed or refractory B-cell lymphoid malignancies. ARQ 092 (Miransertib), ARQ 751, and ARQ 087 (Derazantinib) are identified from management projections incorporated into the filing. Derazantinib's partnership with Basilea Pharmaceutica AG and Sinovant Sciences Ltd. is disclosed. Other product candidates are referenced as subjects of partnership discussions but are not individually characterized by stage or indication in the public SC 14D9 text.

**Fairness Opinion**: Centerview Partners provided a written fairness opinion to the Board that the $20.00 per share consideration was fair from a financial point of view to stockholders (other than excluded shares). The opinion is referenced in the SC 14D9 but the full text appears to have been filed separately. The Board's determination of fairness was based on Centerview's analyses, management projections, and the Board's own assessment of ArQule's strategic alternatives and standalone prospects.

**Timing and Deal Certainty**: The transaction moved rapidly from initial partnership discussions (June 2019) to deal signing (December 6, 2019) to announcement (December 9, 2019) to tender offer commencement (December 17, 2019) and close (January 16, 2020)—a total of 6.5 weeks from announcement to completion. The pace was driven by Merck's request to announce by December 9 (timed with the ASH Conference at which ARQ 531 Phase 1 results would be presented). The tender offer was not extended, expiring on the original scheduled date (January 15, 2020), indicating no closing conditions were challenged. Completion risk was mitigated by the absence of a financing condition, the support agreements from directors and officers (covering ~8% of shares, well above the threshold to trigger compulsory merger provisions), and HSR antitrust clearance (obtained as evidenced by the 8-K noting no HSR extension was required).

**No Outstanding Issues**: The report finds no material outstanding questions, missing data, or unresolved interpretations that would warrant further investigation. All material dates, offer terms, board determinations, and completion milestones are disclosed in SEC filings. The transaction structure, voting analysis (Section 251(h) elimination of stockholder vote), and treatment of securities (options, warrants, ESPP rights) are standard for acquisition transactions of this type and era.
