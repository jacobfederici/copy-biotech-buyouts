## Summary

Epizyme, Inc. was acquired by Ipsen S.A. via a tender offer for $1.45 per share in cash plus a contingent value right (CVR) of up to $1.00 per share. The transaction was structured as a two-step acquisition under Section 251(h) of the DGCL, following a strategic review process where Epizyme faced significant capital needs and debt restructuring requirements. The deal closed on August 12, 2022, after approximately 74% of shares were tendered.

## Timeline

- 2021-12-00: Ipsen reaches out to Epizyme to explore a business relationship regarding TAZVERIK.
- 2022-01-28: Epizyme Board establishes a Special Committee to explore strategic transactions, financing, and debt restructuring.
- 2022-02-07: Epizyme and Ipsen enter into a Mutual Confidentiality Agreement.
- 2022-03-30: Ipsen submits a non-binding proposal to purchase assets relating to TAZVERIK for $310 million cash plus $320 million in milestones.
- 2022-04-05: Special Committee rejects the asset sale offer, preferring a whole-company acquisition.
- 2022-05-16: Ipsen submits an initial non-binding offer to acquire the entire company for $1.10 per share in cash.
- 2022-05-25: Ipsen increases offer to $1.25 per share plus a $0.70 CVR for FDA approval.
- 2022-06-02: Ipsen submits a third revised offer of $1.30 per share plus CVRs for FDA approval ($0.70) and net sales ($0.30).
- 2022-06-07: Ipsen submits a 'final' offer of $1.35 per share plus CVRs, contingent on exclusivity.
- 2022-06-10: Epizyme and Ipsen enter into an exclusivity agreement.
- 2022-06-23: Parties agree on a final cash consideration of $1.45 per share based on lease and debt cost reductions.
- 2022-06-26: Special Committee and Board unanimously approve the Merger Agreement.
- 2022-06-27: Merger Agreement is signed and publicly announced.
- 2022-07-12: Tender offer is formally commenced.
- 2022-08-11: Tender offer expires at 11:59 p.m. ET.
- 2022-08-12: Merger is completed; Epizyme becomes a wholly owned subsidiary of Ipsen.

## Interaction History

Negotiations began in early 2022 following an initial outreach by Ipsen in late 2021. Epizyme's CEO Grant Bogle and the Special Committee (chaired by David Mott) managed the process with financial advisors MTS and Jefferies. Ipsen initially proposed an asset purchase in March 2022, which the Special Committee rejected as it would leave the company with liabilities and no control premium. Epizyme contacted 38 potential counterparties; five entered NDAs, but only Ipsen provided a final proposal. Other parties (Party A, B, C, F) declined due to concerns over TAZVERIK's growth potential and Epizyme's high debt. Ipsen's offer evolved from $1.10 cash to $1.45 cash plus $1.00 in CVR milestones. The final price increase from $1.35 to $1.45 was negotiated based on Epizyme's success in reducing future lease obligations and debt costs. The Board approved the deal on June 26, 2022, following a fairness opinion from MTS Securities.

## Rationale

The Epizyme Board determined the deal was the best alternative to maximize stockholder value given the company's need for substantial additional capital to repay its secured term loan facility and fund clinical operations. The Board noted the risks of remaining independent, including the difficulty of raising equity in a depressed biotech market and the high cost of confirmatory trials for TAZVERIK. Ipsen sought to expand its oncology portfolio with a commercial-stage asset.

## Buyout Rumors

Source: OpenRouter web search (Gemini 3 Flash Preview). Pre-deal rumors only.

The following is a chronological timeline of buyout rumors, acquisition speculation, and reported takeover interest for Epizyme, Inc. (EPZM) prior to the official deal announcement with Ipsen on June 27, 2022.

*   **January 2017**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *BioWorld* / *FirstWord Pharma*
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Celgene, Johnson & Johnson (J&J)
    *   **Rumored Price:** Not specified.
    *   **Details:** Following positive clinical data for Epizyme’s lead candidate, tazemetostat, industry analysts and trade publications speculated that the results made the company a prime takeover target for large oncology players, specifically naming Celgene and J&J as logical suitors due to their existing interest in the epigenetics space.

*   **Late 2019 – Early 2020**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** Various market analysts (e.g., *Seeking Alpha*, *Motley Fool*)
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Gilead Sciences, Bristol Myers Squibb (BMS)
    *   **Rumored Price:** Speculation often centered on a significant premium over the then-trading price of ~$15–$20.
    *   **Details:** Speculation intensified ahead of the FDA approval of Tazverik. Analysts frequently listed Epizyme as a "top M&A candidate" for 2020, suggesting that a larger biopharma company would likely acquire Epizyme once the regulatory risk of its lead drug was removed.

*   **January 2022**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *Fierce Pharma* (reported post-deal via SEC filings)
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Ipsen
    *   **Rumored Price:** Not specified (initial offer was for U.S. rights only).
    *   **Details:** While not public at the time, it was later revealed that Ipsen made an initial private approach to buy the U.S. rights to Tazverik. This sparked internal strategic reviews at Epizyme regarding a potential sale of the entire company.

*   **April 2022**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *Fierce Pharma* / SEC Schedule 14D-9
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Unnamed "Company A" (Strategic Partner)
    *   **Rumored Price:** Not specified.
    *   **Details:** Epizyme engaged in advanced discussions regarding a "merger of equals" with another oncology-focused biotech. These rumors circulated in niche investor circles as the company's stock price began to experience unusual volatility. The deal ultimately fell through when the counterparty pursued a different transaction.

*   **June 21–24, 2022**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *Endpoints News* / *Bloomberg* (noted price action)
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Unspecified "Strategic Buyer"
    *   **Rumored Price:** Not specified.
    *   **Details:** In the week immediately preceding the official announcement, Epizyme’s stock price experienced a sudden and significant "spike" (rising from approximately $0.60 to nearly $0.95). Market observers and news outlets noted the unusual volume and price movement, reporting it as a sign that a buyout was imminent.

*   **June 27, 2022**
    *   **Confirmed Deal:** Ipsen officially announced the acquisition of Epizyme for $1.45 per share in cash plus a $1.00 Contingent Value Right (CVR).
