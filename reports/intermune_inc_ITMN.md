## Summary

InterMune, Inc. entered into a definitive merger agreement with Roche Holdings, Inc. on August 22, 2014, under which Roche acquired all outstanding shares of InterMune for $74.00 per share in cash. The transaction was structured as a tender offer followed by a second-step merger under Section 251(h) of the DGCL, valuing the company at approximately $8.765 billion. The deal focused on InterMune's lead product, pirfenidone, for the treatment of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF).

## Timeline

- 2014-02-01: InterMune reports positive top-line data from Phase 3 ASCEND study of pirfenidone.
- 2014-07-25: Roche CEO Severin Schwan meets with InterMune CEO Daniel Welch to express interest in an acquisition.
- 2014-07-26: Roche submits formal offer letter at $70.00 per share in cash.
- 2014-08-01: Company C submits a competing proposal at $57.00 per share in cash.

- 2014-08-15: Roche increases offer to $74.00 per share in cash.
- 2014-08-22: InterMune Board unanimously approves the Merger Agreement at $74.00 per share.
- 2014-08-24: Public announcement of the transaction.
- 2014-08-29: Commencement of the tender offer.
- 2014-09-26: Tender offer expires at midnight.
- 2014-09-29: Roche accepts shares for payment and completes the merger via Section 251(h).

## Interaction History

Negotiations began in July 2014 when Roche CEO Severin Schwan met with InterMune CEO Daniel Welch. Roche initially proposed $70.00 per share on July 26, 2014. InterMune's board formed an Executive Committee (excluding Director A, who was an executive at a potential competitor, Company A) to evaluate the bid. On August 1, 2014, Company C proposed $57.00 per share, which the board deemed insufficient. Following a leak on August 13, several other companies (B and D) made inquiries but did not provide concrete superior proposals. On August 15, Schwan and Welch negotiated a price increase to $74.00. InterMune pushed for high closing certainty regarding FDA approval risks for pirfenidone. Roche agreed to risk-allocation terms that excluded FDA labeling or approval delays from the 'Material Adverse Effect' definition. The board ultimately accepted the $74.00 offer, noting it was a 63% premium to the undisturbed price.

## Rationale

The InterMune board determined the cash offer provided immediate liquidity and certainty of value, mitigating the significant risks associated with the upcoming U.S. launch of pirfenidone and potential FDA regulatory hurdles. Roche sought to strengthen its respiratory portfolio and leverage its global infrastructure to launch pirfenidone in the U.S. market.
