## Summary

Shire plc acquired New River Pharmaceuticals Inc. for $64.00 per share in an all-cash tender offer followed by a second-step merger. The transaction consolidated Shire's control over VYVANSE, a lead ADHD product candidate previously co-developed by the two companies. The deal was supported by a tender agreement from Chairman and CEO Randal J. Kirk, who controlled approximately 50.2% of the outstanding shares.

## Timeline

- 2005-01-31: New River and Shire enter into an original collaboration agreement for VYVANSE.
- 2006-08-04: Randal Kirk proposes a structure where Shire acquires New River after a spin-off of non-VYVANSE assets.
- 2006-11-15: New River engages Bear Stearns to explore a sale of the company.
- 2007-01-10: Shire submits a non-binding proposal to acquire New River for $61.50 per share in cash.
- 2007-02-18: Shire increases its offer to $63.00 per share; Randal Kirk counters that $64.00 might be acceptable.
- 2007-02-19: Shire Board authorizes $64.00 per share; New River Board unanimously approves the merger.
- 2007-02-20: Merger Agreement and Tender and Support Agreement are signed and publicly announced.
- 2007-03-02: Tender offer formally commences.
- 2007-04-17: Tender offer expires with 96.4% of shares tendered.
- 2007-04-19: Merger completed; New River becomes a wholly owned subsidiary of Shire.

## Interaction History

Negotiations began in earnest in late 2006 following years of collaboration on VYVANSE. In August 2006, New River CEO Randal Kirk suggested a transaction structure involving a spin-off of non-core assets, which Shire initially declined. In January 2007, Shire CEO Matthew Emmens proposed an all-cash acquisition at $61.50 per share. New River's financial advisors, Bear Stearns and Merrill Lynch, contacted 14 other potential acquirers, but no superior proposals emerged. On February 18, 2007, Shire increased its bid to $63.00. Mr. Kirk negotiated this up to $64.00 on February 19, 2007, leading to board approval. Regulatory clearance was obtained via HSR filing on March 1, 2007.

## Rationale

The New River Board determined the merger was in the best interests of shareholders due to the significant cash premium, the risks of remaining an independent small specialty pharmaceutical company, and the resources Shire could provide for the commercialization of VYVANSE. Shire sought to consolidate its interest in VYVANSE and capture the full economics of the product launch.

## Evidence

- The Company Board, at a special meeting held on February 19, 2007, by unanimous vote, approved and adopted the Merger Agreement... and determined that the Offer and the Merger are advisable, fair to and in the best interests of the Company and its shareholders. (SC 14D9, Item 4)
- On April 19, 2007, Purchaser was merged with and into the Company... with the Company surviving the Merger as an indirect wholly owned subsidiary of Parent. (8-K, 2007-04-24)

## Buyout Rumors

Source: OpenRouter web search (Gemini 3 Flash Preview). Pre-deal rumors only.

The following is a chronological timeline of buyout rumors, acquisition speculation, and takeover reports regarding New River Pharmaceuticals Inc. (NRPH) prior to the confirmed acquisition announcement by Shire plc on February 20, 2007.

*   **January 13, 2007**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *The Economic Times* (via Bloomberg/San Francisco)
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Shire plc (implied as the most logical buyer due to existing partnership)
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Not specified.
    *   **Report Details:** Vincent Aita of Kilkenny Capital Management identified New River Pharmaceuticals as a likely "takeover target" for 2007. The report noted that biotech companies in existing partnerships, such as New River’s deal with Shire for its hyperactivity treatment (Vyvanse), were prime candidates for acquisition as large pharmaceutical companies sought to secure new drug pipelines.

*   **February 16, 2007**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** Various market observers/Financial press (retrospectively cited in deal coverage)
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Shire plc
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Speculation intensified as NRPH shares rose significantly in the days immediately preceding the deal.
    *   **Report Details:** Heavy trading volume and a share price increase (closing at approximately $58.00) led to widespread market speculation that a deal with Shire was imminent. Analysts noted that Shire needed to protect its ADHD franchise as Adderall XR faced generic competition in 2009.

*   **March 12, 2007 (Post-Announcement Speculation of a Counter-Bid)**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *The Independent* (UK) / *Pharmafile*
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Unnamed "rival bidders" (at least four other pharmaceutical companies were reported to have been interested).
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Speculation of a counter-bid higher than Shire's $64.00 per share offer.
    *   **Report Details:** Although Shire had already announced its $2.6 billion agreement on February 20, rumors surfaced in early March that a rival bid was being prepared. *The Independent* quoted an analyst describing Shire’s bid as a "sitting duck." Reports suggested that if Vyvanse had received FDA approval earlier (in December 2006), Shire likely would have faced a bidding war from at least four other interested parties. Shire subsequently dismissed these claims, noting that no other formal bidders had emerged during the process.
