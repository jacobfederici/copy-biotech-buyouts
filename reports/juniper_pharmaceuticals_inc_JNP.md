## Summary

Juniper Pharmaceuticals, Inc. was acquired by Catalent Pharma Solutions, Inc., a subsidiary of Catalent, Inc., through a cash tender offer for $11.50 per share, totaling approximately $130 million. The acquisition was completed on August 14, 2018, following the expiration of the tender offer.

## Interaction History

The Board of Directors of Juniper Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (the "Company") began evaluating strategic alternatives in late 2016 after its lead product, COL-1077, failed a Phase 2b clinical trial. On September 7, 2017, the Board approved a strategic reprioritization to focus on its core manufacturing and CDMO businesses and reduce R&D. The Board then initiated a process to explore strategic opportunities, including a potential sale, and began interviewing financial advisors. On October 24, 2017, the Board formally decided to pursue a strategic process to maximize stockholder value and established a Transaction Committee, comprising independent directors Jennifer Good, Richard Messina, Mary Ann Gray, and James Geraghty (Chair), to oversee this process. Goodwin Procter LLP served as the Company's outside legal counsel, advising on fiduciary duties.

On December 14, 2017, the Board engaged Rothschild Inc. as its financial advisor and Chestnut Securities, Inc. for supplemental advisory services. On January 23, 2018, the Company received an unsolicited, non-binding offer from Party A to acquire the Company for $8.20 per share in an all-cash transaction. Following discussions, the Board decided to publicly announce its exploration of strategic alternatives to manage market rumors and to engage with Party A under a confidentiality agreement to encourage an improved offer. Confidentiality agreements with customary non-disclosure and standstill provisions were subsequently executed with Party A (February 15, 2018) and Catalent (February 16, 2018), among others.

By March 15, 2018, 38 parties had expressed interest and executed confidentiality agreements. Six parties (Catalent, Party A, Party B, Party C, Party E, and Party F) submitted preliminary non-binding indications of interest, with prices ranging from $8.00-$9.00 (Party B) to $10.50-$12.50 (Party E), and Catalent offering $10.50. Party D and Party G requested more time. On March 20, 2018, the Board, with Rothschild's assistance, decided to advance all six bidders to the second phase to foster competitive dynamics and encouraged Party D and Party G to participate. Party D submitted an offer of $10.00-$10.50 on March 28, 2018, and Party G offered $12.00-$12.50 on April 9, 2018.

By May 1, 2018, Party D and Party E withdrew. Party B offered $9.00, Party C offered $9.20, Party F offered $9.95, Party H offered $8.25-$9.77, and Catalent offered $11.00. Party A withdrew on May 2, 2018. On May 7, 2018, the Board formally established a Special Committee, consisting of the same independent directors as the Transaction Committee, to manage the strategic alternatives process due to potential conflicts of interest with management. Party G submitted a revised offer of $12.25 per share on May 7, 2018. The Special Committee decided to advance Catalent, Party B, and Party G to the third round, given their higher offers, and instructed Rothschild to encourage improved offers. Party B subsequently increased its offer to $10.00 on May 9, 2018, and Party F to $9.95 on May 10, 2018, with a contingent value rights mechanism proposed by Party F. The Special Committee declined exclusivity requests from Party F, which then withdrew.

On June 12, 2018, Catalent, Party G, and Party B submitted final written offers. Catalent offered $11.00 per share with a 45-day exclusivity request and retention agreements for key employees. Party G offered $11.00 per share with a 45-day exclusivity request and a complex offshore funding structure. Party B offered $11.50 per share with a 10-business day exclusivity request and a commitment to sign within five business days. The Special Committee, after evaluating execution risks, decided to pursue Party B's offer, counter-proposing $11.75 per share, a 3% break-up fee, and management arrangements only after closing, or a 5% reverse break-up fee. Party B accepted the terms but later reduced its offer to $10.85 per share on June 20, 2018, citing transaction expenses and net working capital adjustments, and requested a five-day exclusivity period. The Special Committee, believing this was Party B's best and final offer, decided to proceed with Party B if Catalent's terms remained unchanged.

On June 21, 2018, Catalent confirmed its $11.00 offer as best and final, with a potentially reduced 30-day exclusivity period and retention agreements. The Company then entered into a five-day exclusivity agreement with Party B. On June 25, 2018, while under exclusivity with Party B, Catalent submitted an unsolicited, revised offer of $11.25 per share, removing certain signing contingencies and setting a deadline of June 26, 2018. The Special Committee, unable to engage with Catalent due to exclusivity, directed Rothschild to inform Party B of the higher unsolicited offer and that Party B needed to significantly increase its offer. Party B declined to increase its offer and stated it would not continue negotiations if exclusivity expired without a definitive agreement. After the exclusivity with Party B expired on June 27, 2018, the Company re-engaged with Catalent, requesting its last, best, and final offer. On June 28, 2018, Catalent submitted a revised offer of $11.50 per share, stating it was its best and final. On July 2, 2018, the Board, upon the Special Committee's recommendation and Rothschild's fairness opinion, unanimously approved the Merger Agreement with Catalent for $11.50 per share. The Compensation Committee also approved executive compensation arrangements. After the Merger Agreement was signed, Rothschild received a revised proposal from Party B at $11.25 per share.

## Rationale

The Board considered the Offer Price of $11.50 per share to be favorable, representing a significant premium over recent trading prices and the unaffected share price prior to the announcement of exploring strategic alternatives. The Board believed the cash consideration provided certainty, immediate value, and liquidity to stockholders. They also considered the industry dynamics, including increasing competition and consolidation in the CDMO sector, and the Company's declining margins due to higher supply costs, which posed long-term risks to the Company's standalone prospects. The Board concluded that the value offered in the acquisition was more favorable than continuing as an independent public company, especially after a competitive process involving 92 parties. Rothschild's fairness opinion supported the financial terms of the transaction. The Board also noted the tender offer structure would allow for a relatively short timeframe to completion, reducing uncertainty and disruption.
