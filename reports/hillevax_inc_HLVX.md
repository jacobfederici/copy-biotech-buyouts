## Summary

HilleVax, Inc. entered into a definitive merger agreement with XOMA Royalty Corporation following a strategic review triggered by the failure of its Phase 2b clinical trial for HIL-214. The transaction was structured as a tender offer for $1.95 per share in cash plus a contingent value right (CVR) representing interests in legacy assets, lease savings, and net cash adjustments. The deal was completed via a Section 251(h) merger on September 17, 2025, after approximately 77.48% of shares were tendered.

## Interaction History

Following the failure of the NEST-IN1 trial in July 2024, HilleVax management contacted 33 potential counterparties. Initial interest focused on reverse mergers with private companies (Parties D, E, and F), but the Board eventually pivoted to 'financial buyer' transactions (cash-out deals based on net cash). In May 2025, HilleVax received competing bids from Parent (XOMA), Party J, Party K, and Party L. Party L proposed a bitcoin-related business combination which was eventually abandoned. Parent and Party K emerged as the primary bidders. Negotiations focused heavily on the 'Net Cash' definition and the structure of the CVR, particularly regarding the Harrison Lease security deposit and potential sublease savings. On May 19, 2025, HilleVax countered Parent's offer, requesting 90-100% distribution of lease savings and a specific net cash target of $103.8M. Parent accepted. Final negotiations in July 2025 resolved disputes over post-closing liability recourse and the duration of the legacy asset transaction period (extended from one to two years). The Board approved the final $1.95/share cash price on August 3, 2025, after reviewing a management dissolution analysis showing the deal was superior to a statutory liquidation.

## Rationale

The HilleVax Board determined the transaction was superior to a standalone strategy or a statutory liquidation. Management's dissolution analysis estimated a present value of $2.04 per share in a liquidation scenario, but noted significant risks including unknown contingent liabilities and the need to maintain public company costs during a multi-year wind-down. The Board concluded the XOMA offer provided immediate liquidity and a higher certainty of value, while the CVR allowed shareholders to retain upside from legacy assets and lease subleasing.

## Evidence

- On August 3, 2025, the HilleVax Board held a meeting at which the HilleVax Board unanimously (i) determined that the Offer, the Merger and the other Transactions are fair to and in the best interests of the Company and the Company’s stockholders (SC 14D9).
- Parent completed the acquisition of the Company on September 17, 2025, by causing Merger Sub to merge with and into the Company (8-K, 2025-09-17).

## Decision Notes

The deal was accepted as a cash-out of a failed biotech. CVR milestones include 90% of net proceeds from norovirus program dispositions within 2 years, return of the $1.6M Harrison Lease security deposit, and 90-100% of lease savings. Shares outstanding (50,141,064) sourced from the SC 14D9 'Securities' section as of August 15, 2025.

No pre-deal rumors exist for HilleVax, Inc. (HLVX).

The acquisition of HilleVax by XOMA Royalty Corporation was announced on August 4, 2025, as a definitive merger agreement. Prior to this announcement, there were no documented media reports, market rumors, or specific takeover speculations involving a named buyer or rumored price. 

The company had publicly disclosed in July 2024 that it was "exploring strategic alternatives" following the failure of its Phase 2b clinical trial for its lead norovirus vaccine candidate (HIL-214). While this disclosure signaled to the market that the company was potentially for sale, no specific rumors regarding an acquirer or deal terms surfaced in the press before the formal agreement with XOMA Royalty was made public.
