## Summary

Lisata Therapeutics entered into a definitive merger agreement to be acquired by Kuva Labs Inc. via a tender offer for $4.00 per share in cash plus a contingent value right (CVR) worth up to $3.00. The transaction followed a period of financial distress for Lisata and multiple extensions and amendments to the deal terms due to the buyer's financing delays.

## Timeline

- 2024-11-30: Lisata and Kuva enter into an exclusive license and collaboration agreement for certepetide.
- 2025-01-25: Kuva submits an unsolicited non-binding term sheet for a merger.
- 2025-04-25: Parties execute a non-binding term sheet at $6.00 per share with a 60-day exclusivity period.
- 2026-01-20: Lisata Board approves a binding term sheet at $4.00 per share plus two CVRs.
- 2026-03-06: Original Merger Agreement executed at $5.00 per share plus one $1.00 CVR.
- 2026-04-13: Kuva fails to commence the tender offer by the agreed deadline.
- 2026-05-03: First Merger Agreement Amendment executed to extend commencement deadline to May 29, 2026.
- 2026-05-29: Second Merger Agreement Amendment executed, reducing upfront cash to $4.00 and increasing CVR value to $3.00.
- 2026-06-08: Third Merger Agreement Amendment executed, extending commencement deadline to June 10, 2026.
- 2026-06-10: Kuva Acquisition Corp. commences the tender offer.

## Interaction History

Negotiations began in late 2024 following a licensing deal. Kuva initially proposed a complex merger in January 2025. Throughout 2025, the parties negotiated price points ranging from $5.50 to $7.00, eventually settling on a binding term sheet in January 2026 at $4.00 plus CVRs. After the original merger agreement was signed in March 2026 at $5.00 cash, Kuva faced significant financing hurdles, leading to multiple missed deadlines. To maintain the deal, Lisata negotiated several amendments. In May 2026, Kuva proposed reducing the upfront cash to $4.00 to facilitate financing, while increasing the CVR potential to $3.00. Lisata's board, led by CEO David Mazzo and advised by H.C. Wainwright, accepted these terms due to the company's limited cash runway and lack of superior alternatives.
