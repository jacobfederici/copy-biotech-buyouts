## Summary

CureVac N.V. entered into a Purchase Agreement with BioNTech SE for an all-stock exchange offer valued at approximately $1.25 billion. BioNTech is offering to exchange each CureVac share for BioNTech ADSs based on a fixed-value collar mechanism. The transaction follows a period of patent litigation between the parties regarding mRNA technology, which was settled in conjunction with the merger agreement. The deal includes a post-offer reorganization process under Dutch law to ensure BioNTech acquires 100% ownership of CureVac's operations.

## Interaction History

Negotiations began in February 2025, initially focused on settling high-stakes patent litigation regarding COVID-19 vaccines. James Ryan (BioNTech) and Thaminda Ramanayake (CureVac) led early discussions. On February 26, 2025, CEOs Ugur Sahin and Alexander Zehnder met to pivot from litigation to a strategic combination. BioNTech issued a preliminary IOI on March 21, which was rejected as too low. A revised offer on April 7 increased the implied premium significantly. Due diligence occurred throughout April and May, including site visits to Tübingen. On June 7, BioNTech proposed the final $1.25 billion fixed-value collar structure to mitigate market volatility. The CureVac Settlement Committee, comprised of independent directors Jean Stéphenne, Klaus Schollmeier, and Craig Tooman, oversaw the final stages to ensure fiduciary compliance. Regulatory clearances were obtained from the FTC (HSR expired August 11, 2025) and the European Commission (EU FSR cleared October 10, 2025).

## Rationale

The CureVac Boards determined the deal is in the best interest of the company and its sustainable success. Key drivers included the significant premium over historical trading prices (111% over the April 7 price), the mitigation of market volatility through the fixed-value collar, and the resolution of costly, uncertain patent litigation. The board also noted that BioNTech's scale and reputation would enhance the development of CureVac's mRNA platform.

## Evidence

- The Company Boards unanimously recommend that the Company’s shareholders accept the Offer and tender their Company Shares pursuant to the Offer. (SC 14D9, Item 2(b))
- On June 12, 2025, Buyer and the Company entered into the Purchase Agreement. (SC 14D9, Item 3(b))

## Decision Notes

The deal value is explicitly stated as a $1.25 billion total value target in the background of the offer. Shares outstanding (225,181,647) are as of October 17, 2025. The offer includes a complex Dutch 'Post-Offer Reorganization' to squeeze out minority holders via a legal downstream merger and cancellation of shares.

The following is a chronological timeline of buyout rumors, acquisition speculation, and reported takeover overtures for CureVac N.V. (CVAC) prior to the confirmed deal announcement with BioNTech on June 12, 2025.

*   **March 11, 2020**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *Welt am Sonntag* (later widely cited by *Reuters*, *The New York Times*, and *The Guardian*)
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** United States Government (Trump Administration)
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Not specified; reported as an attempt to secure "exclusive access" to a vaccine.
    *   **Details:** Anonymous German government sources reported that U.S. President Donald Trump offered large sums of money to CureVac to relocate its research to the United States or to secure a COVID-19 vaccine "only for the USA." The report followed a March 2 meeting between then-CEO Daniel Menichella and the White House. German officials and CureVac’s lead investor, Dietmar Hopp, subsequently rejected the overtures, stating the company was not for sale.

*   **July 2, 2020**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *Electrek*, *Endpoints News*, and *Twitter* (Elon Musk)
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Tesla, Inc. (Speculation of a "Manufacturing Takeover")
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Not reported.
    *   **Details:** Following a tweet from Elon Musk stating that Tesla was building "RNA microfactories for CureVac," market speculation intensified regarding a potential acquisition or deep structural integration. While the companies officially announced a "manufacturing partnership" involving Tesla Grohmann Automation, the unusual nature of an automaker partnering with a biotech led to persistent rumors that Tesla might eventually acquire the company to control the "software-defined" vaccine production process.

*   **June 13, 2024**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *BioPharma Dive* and *Fierce Biotech* (Analyst Speculation)
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** GSK (GlaxoSmithKline)
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Not reported.
    *   **Details:** Following a period of clinical setbacks for CureVac’s first-generation COVID-19 vaccine, industry analysts speculated that GSK—CureVac’s primary collaborator—would move to acquire the company outright to secure its second-generation mRNA backbone. Instead of a full takeover, the companies announced a major restructuring of their deal on July 3, 2024, where GSK paid €400 million upfront to take full control of the infectious disease programs, leaving CureVac as an independent oncology-focused firm.

*   **June 11, 2025**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *Wall Street Analysts* (cited in post-deal reports by *BioPharma Dive* and *MedCity News*)
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** BioNTech SE
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Speculated as a "settlement-style" premium.
    *   **Details:** In the days immediately preceding the official announcement, market chatter and analyst notes (such as those from Leerink Partners) suggested that BioNTech might acquire CureVac as a strategic "out-of-court settlement" for their long-running patent litigation. Analysts speculated that buying the company would be cheaper for BioNTech than risking a multi-billion dollar royalty hit in the German and U.S. courts where trials were imminent.
