## Summary

ZymoGenetics, Inc. entered into a definitive merger agreement with Bristol-Myers Squibb Company (BMS) under which BMS, through its subsidiary Zeus Acquisition Corporation, acquired all outstanding shares of ZymoGenetics for $9.75 per share in cash. The transaction was structured as a tender offer followed by a second-step merger, valuing the company at approximately $885 million. The deal consolidated BMS's control over the PEG-IFN-lambda program, which was already under a global collaboration between the two companies.

- 2010-10-07: Tender offer expires with approximately 94.9% of shares tendered.
- 2010-10-12: Merger completed; ZymoGenetics becomes a wholly-owned subsidiary of BMS.

## Interaction History

Negotiations were characterized by a long-standing relationship between the parties, specifically their 2009 collaboration on PEG-IFN-lambda. In May 2010, BMS re-initiated acquisition interest. Initial offers of $8.00-$9.00 and $8.75 were rejected by the ZymoGenetics board, led by Chairman Dr. Bruce Carter and CEO Dr. Douglas Williams, as inadequate. BMS emphasized a deadline of September 7, 2010, to coincide with its own board meeting. ZymoGenetics' financial advisor, Goldman Sachs, conducted a financial analysis but did not broadly solicit other buyers due to BMS's existing rights under the collaboration agreement, which likely deterred other bidders. Final price negotiations moved from $9.50 to $9.75 on August 11, 2010. Regulatory clearance was obtained under the HSR Act, and the deal closed following a successful tender of over 90% of shares, allowing for a short-form merger.

## Rationale

The ZymoGenetics board determined the cash offer provided compelling and certain value compared to the risks of remaining independent, including product development risks for PEG-IFN-lambda and IL-21, and the need for substantial future dilutive equity financing. BMS sought to fully integrate the PEG-IFN-lambda program and leverage ZymoGenetics' expertise in therapeutic proteins and its marketed product, RECOTHROM.

## Decision Notes

The transaction was completed via a tender offer and subsequent short-form merger. The deal value of $885 million is explicitly stated in the 8-K (Item 5.01). Shares outstanding (86,584,072) were sourced from the SC 14D9 as of August 31, 2010.

## Buyout Rumors

Source: OpenRouter web search (Gemini 3 Flash Preview). Pre-deal rumors only.

Based on a review of historical financial reporting and market news prior to the definitive acquisition announcement on September 7, 2010, there were no specific, widely reported buyout rumors or leaked price targets for ZymoGenetics, Inc. (ZGEN).

The acquisition by Bristol-Myers Squibb (BMS) was largely characterized by market analysts as a "bolt-on" deal that grew naturally out of an existing partnership. While ZymoGenetics was occasionally mentioned in broader "biotech consolidation" lists due to its struggling stock price and valuable pipeline, no specific rumors naming a buyer or price appeared in major outlets before the deal.

### **Timeline of Events Leading to Acquisition**

*   **January 13, 2009:** **Bristol-Myers Squibb** and **ZymoGenetics** announce a global collaboration to develop and commercialize PEG-Interferon lambda. BMS pays $85 million upfront. This partnership established the relationship that eventually led to the takeover.
*   **January 7, 2010:** ZymoGenetics prices a public offering of 14 million shares at $6.00 per share to raise capital. At this time, the company was viewed as an independent entity seeking to fund its own operations, with no active takeover speculation reported.
*   **August 2010:** Market activity in ZGEN options begins to increase, but no specific reporting outlet links this to a pending buyout.
*   **September 7, 2010 (Confirmed Deal):** **Bristol-Myers Squibb** and **ZymoGenetics** announce a definitive agreement for BMS to acquire ZGEN for **$9.75 per share** in cash (approximately $885 million). The price represented an 84% premium over the previous day's closing price.

**Conclusion:** No pre-deal rumors exist. The acquisition was a surprise to the broader market, despite the existing co-development partnership between the two companies.
