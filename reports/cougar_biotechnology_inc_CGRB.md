## Summary

Johnson & Johnson acquired Cougar Biotechnology for $43.00 per share in an all-cash tender offer valued at approximately $970 million. The acquisition focused on Cougar's lead oncology asset, abiraterone acetate, which was in Phase 3 clinical trials for prostate cancer. The deal followed a competitive auction process involving multiple bidders and was completed on July 3, 2009, after approximately 84.4% of shares were tendered.

## Interaction History

The transaction resulted from a formal auction process initiated in late 2008. Merrill Lynch contacted approximately 20 potential buyers, with 17 executing confidentiality agreements. Bidder A initially proposed $43.00-$50.00 in stock but refused a collar and failed to provide a formal bid due to extensive diligence requests. Bidder B was highly active, starting at $28.00 and eventually reaching $39.00, but remained below Johnson & Johnson's final price. Johnson & Johnson entered the process later, initially offering $36.00 in March 2009, then $40.00 in April, and finally $43.00 on April 30, 2009. A third party, Bidder C, proposed a mixed cash and CVR structure but withdrew on May 17, 2009. Negotiations with J&J involved removing a closing condition related to third-party consents and securing a retention agreement for CEO Alan Auerbach. The Board, advised by Latham & Watkins, Merrill Lynch, and Leerink Swann, ultimately selected J&J's $43.00 cash offer as the superior proposal.

## Rationale

The Cougar Board determined that the $43.00 cash price provided stockholders with certain value at a significant premium (18.3% over the last trading day and 36.1% over the 3-month average) compared to the risks of remaining independent. These risks included the need for significant additional capital, clinical trial uncertainty, and regulatory hurdles. Johnson & Johnson sought to expand its oncology portfolio with a late-stage prostate cancer asset.

## Decision Notes

The deal was completed via a tender offer followed by a merger. Shares outstanding (20,791,368) were sourced from the SC 14D9 filed June 5, 2009. The implied equity value is calculated based on basic shares; the total deal value including options/warrants was estimated at approximately $970M-$1B in contemporaneous reports, though the upfront equity value for outstanding shares is ~$894M.

*   **May 20, 2009**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** Various market rumors/Trading floor chatter
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Johnson & Johnson

Market participants specifically pointed to Johnson & Johnson due to its existing interest in the prostate cancer space and its history of "snapping up" well-regarded biotech firms.
