## Summary

Tetraphase Pharmaceuticals, Inc., a Watertown, Massachusetts-based commercial-stage antibiotics company whose sole marketed product was XERAVA (eravacycline), was acquired by La Jolla Pharmaceutical Company on July 28, 2020 at $2.00 per share in cash plus contingent value rights of up to $16.0 million in aggregate, representing total upfront equity consideration of approximately $14.5 million (based on 7,263,236 shares outstanding as of June 22, 2020) and a maximum total of approximately $30.5 million. The transaction followed an extended multi-bidder competitive process spanning from early 2019, during which Tetraphase engaged with numerous potential acquirers while managing declining cash reserves, two equity financings, and a near-bankruptcy situation. Two prior acquisition agreements — first with AcelRx Pharmaceuticals in March 2020 and then with Melinta Therapeutics in June 2020 — were terminated before La Jolla's superior unsolicited cash offer prevailed. La Jolla completed the tender offer on July 28, 2020 and Tetraphase was delisted from Nasdaq the same day.

## Interaction History

### 2019 Strategic Process (January–September 2019)

At the January 2019 J.P. Morgan Healthcare Conference, representatives of Tetraphase met with ten potential strategic partners, including AcelRx and Companies B, C, D, E, F, and G. On March 1, 2019, Company F delivered an unsolicited stock-for-stock acquisition proposal to then-CEO Guy Macdonald; the offer would have resulted in Tetraphase stockholders holding approximately 37% of the combined company.

The Tetraphase Board was notified on March 3, 2019, and held a telephonic meeting on March 4, 2019, attended by management, Financial Advisor A, and Law Firm A (outside special counsel for a potential transaction with Company F). The Board directed a market check. On March 5, Financial Advisor A contacted Companies H and I; both declined, stating they were not interested in antibiotic space assets. Officers also contacted Companies E and J; Company J declined. Company E officer initially declined in early March, citing structural and valuation concerns, including unwillingness to seek stockholder approval for a transaction.

From March 12 to April 5, 2019, Tetraphase and Company F engaged in reciprocal due diligence, and the Tetraphase Board reviewed a draft merger agreement on March 21 and March 28. On April 5, Company F withdrew, citing financial and liquidity concerns with Tetraphase's business. In late June 2019, Company F's CEO re-contacted Macdonald; meetings resumed in Philadelphia on July 2. Company C simultaneously renewed interest due to developments with its own business and delivered a stock-for-stock proposal on July 25, 2019 (Tetraphase stockholders would hold approximately 20% of combined company).

The Pricing Committee (L. Patrick Gage, Garen Bohlin, John Freund) directed another market check on July 26, 2019. Janney contacted approximately seven parties between July 26 and August 1. Tetraphase provided data room access to Companies B, C, F, G, I, K, and L. Company G submitted an all-cash proposal for aggregate consideration of $20 million on approximately August 12. By September 4, all parties — G, B, F, I, K, L — had withdrawn, citing market conditions, valuation, and liquidity concerns. Company C also withdrew by August 25, citing market conditions.

### October–November 2019: Company E Discussions and First Equity Financing

On October 2, 2019, Maria Stahl and a Company E officer met in person; the Company E officer stated the Company E board was interested in a strategic transaction. Larry Edwards had succeeded Guy Macdonald as President and CEO effective August 1, 2019. On October 4, the officer reiterated interest. The Pricing Committee met telephonically on October 4 and noted that any equity offering would have to cease if Tetraphase pursued the Company E inquiry; the Committee directed management to call a full Board meeting.

On October 7, Company E delivered a non-binding indication of interest for approximately $14.56 million payable in Company E stock (approximately 14% combined company ownership), with exchange ratio to be set based on market prices at the time of execution. The Tetraphase Board on October 7 determined to halt the public offering and pursue the Company E transaction. Tetraphase engaged four potential financial advisors between October 7–10.

From October 9 to October 18, Company E conducted in-person due diligence at Tetraphase offices; representatives of Company E met with Edwards, Stahl, and Tetraphase's operations team. Company E delivered a draft merger agreement on approximately October 18. The Tetraphase Board reviewed it on October 20 and discussed termination provisions. Multiple calls and meetings followed among Edwards, Stahl, Company E officers, WilmerHale (Tetraphase's outside corporate counsel), and Company E's outside counsel.

On October 23, 2019, Tetraphase engaged Janney Montgomery Scott LLC as financial advisor, both for a fairness opinion and to conduct a market check. The Board's primary concern was that Company E's termination provisions required a specified cash threshold at closing that Tetraphase determined it could not meet, and Company E would not negotiate on this threshold. On October 26, the Board terminated discussions with Company E. Ms. Stahl notified Company E.

Tetraphase re-engaged HCW and, from October 26–29, worked to consummate an equity offering. On October 30, Tetraphase announced an $8.0 million registered direct offering to Armistice Capital at market, consisting of common stock, common stock warrants (containing Black-Scholes Put Provisions requiring any acquirer to redeem warrants at Black-Scholes value), and pre-funded warrants. The financing closed November 1, 2019. Upon closing, Armistice Capital became Tetraphase's largest stockholder on both an actual and fully-diluted basis.

### December 2019–January 2020: Near-Bankruptcy Situation and Company P/Company E Proposals

On December 4, 2019, the Board determined the company should prepare for a potential bankruptcy sale process and engaged Financial Advisors B and C, and Delaware special bankruptcy counsel between December 5–12. Deerfield Management Company, L.P. was contacted on approximately December 19 by Financial Advisor B based on public reports of Deerfield's involvement in the antibiotic sector. Deerfield later indicated it was not interested in Tetraphase given its involvement with Melinta's Chapter 11.

On December 24, Company P (affiliated with Armistice Capital; Steve Boyd is a member of Company P's board of directors) delivered an unsolicited $9.0 million cash proposal. On December 26, the Board directed Financial Advisor B to conduct another market check; Financial Advisor B contacted AcelRx, Company E, G, L, M, Deerfield, and Company O. On December 28, Company E delivered an all-stock proposal valued at approximately $14.2 million.

From January 1–14, 2020, Tetraphase and its advisors negotiated competing proposals from Company P and Company E, discussed bridge financing terms with Company P and Armistice Capital, and evaluated a revised Company E proposal (stalking horse bidder in a chapter 11 proceeding) with expense reimbursement requests of up to $250,000. On January 12, Steve Boyd (Armistice Capital) proposed a $15 million equity investment; HCW sent a term sheet. On January 14, Boyd informed Edwards that Armistice would not fund the Company P acquisition proposal. The Board approved moving forward with an Armistice-led equity financing.

On January 21, 2020, Steve Boyd and Keith Maher (both Armistice Capital principals) joined the Tetraphase Board in connection with the January Offerings. Company E also withdrew on January 21, citing Tetraphase's unwillingness to provide additional expense reimbursement for a potential stalking horse bid. The January Offerings ($17.5 million aggregate in two tranches: $10 million private placement with Armistice and $7.5 million concurrent registered direct with other investors) were announced on January 22 and closed January 24, 2020.

### January–March 2020: AcelRx Negotiations

On January 15, 2020, at the J.P. Morgan Healthcare Conference, Edwards met AcelRx CEO Vincent Angotti. On January 20, Tetraphase director John Freund and Angotti met in Palo Alto; Angotti indicated AcelRx was interested in a potential merger. On January 30–31, Edwards, Stahl, Watt, and Kevin Lloyd (VP Program Management) held in-person meetings at Tetraphase's offices with Angotti and Raffi Asadorian (AcelRx CFO).

On February 7, Tetraphase re-engaged Janney and re-engaged discussions with AcelRx. On February 10, AcelRx delivered an all-stock proposal for $20–$25 million fully-diluted equity value (including Black-Scholes Put Provisions), subject to a $15 million net cash closing condition. On February 11, the Board discussed the proposal and AcelRx's request for exclusivity. On February 13, AcelRx revised its proposal to $13–$18 million fully-diluted equity value with an $8 million net cash closing condition and requested exclusivity. Janney simultaneously contacted twelve third parties; only Company K expressed interest.

On February 28, Company O (Company K's sole stockholder) submitted a non-binding proposal for $10 million cash plus CVRs of up to $5 million; AcelRx submitted a revised proposal (0.748 exchange ratio, approximately $17 million fully diluted including Black-Scholes). On March 1, the Board formed the M&A Committee (Gage, Bohlin, Boyd, Freund, Maher).

By March 4, Companies K and O verbally revised to $15 million cash (no CVR). On March 6, Janney informed AcelRx of the competing cash bid and asked both parties to submit best and final proposals by 5:00 p.m. On March 7, AcelRx's financial advisor orally submitted an updated proposal: 0.791 exchange ratio (~$20 million fully-diluted upfront value including Black-Scholes, with a $5 million net cash closing condition) plus a CVR of up to $12.5 million payable in cash or AcelRx stock. Company O's final written proposal was $15 million cash plus CVRs of up to $2.5 million (based on $20 million in XERAVA net sales by 2021). Company O stated this was "best and final" and it had several more weeks of diligence to complete.

On March 7, the M&A Committee met with Janney and WilmerHale; the Committee selected AcelRx over Company O, noting Company O's unwillingness to increase its offer and the risk of AcelRx withdrawal without exclusivity. AcelRx was granted exclusivity through March 11. On March 8, Janney communicated the decision to Company K and Company O. The parties negotiated the AcelRx merger agreement, CVR agreement, and voting agreements through March 15.

On March 11, Steve Boyd (Armistice Capital) agreed to a fixed exchange ratio for Armistice warrants in connection with the merger. On March 13, Stahl contacted all other warrant holders (five total with Black-Scholes Put Provisions); after market close on March 13, four agreed to enter voting agreements and fix exchange ratios; the fifth (which did not own common stock) agreed to an exchange agreement.

On March 15, 2020, the AcelRx Board unanimously approved the transactions. The Tetraphase Board also met on March 15, with Janney and WilmerHale; Janney delivered an oral fairness opinion (later confirmed in writing) that the Merger Consideration was fair from a financial point of view to Tetraphase common stockholders. The Tetraphase Board unanimously approved the merger agreement. The merger agreement was executed on March 16, 2020. The exchange ratio was 0.6303 AcelRx shares per TTPH share, reflecting an approximately $14.4 million fully-diluted upfront equity value on the execution date (AcelRx stock had declined from $1.43 at time of proposal to $1.03 at signing due to COVID-related market volatility).

### May 2020: La Jolla Proposal and AcelRx Bidding War

From March 26 through June 4, 2020, Tetraphase and AcelRx conducted activities in preparation for the special stockholder meeting scheduled for June 8, 2020, including filing a Form S-4 registration statement and preparing the proxy statement.

On May 1, 2020, Kevin Tang, Chairman of La Jolla Pharmaceuticals Company, contacted Edwards via email requesting a discussion. Edwards informed AcelRx's CFO Asadorian. Edwards and Tang had a general discussion on May 1 based on publicly available information. Tang attempted to contact Edwards multiple times thereafter. On May 5, Edwards called Tang and stated he could not speak further.

On May 6, AcelRx's outside counsel Cooley LLP sent a letter to Tetraphase alleging that Edwards's May 1 conversation with Tang constituted a material breach of the non-solicitation provisions of the AcelRx merger agreement. On the same day, La Jolla delivered an unsolicited proposal to Tetraphase for $22 million in cash plus $12.5 million in CVRs and also publicly filed the proposal on a Form 8-K. Tetraphase informed AcelRx in accordance with the merger agreement terms. Cooley sent a second letter reiterating its breach allegation. WilmerHale responded on behalf of Tetraphase denying any breach. On May 27, AcelRx CEO Angotti irrevocably waived all rights and remedies arising from the May 6 correspondence.

On May 7, the Tetraphase Board determined the La Jolla Proposal could reasonably be expected to lead to a Superior Offer. Tetraphase and La Jolla executed an Acceptable Confidentiality Agreement (no standstill) on May 8, and La Jolla was given data room access. La Jolla representatives (including Kevin Tang) and representatives of Janney held telephonic diligence sessions on May 8, 10, and 11. On May 12, La Jolla delivered an amended proposal of $22.3 million cash + $12.5 million CVRs with a draft merger agreement (tender offer structure), and the Board again found it could reasonably be expected to lead to a Superior Offer.

On May 14, Melinta (emerging from Chapter 11 bankruptcy with Deerfield as sole stockholder) delivered an unsolicited proposal for approximately $27.0 million cash + $12.5 million CVRs. The Board found it could reasonably be expected to lead to a Superior Offer and provided data room access. Paul Hastings LLP represented Melinta; WilmerHale represented Tetraphase. Deerfield management also communicated the Melinta proposal orally.

From May 15–22, Deerfield and Melinta conducted due diligence, holding calls on May 18, 20, and 22. On May 19, Paul Hastings sent revised transaction documents. WilmerHale and Paul Hastings negotiated merger agreement terms from May 19–21, including the terms of a Deerfield guarantee of Melinta's obligations.

On May 21, Melinta submitted the Amended Melinta Proposal: $1.21/share upfront (approximately $27.0 million aggregate), subject to a $3.0 million closing net cash condition, plus $12.5 million CVRs. At the May 21 Board meeting, Janney reviewed the proposal's financial aspects (including warrant treatment); the Board determined it was a Superior Offer. Tetraphase issued a Determination Notice to AcelRx; AcelRx had until May 29 to propose revisions.

On May 24, Angotti and Edwards discussed the Board's determination. On May 25, AcelRx's financial advisor contacted Janney and stated the AcelRx Board was meeting; overnight on May 25 AcelRx submitted a revised proposal for approximately $30.0 million upfront (AcelRx stock and cash) plus $14.5 million CVRs. The AcelRx proposal prohibited disclosure to third parties or the public without AcelRx's prior consent.

On May 26, the M&A Committee consulted with Janney and WilmerHale; AcelRx's financial advisor stated AcelRx would permit Tetraphase to confidentially discuss the proposal with warrantholders. Three of five Black-Scholes warrant holders declined to amend their voting or exchange agreements, preferring the certainty of the cash Melinta Proposal. Later that evening, the Board met twice; at the second meeting the Board determined that the revised AcelRx proposal caused the Amended Melinta Proposal to cease being a Superior Offer and approved the first amendment to the AcelRx merger agreement. AcelRx agreed not to require a purchase price adjustment regarding uncooperating warrantholders, and agreed that further Melinta or La Jolla proposals after the amendment date would not be disregarded. The termination fee payable by Tetraphase was increased from $810,000 to $1,441,000. On May 27, the first AcelRx merger agreement amendment was executed.

On May 27, Melinta immediately delivered the Second Amended Melinta Proposal: $34.0 million cash ($1.79/share, no closing net cash condition) plus $16.0 million CVRs. The Board determined this was a Superior Offer; AcelRx had until June 1 to match.

On May 28, Edwards took a call from Kevin Tang of La Jolla; they discussed due diligence matters. That same evening AcelRx submitted another revised proposal: approximately $38.0 million upfront ($0.5872 cash + 0.7409 AcelRx shares per TTPH share; based on AcelRx's May 27 closing price) plus $16.0 million CVRs, subject to a $5 million closing net cash condition. The Board that evening determined that this revised AcelRx proposal caused the Second Amended Melinta Proposal to cease being a Superior Offer and approved a second amendment to the AcelRx merger agreement. The second amendment was executed on May 29.

On May 29, La Jolla requested a due diligence call regarding regulatory matters, which was held. On May 29, Melinta also indicated it had reviewed the press release and had not yet determined whether to submit another proposal.

On May 31, Melinta submitted the Third Amended Melinta Proposal: approximately $39.0 million cash ($1.79/share, no closing net cash condition) plus $16.0 million CVRs. On June 1, the Board determined it was a Superior Offer; AcelRx had until June 3 to match.

On June 3, AcelRx publicly announced it would not further revise its offer. The Board met that evening, received Janney's oral fairness opinion on the Melinta Offer Price, and instructed management to finalize documentation with Melinta. On June 3–4, Paul Hastings and WilmerHale finalized the Melinta Merger Agreement (Tetraphase rejected Melinta's request for a $1,778,000 termination fee but ultimately the agreed fee was $1,778,000). On June 4, the Board unanimously approved the AcelRx merger agreement termination and the Melinta merger agreement. Tetraphase delivered notice of AcelRx merger agreement termination and paid AcelRx a termination fee of $1,778,000. The Melinta Merger Agreement was executed. Melinta (Toronto Transaction Corp., Inc. as Purchaser) commenced the tender offer on June 12, 2020 at $1.79/share cash plus CVRs of up to $16.0 million.

### June 2020: La Jolla's Winning Proposal

On June 10, 2020, La Jolla began open-market purchases of Tetraphase common stock. On June 19, 2020, La Jolla delivered the June La Jolla Proposal to the Tetraphase Board: $43.0 million upfront cash plus $16.0 million CVRs, structured as (i) $2.00/share for common stock (including RSUs, PRSUs, and pre-funded warrants), (ii) $2.68/share for November 2019 warrants, and (iii) $2.69/share for January 2020 warrants. Draft transaction documents were included. The Tetraphase Board met on June 20 to evaluate the proposal; Janney reviewed financial aspects. The Board determined the proposal could reasonably be expected to lead to a Superior Offer. Tetraphase provided notice to Melinta and began negotiations. La Jolla's counsel Gibson, Dunn & Crutcher LLP and WilmerHale exchanged drafts of the merger agreement from June 20–21.

On June 21, the Board determined the June La Jolla Proposal was a Superior Offer; Melinta given until June 26 to propose revisions. On June 22, La Jolla filed a Schedule 13D disclosing approximately 14.9% beneficial ownership of Tetraphase common stock through open-market purchases beginning June 10. Melinta waived its four business day match rights later that day, confirming it would not propose any revisions. The Tetraphase Board was updated on Melinta's response; Janney contacted Deerfield and Melinta representatives to confirm they would not propose revisions.

WilmerHale and Melinta's counsel finalized the Melinta Letter Agreement reflecting Melinta's waiver of match rights on June 22–23. During June 22–23, Tetraphase management contacted all Black-Scholes warrant holders and finalized documentation regarding warrant treatment in the La Jolla transactions.

In the evening of June 23, 2020, the Tetraphase Board met telephonically; Janney delivered an oral fairness opinion that the La Jolla Offer Price was fair from a financial point of view to Tetraphase common stockholders. The Board unanimously approved termination of the Melinta Merger Agreement and approval of the La Jolla Merger Agreement. On June 24, 2020, the La Jolla Merger Agreement was executed; Tetraphase paid Melinta a termination fee of $1,150,000. Melinta filed a final Schedule TO amendment terminating the Melinta Offer (no shares were purchased under the Melinta Offer). La Jolla commenced the tender offer on June 29, 2020. On July 28, 2020, La Jolla completed the acquisition. Tetraphase was delisted from Nasdaq the same day.

## Rationale

The Tetraphase Board's stated rationale for accepting the La Jolla Offer (as disclosed in the second SC 14D9, accession 0000950123-20-006482) was that the June La Jolla Proposal was a Superior Offer as defined under the Melinta Merger Agreement. The Board cited the following considerations as disclosed in the filing:

First, the upfront consideration from La Jolla ($2.00/share cash) was all-cash, providing certainty and immediate value to Tetraphase stockholders compared to alternatives that included stock consideration. Second, the La Jolla Proposal, at approximately $43.0 million upfront, represented a substantially higher value than the Melinta Offer ($1.79/share upfront, approximately $39.0 million aggregate as of the Third Amended Melinta Proposal) and the prior AcelRx merger agreement (which, by the time of its termination, offered approximately $38.0 million upfront but partly in AcelRx stock whose price had been declining and volatile). Third, La Jolla had already acquired approximately 14.9% of Tetraphase common stock through open-market purchases, demonstrating commitment to the transaction. Fourth, Melinta waived its match rights and confirmed it would not propose revisions to match the La Jolla Proposal, and Melinta agreed to a termination fee of $1,150,000.

For the earlier Melinta Offer recommendation (first SC 14D9, accession 0001193125-20-167749), the Board cited: (i) the upfront consideration in cash providing certainty compared to AcelRx stock-and-cash consideration with volatile AcelRx stock; (ii) an approximate 16.0% premium to the implied AcelRx merger consideration based on AcelRx's June 3, 2020 closing price; (iii) extensive competitive negotiations with AcelRx having produced what the Board believed was AcelRx's highest price; (iv) the inability to obtain superior financing and the risks of ongoing standalone operations, including continuing cash burn and the risk of bankruptcy; (v) XERAVA's commercial uncertainties and the resources required to commercialize; and (vi) Janney's fairness opinion dated June 3, 2020.

The Board also disclosed negative factors: (i) Tetraphase would cease to exist as an independent entity; (ii) stockholders would receive only the CVR as upside and CVR payments depend on achieving net sales milestones that may not be reached; and (iii) the termination fee of $1,778,000 (for the AcelRx termination) limited Tetraphase's flexibility.
