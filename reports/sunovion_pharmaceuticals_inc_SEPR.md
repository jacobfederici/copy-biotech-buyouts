## Summary

Sepracor Inc. entered into a definitive merger agreement with Dainippon Sumitomo Pharma Co., Ltd. (DSP) under which DSP's subsidiary, Aptiom, Inc., acquired all outstanding shares of Sepracor for $23.00 per share in cash. The transaction was structured as a two-step tender offer followed by a short-form merger, providing DSP with a fully integrated commercial platform in the United States.

## Interaction History

Negotiations began in late 2008 with several parties (Companies A, B, C, D, and E) and a private equity consortium involving Chairman Timothy Barberich. DSP entered the process in May 2009 via its advisor Thomas Weisel Partners. DSP initially proposed $21.50 in June 2009. Meanwhile, the PE consortium's interest cooled, with their valuation dropping to near the $15.00 market price by July. Company C proposed a merger of equals with no premium, which the Board found less attractive than DSP's cash offer. DSP's offer was negotiated upward from $21.50 to $22.25 on August 10, and finally to $23.00 on August 14 after Sepracor's Special Committee, led by Robert Cresci and advised by J.P. Morgan and Jefferies, pushed for a higher premium reflecting strong Q2 results and synergy value. Exclusivity was granted to DSP on August 17. Final terms were settled by September 2, including a 3% termination fee ($77.4M).

## Rationale

The Sepracor Board determined the cash offer provided certain value at a significant premium (27.6% over the last trading day) compared to the risks of remaining independent, including impending generic competition and clinical/regulatory hurdles. For DSP, the acquisition provided a fully integrated U.S. commercial platform to leverage its global business.

## Buyout Rumors

Source: OpenRouter web search (Gemini 3 Flash Preview). Pre-deal rumors only.

Sepracor Inc. (SEPR) was a frequent subject of takeover speculation for several years prior to its eventual acquisition by Dainippon Sumitomo Pharma (DSP) in September 2009. Analysts often cited the company’s established U.S. sales force and its blockbuster insomnia drug, Lunesta, as primary attractions for larger pharmaceutical firms.

The following is a chronological timeline of reported rumors and speculation regarding a potential acquisition of Sepracor Inc. before the confirmed deal announcement on September 3, 2009.

*   **October 4, 2004**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *BioCentury*
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** General speculation (Big Pharma)
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Not reported
    *   **Details:** Reported that investors viewed Sepracor as a "prime example" of a mid-cap company with a late-stage product (the insomnia drug then known as Estorra/eszopiclone) that made it an attractive target for larger firms looking to move the needle on earnings.

*   **Early 2007 – Late 2008**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** Various (Analyst reports from firms such as Piper Jaffray and Credit Suisse)
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** GlaxoSmithKline, Pfizer, AstraZeneca, Sanofi-Aventis
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Not reported
    *   **Details:** During this period, Sepracor was repeatedly named in "Top Takeover Target" lists by industry analysts. Speculation was driven by the "patent cliff" facing major pharmaceutical companies and Sepracor's 1,200-person U.S. sales force, which was seen as a "plug-and-play" asset for foreign firms or domestic majors with new CNS (Central Nervous System) drugs.

*   **August 2009**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *The Wall Street Journal*, *Reuters*, and *Bloomberg* (citing market activity)
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Unnamed Japanese pharmaceutical companies
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Not reported
    *   **Details:** In the weeks immediately preceding the deal, market speculation intensified regarding a Japanese suitor. Reports noted that Japanese drugmakers (such as Takeda and Eisai) had recently completed multi-billion dollar U.S. acquisitions, and Sepracor’s stock price began to experience volatility and upward pressure based on rumors that a deal was imminent.

*   **September 1, 2009**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *BNET* (CBS MoneyWatch) and various market wire services
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Dainippon Sumitomo Pharma (DSP)
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Speculation of a "significant premium" over the then-trading price of ~$17–$18.
    *   **Details:** Specific rumors naming Dainippon Sumitomo surfaced just 48 hours before the official announcement. Analysts noted that DSP needed a U.S. platform to launch its schizophrenia drug, lurasidone, and that Sepracor was the most logical fit. This speculation was confirmed on September 3, 2009, when the companies announced a definitive agreement for $23.00 per share.
