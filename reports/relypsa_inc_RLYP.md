## Summary

Relypsa, Inc. entered into a definitive merger agreement with Galenica AG on July 20, 2016, under which Galenica's subsidiary, Vifor Pharma USA Inc., launched a tender offer to acquire all outstanding shares of Relypsa for $32.00 per share in cash. The transaction, valued at approximately $1.53 billion on a fully diluted basis, was structured as a two-step acquisition under Section 251(h) of the DGCL. The deal followed a competitive process where Relypsa engaged with multiple potential buyers after rumors of a sale circulated in the press. The acquisition was completed on September 1, 2016, following the successful tender of approximately 85.6% of Relypsa's shares.

## Interaction History

Negotiations began in earnest in April 2016 following press rumors of a sale. Galenica, an existing partner through its VFMCRP joint venture, expressed interest on April 8, 2016. Relypsa CEO John Orwin met with Galenica/VFMCRP executives Stefan Schulze and Christoph Springer in mid-April to discuss strategic rationale. On May 18, 2016, Galenica offered $29.00 per share. The Relypsa Board, advised by Centerview and Latham & Watkins, delayed a response until after the PDUFA date for competitor drug ZS-9. Following a CRL for ZS-9 on May 27, Relypsa reached out to other potential buyers (Party A, B, and C). Party B declined due to earnings dilution; Party C declined without specific reason. Party A conducted diligence but ultimately withdrew on July 5, citing strategic priorities. Galenica raised its offer to $30.25 on June 14. Relypsa countered, suggesting $34.00. Galenica proposed a lower upfront price with a CVR, which Relypsa rejected. On June 23, Galenica made its 'best and final' offer of $32.00. Relypsa Board member John Butler recused himself from deliberations due to potential conflicts. After finalizing the merger agreement terms, including a $49 million termination fee, the Board approved the $32.00 deal on July 20, 2016.

## Rationale

The Relypsa Board recommended the deal based on the significant premium (59% over the unaffected price), the certainty of cash value, and the risks of remaining independent, including the need for substantial future capital raises and competition from AstraZeneca's ZS-9. For Galenica, the acquisition was strategically motivated by the upcoming separation of Vifor Pharma and the desire to establish a direct commercial presence in the U.S. by leveraging Relypsa's nephrology and cardiology sales force to promote Vifor's existing product portfolio.

## Decision Notes

The deal value of $1.53B is based on the fully diluted share count including options and RSUs as analyzed by Centerview. Shares outstanding (44,882,597) taken from Item 1 of the 14D-9. The transaction did not include a CVR despite Galenica's mid-negotiation suggestion. Completion was achieved via Section 251(h), bypassing a shareholder vote.

## Buyout Rumors

Source: OpenRouter web search (Gemini 3 Flash Preview). Pre-deal rumors only.

Before the official announcement on July 21, 2016, that Galenica AG (Vifor Pharma) would acquire Relypsa, Inc. for $32 per share, the company was the subject of persistent takeover speculation. The following is a chronological timeline of reported rumors and speculation:

*   **December 4, 2015**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *StreetInsider* / *Warrior Trading News*
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Merck & Co. (MRK)
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Not specified, but the stock jumped 25% (from ~$22 to ~$27) within 15 minutes of the report.
    *   **Details:** Reports surfaced that Merck was planning a bid for Relypsa. This speculation followed AstraZeneca’s $2.7 billion acquisition of Relypsa’s primary competitor, ZS Pharma, in November 2015. Both companies declined to comment.

*   **April 7, 2016**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *TheStreet* / *Reuters*
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Multiple "unnamed potential buyers" (later identified by *Reuters* as including companies that lost the bidding war for ZS Pharma, such as Actelion).
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Not specified; shares rose 48% on the news.
    *   **Details:** Reported that Relypsa was exploring a sale and "fielding takeout offers" from several parties. The company was said to be working with investment bank Centerview Partners to review these offers.

*   **April 8, 2016**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *Reuters*
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Actelion Pharmaceuticals and other unsuccessful bidders for ZS Pharma.
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Not specified.
    *   **Details:** *Reuters* confirmed that Relypsa was working with Centerview Partners to evaluate interest from multiple pharmaceutical companies. The report noted that discussions were in early stages and might not lead to a deal.

*   **April 22, 2016**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *BioSpace* (citing resurfaced rumors)
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Actelion, Sanofi, or AstraZeneca.
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Not specified; stock saw a "small hop" as rumors resurfaced.
    *   **Details:** Speculation intensified that AstraZeneca might bid for Relypsa to monopolize the hyperkalemia market, or that Sanofi (Relypsa’s existing commercial partner) or Actelion (which had bid $2.5 billion for ZS Pharma) would step in.

*   **May – June 2016**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** Various (General Market Speculation)
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Actelion, Sanofi, Pfizer, and Biogen.
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Analysts at the time suggested a potential takeout price in the range of $40–$50 per share, though no specific bid at that level was ever confirmed.
    *   **Details:** Speculation continued throughout the late spring, particularly after the FDA issued a Complete Response Letter (rejection) to AstraZeneca’s ZS-9 in late May, which theoretically increased the value of Relypsa’s already-approved drug, Veltassa.
