## Summary

In May 2018, Eli Lilly and Company, through its subsidiary Bluegill Acquisition Corporation, successfully completed a tender offer to acquire ARMO BioSciences, Inc. for $50.00 per share in cash. The acquisition was driven by Lilly's interest in ARMO's lead product candidate, pegilodecakin (AM0010), an immuno-oncology asset. The deal followed a competitive process involving outreach and negotiations with several large pharmaceutical companies, culminating in Lilly's successful bid and the subsequent completion of the tender offer and short-form merger.

## Interaction History

In the first quarter of 2018, ARMO BioSciences met with various third parties at a healthcare conference to explore strategic opportunities. This led to an initial non-disclosure agreement (NDA) with a large pharmaceutical company ("Company A") on January 29, 2018.

On March 9, 2018, Company A provided a verbal, non-binding indication of interest to acquire ARMO for $60.00 per share in cash. The ARMO Board convened, directed management to seek an increased written offer, and engaged Centerview Partners as its financial advisor. Concurrently, ARMO held preliminary discussions with other interested parties, executing NDAs with "Company B" on March 14, Eli Lilly (Parent) on March 19, and "Company C" on March 26.

On March 23, 2018, Company A submitted a written proposal to acquire ARMO for $60.00 to $65.00 per share in cash, contingent on a 30-day exclusivity period. The ARMO Board declined to grant exclusivity, prioritizing a competitive process. Company A initially agreed to proceed without exclusivity. Meanwhile, Eli Lilly and ARMO management held multiple due diligence calls.

In April 2018, the competitive landscape shifted. Company B and Company C both withdrew from the process after conducting due diligence. On April 13, Eli Lilly delivered an initial written offer of $55.00 per share in cash with no financing contingencies. Following the release of positive clinical data from a competitor on April 16, which impacted the market perception of ARMO's lead indication, Company A also withdrew its interest on April 23.

On May 4, Eli Lilly informed ARMO that its offer price would be reduced below $55.00, citing its due diligence and development plans. On May 7, Eli Lilly officially lowered its offer to $48.00 per share. The ARMO Board met, reviewed the offer against updated financial projections that accounted for the new competitive data and challenging capital markets, and instructed management to negotiate a higher price.

On May 8, ARMO's CEO urged Eli Lilly to improve the offer, resulting in a revised bid of $50.00 per share. The ARMO Board met the next day to review the $50.00 offer. After considering the lack of other bidders, the competitive risks, and receiving a fairness opinion from Centerview Partners, the ARMO Board unanimously approved the Merger Agreement on May 9, 2018.

## Rationale

The ARMO Board concluded that the $50.00 per share cash offer represented the most attractive alternative for maximizing stockholder value. This decision was influenced by the competitive threat posed by recently announced positive clinical data from a rival pharmaceutical company in the non-small cell lung cancer indication, which was a key value driver for ARMO's lead asset, AM0010. Furthermore, the Board noted that capital raises necessary to fund larger registrational studies were likely no longer available on acceptable terms given the recent decline in ARMO's stock price. The lack of remaining viable competing bidders after an extensive market check further underscored the value and certainty of the Eli Lilly transaction.
