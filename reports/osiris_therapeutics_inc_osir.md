## Summary

Osiris Therapeutics, Inc., a Maryland corporation and regenerative medicine company focused on wound care and orthopedic products based on placental tissue technology, was acquired by Smith & Nephew plc through a cash tender offer at $19.00 per share. Smith & Nephew Consolidated, Inc. (Parent), an indirect wholly owned subsidiary of Smith & Nephew plc, and its Maryland subsidiary Papyrus Acquisition Corp. (Purchaser) conducted the tender offer beginning March 20, 2019. The offer was initiated following a period of bilateral commercial discussions that began in mid-2018, escalated to full-company acquisition interest in December 2018, and culminated in a signed Merger Agreement on March 12, 2019. The offer price was negotiated upward from an initial $17.50 per share to $18.00, then $18.75, and finally $19.00 per share on the day the Board approved the transaction. As of the April 17, 2019 expiration, 21,079,693 shares (approximately 61% of outstanding shares) were validly tendered; all conditions were satisfied, Purchaser accepted the shares, and the merger was consummated the same day without a stockholder vote under MGCL Section 3-106.1. Shares were delisted from Nasdaq on April 17, 2019. A separate bidder, identified as "Company A" in the filing, had proposed to acquire only the wound care business for $250–$300 million and was informed of the signed Merger Agreement on March 13, 2019.

## Interaction History

### Background and Initial Commercial Outreach (Mid-2018)

In mid-2018, a representative of an unidentified company ("Company A") contacted Peter Friedli, Chairman of the Board and co-founder of Osiris, to discuss a potential acquisition of the Company's wound care business, which the filing identifies as including the Grafix and Stravix product lines. Separately in mid-2018, a representative of Osiris reached out to three other wound care industry companies to discuss potential business collaborations or partnerships with respect to the wound care business; those three companies indicated they were not interested. On May 30, 2018, Osiris entered into a mutual non-disclosure agreement with Company A.

On June 20, 2018, representatives of Osiris met with Company A at Company A's headquarters to discuss a potential acquisition of the wound care business.

### Initiation of Smith & Nephew Discussions (July–August 2018)

On July 9, 2018, Michael Sinclair, Director, Pipeline Strategy of Osiris, emailed the President, U.S. of Smith & Nephew to discuss whether Smith & Nephew had interest in a commercial collaboration or partnership regarding Osiris's wound care business. On July 12, 2018, the President, U.S. of Smith & Nephew responded indicating interest in an initial discussion regarding a potential commercial arrangement; the Company sent a draft mutual NDA the same day. On July 24, 2018, Osiris and an affiliate of Smith & Nephew entered into the mutual NDA (effective July 12, 2018), permitting the sharing of confidential information in connection with exploration of a potential commercial arrangement.

In late July 2018, Sinclair and Jason Keefer, Interim CEO, held calls with the Vice President, Corporate Development of Smith & Nephew to coordinate an in-person meeting and discuss the agenda. On August 2, 2018, Company representatives met with Smith & Nephew representatives at Osiris's headquarters in Columbia, Maryland. Osiris provided an overview of the company, its technology, and its products; Smith & Nephew provided an overview of its wound care portfolio and salesforce. During August 2018, Smith & Nephew conducted due diligence on Osiris, submitting information requests with focus on the wound care business. Parties also began exploring a potential commercial arrangement.

In late August and early September 2018, the VP, Corporate Development of Smith & Nephew held telephonic follow-up conversations with Sinclair to pursue due diligence matters and confirm continued interest in a potential commercial arrangement.

### Smith & Nephew Signals Interest in Full Acquisition (September–December 2018)

On September 21, 2018, the VP, Corporate Development of Smith & Nephew confirmed with Sinclair that Smith & Nephew remained interested in continuing discussions after consulting its executive team. The VP also raised the possibility of separating the wound care business from Osiris's other products to facilitate a business partnership and suggested an in-person meeting later in 2018. In early to mid-October 2018, Sinclair and Keefer held calls with the VP, Corporate Development of Smith & Nephew to coordinate the proposed meeting.

On October 30, 2018, Osiris representatives met with Smith & Nephew representatives at Osiris's headquarters to discuss a potential strategic transaction with respect to the wound care business; Osiris presented its wound care overview. On November 14, 2018, the VP, Corporate Development of Smith & Nephew communicated to Sinclair and Keefer that Smith & Nephew was interested in exploring an acquisition of the wound care business and was continuing to evaluate the Company's other products.

On November 20, 2018, Sinclair and Keefer met telephonically with Phil Cowdy, Executive Vice President, Corporate Development of Smith & Nephew, and the VP, Corporate Development. Cowdy informed the Company that Smith & Nephew had engaged Lazard Freres & Co. LLC as its financial advisor, that Smith & Nephew wanted to proceed with discussions regarding an acquisition of the wound care business, and that it sought a meeting with Friedli. Smith & Nephew was still evaluating Osiris's other products at this time.

On November 26, 2018, Osiris announced the appointment of Samson Tom as President and CEO. On November 27, 2018, Osiris met with Company A at its headquarters to continue discussions of a potential acquisition of the wound care business.

On December 6, 2018, Tom and Sinclair spoke telephonically with the VP, Corporate Development of Smith & Nephew and a Lazard representative. The VP informed Osiris that Smith & Nephew was now interested in acquiring all of the outstanding stock of the Company, not just the wound care business. Smith & Nephew requested updated financial information from the Company. On December 10, 2018, the Company provided Smith & Nephew with updated financial statements for the whole Company.

Meanwhile, on December 13, 2018, Company A met with Friedli in Zurich and proposed to acquire Osiris's wound care business for $250–$300 million. On December 14, 2018, Friedli met with Cowdy in Zurich to discuss a potential strategic transaction involving the whole Company. They agreed that Osiris would provide Smith & Nephew with updated financial statements and projections in January 2019 and schedule an in-person meeting for late January 2019. On December 18, 2018, Tom and Sinclair held a call with Cowdy, the VP, Corporate Development, and David Houldridge, Associate, Strategy and Business Development of Smith & Nephew, to discuss next steps. On December 27, 2018, Company A provided a due diligence request list; on December 28, the Company opened a virtual data room for Company A.

### Parallel Due Diligence Tracks and Competing Proposal (January 2019)

In mid-January 2019, the Company responded to various due diligence requests from Smith & Nephew. On January 10, 2019, Company A met with Osiris representatives at Company headquarters for a due diligence site visit focused on manufacturing, operations, procurement, regulatory, and quality. On January 18, 2019, Company A met with Osiris representatives near Company headquarters focused on finance and sales.

On January 22, 2019, the Company opened a virtual data room to share confidential information with Smith & Nephew and began uploading materials in advance of the planned in-person meeting. On January 25, 2019, Smith & Nephew representatives met at Company headquarters to discuss cellular-tissue products, operations, and financial statements. On January 30, 2019, Cowdy called Friedli to confirm Smith & Nephew's continued interest and intent to move forward with due diligence.

On February 1, 2019, Company A met with Tom and Friedli in person in New York City and proposed to acquire the wound care business for $270–$290 million. The Company's representatives responded that the Company would consider the proposal.

### Smith & Nephew's Initial Offer and Board Deliberations (February 2019)

On February 12, 2019, Cowdy met with Friedli in Zurich and presented a written non-binding indication of interest to acquire all outstanding Osiris stock for $17.50 per share in cash, subject to completion of due diligence and execution of mutually acceptable support agreements with certain large stockholders. Friedli told Cowdy he would take it to the Board.

On February 14, 2019, Friedli told Cowdy he had discussed the proposal with Tom and Hogan Lovells and was scheduling a Board meeting. In mid-February 2019, Cowdy and Friedli discussed the possibility of a post-closing consulting arrangement for Friedli, and agreed to defer further discussion until after a transaction was announced.

On February 19, 2019, the Board held a telephonic special meeting with a Hogan Lovells representative present. Friedli described his recent conversations with Smith & Nephew and the $17.50 per share offer. The Board discussed the premium the $17.50 price represented relative to recent trading prices, the Company's prospects as a stand-alone business, challenges it faced in the marketplace due to competitive dynamics and its size relative to competitors, and marketplace trends. Hogan Lovells provided an overview of Board duties under Maryland law. The Board authorized Friedli to continue discussions with Smith & Nephew and to engage an investment bank to render a fairness opinion.

Also on February 19, 2019, Tom and Sinclair told Company A by telephone that the Company needed additional time to evaluate Company A's proposal.

### Price Negotiation and Exclusivity (February–March 2019)

On February 20, 2019, Friedli provided a counter-proposal to Cowdy of $18.50 per share. Cowdy said Smith & Nephew needed additional time to consider. On February 22, 2019, Cowdy told Friedli that $18.00 per share was reflective of Smith & Nephew's valuation. On February 25, 2019, Cowdy reconfirmed the $18.00 position. On February 26, 2019, the Board met telephonically with management and Hogan Lovells; Friedli summarized the negotiations and expressed his view that $18.00 was the highest price Smith & Nephew would pay. Friedli reported he had engaged Cantor Fitzgerald & Co. to render a fairness opinion.

On February 27, 2019, the Company provided Smith & Nephew with a draft 10-K and fourth quarter and year-end earnings release. Also on February 27, 2019, Hogan Lovells spoke with Paul Weiss, Smith & Nephew's counsel, regarding Board duties under Maryland law and the Company's proposal for a "go shop" provision. Smith & Nephew, through Paul Weiss, responded on February 28, 2019 that it would not agree to a go shop but would agree to a termination fee toward the lower end of or slightly below the 3%–4% customary range.

On March 1, 2019, Cowdy transmitted to Friedli the revised March 1 Letter, which included an offer price of $18.00 per share in cash, no financing contingency, an exclusive negotiating period through March 12, 2019 (with one automatic two-week extension if making progress), and customary nine-month standstill provisions. The same day, Hogan Lovells confirmed the Company would drop the go shop request, and the parties agreed in principle to a termination fee equal to 2.8% of equity value. The Board met telephonically, received Friedli's update, and authorized Friedli to execute the March 1 Letter. The Company countersigned the March 1 Letter on March 2, 2019.

### Formal Negotiation and Increased Offer (March 2019)

From March 1 through March 11, 2019, Smith & Nephew continued its due diligence. The Company provided additional diligence materials, and representatives held frequent calls.

On March 5, 2019, Paul Weiss sent an initial draft Merger Agreement to Hogan Lovells, providing for a two-step tender offer and merger structure with customary no-shop and fiduciary out provisions and a 2.8% termination fee on a fully diluted basis. Also on March 5, 2019, Friedli met with Simon Fraser, President, Advanced Wound Management of Smith & Nephew, and Cowdy in London. Friedli requested an increase to $18.50 per share. Smith & Nephew also conducted due diligence at Company headquarters on compliance and tax. On the same day, Cantor Fitzgerald and Lazard held a conference call on transaction matters.

On March 6, 2019, Smith & Nephew again visited Company headquarters for further due diligence. Certain Board members met telephonically with management and Hogan Lovells; Cantor Fitzgerald was also present and described its fairness opinion process. On March 7, 2019, Smith & Nephew again visited Company headquarters. Hogan Lovells sent a revised draft Merger Agreement to Paul Weiss. Paul Weiss sent an initial draft Tender and Support Agreement to Hogan Lovells.

On March 8, 2019, Friedli, Nawana (CEO of Smith & Nephew), and Cowdy held telephonic discussions on the offer price. Nawana reiterated Smith & Nephew's interest and indicated Smith & Nephew would consider increasing the price in light of, among other things, recent increases in the Company's trading price. Friedli electronically transmitted a request that Smith & Nephew increase to $18.75 per share, noting the increased stock price since Smith & Nephew's $18.00 offer. On March 9, 2019, Cowdy indicated Smith & Nephew would agree to $18.75 per share.

On March 10, 2019, Paul Weiss sent a revised draft Merger Agreement. On March 11, 2019, the Board met telephonically with management, Hogan Lovells, and Cantor Fitzgerald. Friedli updated the Board on Smith & Nephew's $18.75 per share offer. Cantor Fitzgerald made a financial presentation and verbally rendered a fairness opinion at $18.75. Hogan Lovells summarized the key provisions of the Merger Agreement, the Tender and Support Agreement, and the timeline for closing. Tom provided an update on due diligence. After discussion and consideration, the Board unanimously approved the Merger Agreement and resolved to recommend the Offer.

Following the conclusion of the March 11 Board meeting, Friedli informed Cowdy by telephone that the Board had approved the transaction but that the Company was continuing to monitor the stock price. During the day of March 11, 2019, Friedli had calls with Hogan Lovells regarding an acceptable offer price considering the equity market's valuation of the Company based on current trading prices. Later on March 11, 2019, Friedli proposed $19.00 per share to Cowdy, which the Board believed was necessary to provide value in excess of the then-current public equity market valuation based on the March 11 closing price. Following further negotiations, Nawana and Cowdy confirmed Smith & Nephew would increase the offer price to $19.00 per share. Cantor Fitzgerald updated its analysis and delivered a written fairness opinion dated March 12, 2019 at $19.00 per share.

In the morning of March 12, 2019, the parties agreed on the final Merger Agreement. Parent, Purchaser, Smith & Nephew, and the Company executed the Merger Agreement. Parent, Purchaser, and Friedli executed the Tender and Support Agreement. Before the opening of trading on Nasdaq, the Company issued a press release announcing the Merger Agreement and forthcoming tender offer.

### Post-Signing and Completion

On March 13, 2019, the Company advised Company A by telephone of the signed Merger Agreement and sent written notice requesting return or destruction of Company A's confidential materials under the non-disclosure agreement.

On March 17, 2019, Cowdy and Friedli agreed in principle on the terms of a two-year post-closing transitional consulting arrangement for Friedli at $250,000 per year. Other terms had not yet been negotiated as of the date of the SC 14D9.

On March 20, 2019, Purchaser commenced the Offer, and Osiris filed this SC 14D9.

On April 10, 2019, Gainey McKenna & Egleston filed a class action lawsuit against Osiris and its directors in connection with the Offer.

The Offer expired at 12:01 a.m. Eastern Time on April 17, 2019. Equiniti Trust Company, as depositary and paying agent, advised that 21,079,693 shares (not including 416,982 shares tendered by notice of guaranteed delivery) had been validly tendered and not withdrawn, representing approximately 61% of outstanding shares. All conditions were satisfied. Purchaser accepted the shares for payment on April 17, 2019. The merger was effected the same day, April 17, 2019, under MGCL Section 3-106.1 without a stockholder vote. Each outstanding share (other than shares owned by Purchaser or any Company subsidiary) was canceled and converted into the right to receive $19.00. The aggregate consideration paid in the Offer and the Merger was approximately $660 million (per 8-K). The Company was delisted from Nasdaq on April 17, 2019.

As a result of the merger, Peter Friedli, Thomas Knapp, Willi Miesch, and Charles Reinhart ceased to be directors. Michael Zagger was appointed President of the Company; Scott Schaffner was appointed Secretary and Treasurer. Samson Tom continued as CEO. Joel Rogers, Jason Keefer, Alla Danilkovitch, and James Black ceased to be officers.

### Tender and Support Agreement

Concurrently with execution of the Merger Agreement, Peter Friedli entered into a Tender and Support Agreement with Parent and Purchaser. Friedli irrevocably and unconditionally agreed to tender his shares and vote them in accordance with the agreement. As of March 19, 2019, Friedli directly owned 10,204,404 shares (approximately 30% of outstanding). Including shares held by New Venturetec AG (4,103,301 shares), Friedli's minor daughter (500,000 shares), and his spouse (2,750 shares), Friedli held voting power over 14,810,455 shares in total. Friedli disclaimed beneficial ownership in the shares held by his daughter, spouse, and New Venturetec AG beyond his pecuniary interest, and he indicated he did not have discretionary authority over the New Venturetec AG shares for purposes of tendering.

The Tender and Support Agreement terminates upon (x) valid termination of the Merger Agreement, (y) consummation of all transactions contemplated by the Merger Agreement, or (z) mutual written consent.

## Rationale

The SC 14D9 (accession 0001047469-19-001440, Item 4) states the following as material factors and benefits considered by the Board in approving the transaction:

The Board considered that the Offer Price of $19.00 per share represented a 37% premium to the Company's 90-day volume-weighted average stock price. The Board considered the certainty of value and immediate liquidity that the all-cash consideration provided to stockholders relative to market and long-term business risks related to the Company's future growth prospects. The Board noted that the transaction was not subject to a financing condition and that Parent had sufficient cash resources.

The Board considered the "current business environment and challenges that the Company faces, including the highly competitive and evolving field in which the Company operates and the difficulty in obtaining coverage and adequate reimbursement from public and private insurers and health systems for its products." The Board also considered competitive landscape challenges due to the Company's "size relative to its competitors, as well as trends in the marketplace."

The Board considered the potential benefits of exploring business combination transactions with alternative potential acquirors but determined that a broader outreach could delay a potential transaction, putting at risk a deal with Parent at the negotiated price and terms, and that any interested party would be able to pursue a transaction despite the Merger Agreement. The Board considered continuing as an independent company and ultimately determined that the Transaction was more favorable than that alternative.

The Board noted it believed it had obtained the highest price Parent was willing to pay, as evidenced by the increase from Parent's initial offer of $17.50 per share to $19.00, and that the negotiations were conducted at arm's length by the Chairman of the Board with the knowledge and direction of the Board and with the assistance of Hogan Lovells.

The Board considered the speed of completion available through the tender offer structure and the ability to close without a stockholder vote under MGCL Section 3-106.1, thereby reducing business disruption during the pendency period.

The Board also considered a fairness opinion from Cantor Fitzgerald & Co. dated March 12, 2019, concluding that, as of that date, the $19.00 per share cash consideration to be received by holders of Company Common Stock (other than the Specified Holders: Smith & Nephew, Parent, Purchaser, Friedli, and their respective affiliates) was fair from a financial point of view. Cantor Fitzgerald was paid $700,000 upon delivery of its opinion.

The Board identified negative factors including: the termination of stockholders' participation in any future earnings or growth; potential business disruption from announcement; non-solicitation covenant restrictions; the $18,682,450 termination fee payable under certain circumstances; the risk the transaction might not be completed; the absence of appraisal rights under Maryland law; regulatory approval requirements; and potential conflicts of interest from directors' and officers' financial interests in the transaction.

## Decision Notes

This packet is accepted as a completed acquisition. All required elements are present: buyer (Smith & Nephew plc and its subsidiary Smith & Nephew Consolidated, Inc.), target (Osiris Therapeutics, Inc.), transaction type (cash tender offer followed by short-form merger), and completion evidence (8-K filed 2019-04-17 under Item 2.01 reporting closing of the merger).
