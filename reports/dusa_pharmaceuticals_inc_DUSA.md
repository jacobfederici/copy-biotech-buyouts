## Summary

DUSA Pharmaceuticals, Inc. entered into a definitive merger agreement with Sun Pharmaceutical Industries Limited and its subsidiary Caraco Pharmaceutical Laboratories, Ltd., under which Sun Pharma acquired DUSA for $8.00 per share in an all-cash tender offer. The transaction followed a formal strategic review process initiated by DUSA in early 2012, which involved contacting over 30 potential partners and resulted in a competitive bidding process between Sun Pharma and other pharmaceutical entities.

## Interaction History

In early 2012, DUSA's CEO Robert Doman and Leerink Swann initiated a strategic review, contacting over 30 potential partners. Sun Pharma expressed interest in April 2012. By July, Sun Pharma, Company B, and Company F emerged as bidders. Sun Pharma initially offered $6.98/share, while Company B offered $7.50-$7.90 (with a financing contingency) and Company F offered $7.50-$8.50. Negotiations continued through August and September, with Sun Pharma increasing its bid to $7.50 and then $7.70. Company F offered $7.60 for an accelerated close, which DUSA rejected to maintain the process. In October, Sun Pharma increased its bid to $8.00. Company B later proposed $8.30-$8.50 but lacked committed financing and had previously withdrawn from the process due to internal issues. DUSA's board, concerned about the certainty of Company B's offer and the risk of Sun Pharma withdrawing its $8.00 bid, decided to proceed with Sun Pharma. Final negotiations on the FTC divestiture cap and termination fees concluded on November 7, 2012.

## Rationale

The DUSA board recommended the deal because it provided immediate cash value at a 38% premium over the market price. They noted the risks of remaining independent, including heavy reliance on a single product (Levulan) in a competitive market and the need for significant capital to diversify the pipeline. Sun Pharma's offer was deemed superior due to the absence of a financing contingency compared to other bidders.
