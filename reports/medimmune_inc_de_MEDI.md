## Summary

AstraZeneca PLC acquired MedImmune, Inc. for $58.00 per share in an all-cash tender offer valued at approximately $15.6 billion. The acquisition was designed to accelerate AstraZeneca's biologics strategy by integrating MedImmune's research, development, and manufacturing capabilities with AstraZeneca's existing biologics business, including Cambridge Antibody Technology (CAT). The transaction closed following a tender offer in which approximately 96% of shares were tendered, followed by a short-form merger.

## Interaction History

In early 2007, MedImmune faced pressure from stockholders regarding stock performance, leading the Board to authorize a strategic review on March 7, 2007. Goldman Sachs contacted over 20 potential buyers. Eight companies, including AstraZeneca, signed confidentiality agreements and received management presentations. AstraZeneca's CEO David Brennan met with MedImmune CEO David Mott on March 29. AstraZeneca initially bid $50.00 per share on April 5. Following a European news leak on April 12, MedImmune confirmed the process publicly. After a second round of bidding and negotiations over retention terms for David Mott and James Young, AstraZeneca raised its offer to $58.00 per share on April 22. The MedImmune Board, advised by Goldman Sachs and Dewey Ballantine, unanimously approved the offer that same day, citing the significant premium and lack of financing contingency.

## Rationale

AstraZeneca sought to accelerate its biologics strategy and diversify its R&D base by adding vaccines and monoclonal antibodies. MedImmune's board recommended the deal because it provided a 53.3% premium over the unaffected stock price, addressed stockholder dissatisfaction with short-term performance, and offered certain value through an all-cash consideration without financing contingencies.

## Evidence

- On April 22, 2007, the Company Board... determined that the Merger Agreement and the transactions contemplated thereby, including the Offer and the Merger, are advisable, fair to and in the best interests of the Company’s stockholders (0001193125-07-103848).
- On June 18, 2007... Purchaser was merged with and into MedImmune (the 'Merger'), with MedImmune surviving the Merger as an indirect wholly owned subsidiary of Parent (0001104659-07-048358).

## Decision Notes

The deal value of $15.6 billion is explicitly stated in the press release and SC 14D9. Shares outstanding (238,141,500) were sourced from the SC 14D9 Item 1(b) as of April 30, 2007. The transaction was completed via a tender offer followed by a short-form merger on June 18, 2007.

## Buyout Rumors

Source: OpenRouter web search (Gemini 3 Flash Preview). Pre-deal rumors only.

Before the confirmed announcement of its acquisition by AstraZeneca on April 23, 2007, MedImmune, Inc. (MEDI) was the subject of significant takeover speculation and public pressure from activist investors.

The following is a chronological timeline of rumors and speculation regarding a potential buyout of MedImmune:

*   **December 13, 2006**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *FierceBiotech* / *Washington Business Journal*
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** Large pharmaceutical companies (specifically **Pfizer** was noted by analysts as a logical suitor due to its interest in the vaccine market).
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Not specified, though investor David Katz suggested a buyer would pay "as much or more than the company is currently worth."
    *   **Details:** David Katz of Matrix Asset Advisors publicly urged the MedImmune board to put the company up for sale to a large pharmaceutical firm to capitalize on the "hot market" for mergers. The board swiftly rejected the advice at that time, stating they believed in staying independent.

*   **February 2007**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *The Guardian* / SEC Filings
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** General speculation of a "corporate raider" target.
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** N/A
    *   **Details:** Billionaire investor **Carl Icahn** disclosed he had purchased 2.8 million shares (approximately 1% of the company). This disclosure immediately fueled speculation that he would push for a sale or a major restructuring, as was his typical strategy.

*   **April 12, 2007**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *The Street* / *Forbes* / *MarketWatch*
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** "Third parties" / Big Pharma.
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** N/A (Stock was trading at approximately $37.84).
    *   **Details:** MedImmune officially announced it had hired Goldman Sachs to "explore strategic alternatives," including a potential sale. This announcement was widely reported as a "bowing to pressure" from Carl Icahn and other disgruntled shareholders. While no specific buyer was named in the initial press release, the move effectively put the company "on the auction block."

*   **April 13–22, 2007**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *The Guardian* / *FierceBiotech* / *The New York Times*
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** **AstraZeneca**, **GlaxoSmithKline (GSK)**, and **Sanofi-Aventis**.
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** Analysts estimated a potential deal price between **$45 and $55 per share**.
    *   **Details:** Following the April 12 announcement, intense speculation emerged regarding a "bidding war." AstraZeneca was frequently cited as the frontrunner because of its need to bolster its biologics pipeline following several clinical trial setbacks. GlaxoSmithKline and Sanofi-Aventis were also rumored to be interested in MedImmune’s vaccine technology (FluMist).

*   **April 23, 2007 (Pre-Market/Early Morning)**
    *   **Reporting Outlet:** *The Street* / *FierceBiotech*
    *   **Rumored Acquirer:** **AstraZeneca**
    *   **Rumored Price/Premium:** **$58 per share**
    *   **Details:** Reports surfaced just before the official announcement that AstraZeneca had won the bidding process. The rumored price of $58 per share represented a 53% premium over the closing price on April 11 (the day before the strategic review was announced). This was confirmed shortly thereafter by the official deal announcement.
