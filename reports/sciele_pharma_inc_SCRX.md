## Summary

Sciele Pharma, Inc. was acquired by Shionogi & Co., Ltd. via a cash tender offer for $31.00 per share, valuing the company at approximately $1 billion. The transaction was structured as a two-step acquisition with a tender offer followed by a short-form merger, aimed at expanding Shionogi's presence in the U.S. pharmaceutical market.

## Timeline

- 2008-04-29: Jon Saxe (Sciele Director) receives initial call from Goldman Sachs on behalf of Shionogi.
- 2008-05-29: Sciele and Shionogi execute a Confidentiality and Standstill Agreement.
- 2008-06-04: Preliminary management meetings held in Atlanta between Shionogi and Sciele executives.
- 2008-06-30: Shionogi submits initial expression of interest at $28.50 per share with exclusivity request.

- 2008-09-01: Merger Agreement executed and transaction publicly announced.

- 2008-10-08: Subsequent offering period expires with 92% of shares tendered.
- 2008-10-09: Merger completed; Sciele becomes a wholly-owned subsidiary of Shionogi.

## Interaction History

Negotiations began in April 2008 when Goldman Sachs contacted Sciele director Jon Saxe. Sciele management, led by CEO Patrick Fourteau, engaged in parallel discussions with Shionogi and several private equity firms (Companies A, B, C, D, and E). Shionogi initially offered $28.50 in June, which was rejected as inadequate. Throughout July and August, Sciele provided due diligence access to Shionogi and several other bidders. Company B provided a higher potential range ($28.80-$33.38) but was viewed as having higher execution risk due to financing contingencies and a longer timeline. Shionogi increased its bid to $30.50 in late July and finally to $31.00 on August 28, 2008, characterizing it as 'best and final.' The Sciele Transaction Committee negotiated a $25 million breakup fee. On August 29, the Sciele Board determined that Shionogi's offer provided the best combination of value, speed, and certainty, particularly given the lack of a financing condition.

## Rationale

The Sciele Board recommended the deal because the $31.00 price represented a 57.7% premium over the closing price on the day prior to approval. The Board cited the certainty of cash value, the lack of a financing condition in Shionogi's offer, and the risks of remaining an independent company, including regulatory and generic competition risks. Shionogi sought the acquisition to establish a robust commercial platform in the United States.

## Evidence

- The Sciele Board, at a meeting duly called and held on August 29, 2008, by unanimous vote of all directors present at the meeting: declared that the Merger Agreement and the transactions contemplated thereby... are fair to and in the best interests of Sciele and its stockholders (SC 14D9).
- On October 9, 2008, Merger Sub was merged with and into Sciele (the 'Merger') pursuant to the terms of the Merger Agreement, with Sciele surviving as an indirect wholly-owned subsidiary of Parent (8-K, 2008-10-09).
