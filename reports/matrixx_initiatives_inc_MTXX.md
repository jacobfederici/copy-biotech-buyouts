## Summary

Matrixx Initiatives, Inc. entered into a definitive merger agreement with affiliates of H.I.G. Capital, LLC on December 14, 2010, following a period of significant regulatory and legal pressure regarding its Zicam products. The transaction was structured as a two-step tender offer, initially priced at $8.00 per share and later increased to $8.75 per share. The deal was contingent on the settlement of massive product liability litigation, which Matrixx achieved simultaneously with the merger signing. The acquisition was completed via a short-form merger on February 18, 2011, after H.I.G. utilized a top-up option to reach 90% ownership.

## Interaction History

Negotiations began in early 2010 when Joseph Falsetti of Primus Therapeutics (affiliated with H.I.G. Capital) approached Matrixx. Matrixx, led by CEO William Hemelt and General Counsel Samuel Cowley, initially insisted on a standstill agreement. H.I.G. proposed $6.50 per share in February 2010, which the Board found inadequate given the company's historical performance, despite a 2009 FDA warning letter that forced a recall of 40% of its revenue base. Throughout mid-2010, Sawaya Segalas advised the Board on valuation. In August 2010, the Board countered that they would consider a deal above $8.00. H.I.G. met this price in September 2010. A major hurdle arose in November 2010 when H.I.G. sought to condition the deal on specific litigation settlement terms; Matrixx briefly broke off talks. Discussions resumed as Matrixx finalized a $15.5 million settlement with over 2,000 personal injury claimants. On December 13, 2010, the Board unanimously approved the $8.00 offer. During the tender period, the price was bumped to $8.75 on February 1, 2011, to ensure sufficient participation.

## Rationale

The Matrixx Board recommended the deal due to the significant risks of remaining independent, including ongoing costs and liability from product liability and securities litigation, a challenging regulatory environment following the 2009 FDA warning letter, and the difficulty of competing as a small public company with a single-brand business. The cash offer provided immediate liquidity and a substantial premium (56% over the pre-announcement price) while mitigating the uncertainty of the company's financial prospects.

## Decision Notes

The deal value is based on the final $8.75 per share price multiplied by the 9,398,587 shares outstanding as of Nov 30, 2010, which aligns with the 'approximately $83 million' total consideration stated in the Feb 22, 2011 8-K. The offer price was increased from $8.00 to $8.75 on Feb 1, 2011.

## Buyout Rumors

Source: OpenRouter web search (Gemini 3 Flash Preview). Pre-deal rumors only.

Based on available historical records, there are no documented pre-deal rumors, acquisition speculation, or buyout reports for Matrixx Initiatives, Inc. (MTXX) prior to the official announcement of its acquisition.

The company was acquired by **H.I.G. Capital** via a definitive merger agreement announced on **December 14, 2010**. Before this date, public and financial reporting regarding Matrixx Initiatives was almost exclusively focused on its significant legal and regulatory challenges, specifically:
*   **FDA Regulatory Actions:** The June 2009 FDA warning letter and subsequent product recalls of Zicam.
*   **Securities Litigation:** The class-action lawsuit (*Matrixx Initiatives, Inc. v. Siracusano*) that eventually reached the U.S. Supreme Court.

The $75.2 million ($8.00 per share) offer from H.I.G. Capital was reported as a 56.3% premium over the previous day's closing price, and the deal included a "go-shop" period allowing the company to solicit other bids for 40 days after the announcement, further indicating that a competitive or rumored bidding process was not active prior to the H.I.G. agreement.
