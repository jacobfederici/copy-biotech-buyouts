## Summary

Sucampo Pharmaceuticals, Inc., a Rockville, Maryland-based specialty biopharmaceutical company, was acquired by Mallinckrodt plc in a cash tender offer at $18.00 per share. Mallinckrodt initiated contact in September 2017; the Merger Agreement was signed December 23, 2017 and publicly announced December 26, 2017. The tender offer commenced January 16, 2018, expired February 13, 2018 with approximately 83% of outstanding shares tendered, and the Section 251(h) back-end merger was effected the same day, converting all remaining shares at the same $18.00 price and making Sucampo a wholly owned indirect subsidiary of Mallinckrodt. Total transaction consideration was approximately $1.2 billion net of cash acquired. Sucampo's commercial product AMITIZA (lubiprostone) and pipeline assets VTS-270 and CPP-1X/sulindac were central to Mallinckrodt's strategic rationale.

## Interaction History

Sucampo's board maintained a standing Transaction Committee — originally composed of John H. Johnson (Lead Independent Director and Chair), Maureen E. O'Connell, and Timothy P. Walbert, reconstituted at the September 12, 2017 board meeting with the addition of Robert J. Spiegel — that had been in place since at least April 2016 to review strategic alternatives. The committee had previously overseen unsuccessful discussions with three external parties: Party A (a publicly traded pharmaceutical company that made an unsolicited proposal in October 2014; discussions terminated December 2014 when the stock price rose above the offer price following company announcements); Party B (a private equity fund in China that made an unsolicited proposal in April 2016 and terminated in November 2016 after failing to raise equity capital and proposing a stock-for-stock deal the founders would not accept); and Party C (an unidentified private China-based fund that submitted an unsolicited proposal in 2016 and terminated discussions in July 2016 citing generic entry risk to AMITIZA). During the Party B process, Sucampo also canvassed holders of AMITIZA marketing rights regarding acquisition interest; none expressed interest.

The Mallinckrodt process was initiated by Mallinckrodt on September 25, 2017, when Josh Schafer (VP Business Development and Licensing, Mallinckrodt) contacted Woody Bryan (SVP Business Development, Sucampo) by email. Three days later, Mr. Schafer informed Mr. Bryan that Mallinckrodt was interested in exploring an acquisition. Mr. Greenleaf, having been informed of the inquiry, consulted Mr. Johnson and agreed that Mallinckrodt should be invited to submit a formal proposal. Mr. Bryan requested that CEO Mark Trudeau call Mr. Greenleaf directly.

On October 9, 2017, Mr. Greenleaf and Mr. Trudeau held a call. Mr. Greenleaf conveyed that Sucampo was not for sale but that any proposal would receive appropriate board consideration. Mr. Trudeau requested a meeting to discuss in more detail, which Mr. Greenleaf, with Mr. Johnson's concurrence, agreed to.

On October 12, 2017, Mr. Greenleaf and Mr. Bryan met with Mr. Trudeau, Mr. Schafer, and Gary Philips, M.D. (EVP and Chief Strategy Officer, Mallinckrodt) at Mallinckrodt's New Jersey offices to discuss Sucampo's strategy. SCMP shares were trading at approximately $10.00–$10.40 at the time.

On October 20, 2017, Mr. Trudeau called Mr. Greenleaf to convey a preliminary non-binding offer in the range of $17.00–$19.00 per share, cash, subject to due diligence. On October 21, the Transaction Committee met and, with Cooley attending, discussed fiduciary duties. The committee authorized providing limited due diligence to obtain a single price rather than a range, and authorized engaging Jefferies LLC as financial advisor. Sucampo's filing cites Jefferies' reputation, healthcare M&A experience, and prior familiarity with Sucampo as reasons for the engagement.

Between the October 21 committee meeting and October 31, Sucampo and Mallinckrodt exchanged drafts of a confidentiality agreement. On October 31, during a call about the confidentiality agreement, Mr. Trudeau proposed a new price range. Mallinckrodt submitted a revised non-binding offer of $18.00–$18.50 later that day. The next day, November 1, Mallinckrodt submitted a further revision to $17.50–$18.50 following review of additional diligence materials. The parties executed the Confidentiality Agreement (with standstill) on November 3.

Between November 8 and November 27, Sucampo and Mallinckrodt management held a series of calls on commercial, clinical, and regulatory matters. Sucampo opened a limited data room on November 12 and provided full access beginning November 28.

On November 25, Mr. Trudeau called with a revised non-binding proposal of $17.50 per share, citing financial model assumptions that required further diligence investigation. The Transaction Committee convened the next day, November 26, with Mr. Edick, management, Cooley, and Jefferies. Jefferies reviewed the implied premium. The Transaction Committee discussed Sucampo's stand-alone risks — principally, expected AMITIZA generic competition starting in 2021 — and noted that the founders had recently sold shares at prices below the offer range per public filings, which the committee viewed as suggesting founder support for a transaction at the proposed levels. The committee authorized a counterproposal of $19.00 per share. Mr. Greenleaf conveyed the $19.00 counter to Mr. Trudeau that same day. Mr. Trudeau declined, stating he lacked board authority for $19.00 and Mallinckrodt's valuation would not support it. Mr. Trudeau revised to $18.00 as a final position.

On November 27, the Transaction Committee, with Mr. Edick, management, Cooley, and Jefferies, reviewed the situation. The $18.00 offer represented a 70.6% premium to the November 24 closing price of $10.55. The committee also evaluated whether a formal market check should be conducted. The committee concluded that a market check was unlikely to produce additional interest based on management's reports of lack of interest from general business development discussions, results of prior market checks, absence of the most logical acquirers, and the committee's own assessment of limited market interest in short-duration royalty assets like AMITIZA. The committee approved proceeding at $18.00 per share. Mr. Greenleaf communicated this to Mr. Trudeau the same day, conditioning it on completion of due diligence and execution before Christmas.

On November 28, Mallinckrodt delivered a written non-binding indication confirming $18.00 per share. Sucampo provided full data room access the same day.

On November 29, Sucampo sent Mallinckrodt a draft merger agreement providing for a tender offer, Section 251(h) back-end merger, and a 2.5% termination fee. On November 30, the Transaction Committee met with Mr. Edick, management, Cooley, and Jefferies. Management presented updated financial projections based on the March 2017 board-approved projections updated for 2018 AMITIZA Japan pricing and OTC generic AMITIZA assumptions. The committee reviewed Jefferies' preliminary financial analysis of the $18.00 price based on those projections. The committee also reviewed and accepted Jefferies' disclosure of material relationships with Mallinckrodt.

On December 1, Mr. Greenleaf and Mr. Trudeau discussed diligence status and targeted signing date. On December 7, Bloomberg published speculation about a potential Sucampo sale. SCMP stock closed at $16.00. Sucampo received no acquisition inquiries from any third party before or after the article.

On December 8, Wachtell sent Cooley a revised merger agreement draft increasing the termination fee to 4.0% and adding a founders' tender and support agreement requirement. On December 12, Sucampo's full board met with management, Cooley, and Jefferies. Jefferies updated the board on financial aspects of the transaction. Cooley reviewed fiduciary duties. The board discussed the market check issue, reaffirmed the Transaction Committee's conclusion against conducting one, and noted the absence of any third-party inquiries since the Bloomberg article. The board formally appointed Mr. Edick to the Transaction Committee. Following the board meeting, Mr. Greenleaf called Mr. Trudeau.

On December 13, Cooley sent Wachtell a revised draft with a 3.0% termination fee and no founder support agreement. On December 14, Wachtell and Cooley held calls, and Wachtell sent a further revised merger agreement and a proposed founders' tender and support agreement.

On December 15, Mr. Trudeau called Mr. Greenleaf to ask whether the board would consider contingent value rights (a structured deal) rather than all cash. Mr. Greenleaf stated the board would not accept anything below $18.00 cash per share.

On December 17, Mr. Trudeau called Mr. Greenleaf to confirm proceeding at $18.00 per share in cash and stated Mallinckrodt expected the founders to sign tender and support agreements. On December 18, Mr. Greenleaf updated the board and then participated in a conference call with Mr. Trudeau, Gary Phillips, Dr. Frank Scholz (EVP of Global Operations and President, Specialty Generics), and Jared Freedberg (VP Business Development, Mallinckrodt), along with Wachtell, to discuss the founders' support agreement and Sucampo's concern about potential delay.

That same day, Mr. Greenleaf contacted the founders (after they agreed to confidentiality), disclosed the proposed transaction, and requested execution of tender and support agreements. Cooley held calls with the founders' counsel, McGuireWoods LLP, and provided a draft support agreement. On December 20, McGuireWoods provided comments that Mallinckrodt accepted, and the founders agreed to execute the Support Agreement at signing. Wachtell and Cooley also exchanged merger agreement drafts throughout the week of December 18, resolving open issues including the termination fee.

On December 19, Wachtell provided Cooley Mallinckrodt's debt commitment letter related to new term loan financing of up to $500 million from Deutsche Bank AG New York Branch.

On December 22, Cooley sent Wachtell a revised merger agreement setting the termination fee at $44 million (approximately 3.5% of equity value including convertible notes). On December 22, the board held a special telephonic meeting. Mr. Greenleaf updated the board. Jefferies delivered oral and written fairness opinions on the $18.00 cash consideration. Cooley reviewed fiduciary duties and summarized the merger agreement terms, including non-solicitation provisions, recommendation modification process, termination rights, and the $44 million termination fee. The board — in executive session without Mr. Greenleaf — also considered the Compensation Committee's recommendations for executive AIP payments, Transaction Bonuses, and RSU vesting acceleration for tax planning purposes. The board unanimously approved the Merger Agreement and recommended that stockholders tender.

On December 23, 2017, Mallinckrodt's transaction and audit committees approved the Merger Agreement, the debt commitment letter was executed, and all parties signed the definitive Merger Agreement and the Tender and Support Agreement covering approximately 32% of outstanding shares (RJ Fund, LLC; Ryuji Ueno Foundation, Inc.; SK Impact Fund, LLC; Sachiko Kuno Foundation, Inc.).

The transaction was publicly announced December 26, 2017. The tender offer commenced January 16, 2018. An FDA communication on December 28, 2017 extended the AMITIZA pediatric sNDA PDUFA date by three months to April 28, 2018. The tender offer expired February 13, 2018, with approximately 83% of shares tendered. The Section 251(h) back-end merger closed the same day.

## Rationale

Per the SC 14D9 (accession 0001193125-18-011313), the board's stated rationale for recommending the Offer included:

The Offer was all-cash, providing certainty, immediate value, and liquidity to stockholders. The $18.00 per share consideration represented a 70.6% premium to the November 24, 2017 close of $10.55 (the last trading day before the Transaction Committee authorized proceeding at $18.00), a 20% premium to the December 6, 2017 close of $14.95 (the last day before Bloomberg speculation), and a 44% premium to the 30-day average and a 51% premium to the 90-day average as of December 6, 2017.

The board assessed Sucampo's stand-alone prospects and concluded that the $18.00 Offer Price was more favorable to stockholders than other strategic options. Key risks cited were: (a) expected generic competition for branded AMITIZA beginning in 2021 and the anticipated significant decline in revenue and EBITDA thereafter; (b) development and commercialization risks for VTS-270 and CPP-1X/sulindac; and (c) risks of identifying and acquiring additional pipeline assets.

The board determined that it had negotiated the highest price Mallinckrodt was willing to pay. The founders, who held approximately 32% of outstanding shares and had been selling shares at prices below the Offer Price per public filings (prior to becoming aware of the proposed transaction), agreed to enter into the Tender and Support Agreement — which the board viewed as further evidence of the transaction's reasonableness.

The board noted the Transaction Committee had concluded, based on management feedback, prior market checks, the absence of inquiries following the December 7, 2017 Bloomberg article, and its own assessment of Sucampo's portfolio, that a formal market check was unlikely to generate additional acquisition interest and could risk a leak that could undermine the Mallinckrodt transaction.

The board also cited the delivery by Mallinckrodt of a committed $500 million debt commitment letter from Deutsche Bank AG New York Branch, the absence of a financing condition in the Offer, and the structure of the Offer as a Section 251(h) back-end merger (eliminating the need for a shareholder vote and reducing timing uncertainty).

Negative factors acknowledged by the board included: Sucampo would cease to be publicly traded; the $44 million termination fee could deter competing offers; the transaction was taxable to stockholders; and if the Offer were not consummated, the stock price might decline and the company would have incurred significant transaction costs.
