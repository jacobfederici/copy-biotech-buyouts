## Summary

Cadence Pharmaceuticals Inc, a specialty pharmaceutical company with a single commercial product, OFIRMEV (intravenous acetaminophen), accepted an all-cash tender offer from Mallinckrodt plc at $14.00 per share on February 10, 2014. The offer valued the company at approximately $1.25 billion on a fully diluted basis. The tender offer expired on March 18, 2014, with 87.9% of outstanding shares tendered. Following successful completion of the tender offer and satisfaction of merger conditions, the companies consummated a merger on March 19, 2014, under Section 251(h) of the Delaware General Corporation Law, with Cadence becoming an indirect wholly owned subsidiary of Mallinckrodt.

## Interaction History

The transaction emerged from Cadence's proactive evaluation of strategic alternatives and subsequent competitive interest from multiple parties. In early July 2013, an unnamed large pharmaceutical company (referred to in the filing as 'Party A') approached CEO Theodore Schroeder to express acquisition interest. The companies executed a confidentiality agreement on August 19, 2013, and conducted diligence meetings and calls throughout August 2013 to evaluate performance and growth prospects. However, the discussions stalled after both parties determined that intellectual property uncertainty in Cadence's patent portfolio posed material challenges, and they agreed not to pursue further discussions at that time.

Interest rekindled following a significant favorable development in Cadence's IP landscape. On November 11, 2013, Mark Trudeau, the CEO of Mallinckrodt plc, contacted Schroeder by telephone to express interest in an all-cash acquisition of Cadence. This outreach came just three days before a November 14, 2013 court ruling in which the U.S. District Court for the District of Delaware ruled in favor of Cadence in its patent infringement lawsuit against Exela Pharma Sciences over OFIRMEV (acetaminophen injection), the Company's single commercial product. The court rejected Exela's invalidity challenges and found infringement of Exela's Abbreviated New Drug Applications for a generic version, substantially strengthening Cadence's IP position and market exclusivity prospects. Schroeder indicated willingness to discuss a potential transaction.

Mallinckrodt engaged Gary Phillips, its Senior Vice President and Chief Strategy Officer, to conduct preliminary diligence discussions starting November 18, 2013. Following this initial call, Cadence and Mallinckrodt executed a confidentiality agreement with standstill provision on December 6, 2013. Meanwhile, on December 12, 2013, Schroeder spoke with the CEO of Party A to revisit that party's interest. Party A indicated willingness to pursue an acquisition but indicated the offer would necessarily contain a significant stock component due to Party A's relative size. Schroeder emphasized the Board's strong preference for substantial upfront cash to maximize shareholder value and minimize equity risk.

Mallinckrodt pressed forward with substantive engagement. On December 18, 2013, Schroeder led Cadence's management team in a business presentation to Trudeau and other Mallinckrodt executives in Chicago, Illinois, further developing Mallinckrodt's understanding of Cadence's operations and prospects.

In the lead-up to the JP Morgan Healthcare Conference in January 2014, Cadence's Board reassessed its strategic options. On December 20, 2013, the Board held a telephonic meeting with its financial and legal advisors, Lazard and Latham & Watkins. Cadence had long maintained a relationship with Lazard (since September 2010) for financial advisory services on strategic transactions. The Board now retained Centerview Partners LLC as a co-financial advisor, believing Centerview's expertise in the pharmaceutical industry would add value to the Board's deliberations. The Board determined to pursue a potential sale process, citing risks associated with remaining independent as a single-product company in a highly regulated industry. The Board authorized management to continue discussions with Mallinckrodt and Party A, to schedule meetings with additional potential buyers at the JP Morgan Healthcare Conference, and to report back on January 23, 2014 on whether to commence a formal market solicitation.

Mallinckrodt increased pressure on January 9, 2014, when Schroeder called Trudeau to convey that other parties might be interested and that Cadence would seek the highest price. Schroeder emphasized that valuation would be the paramount concern for the Board, but also highlighted the importance of Mallinckrodt's ability to complete diligence quickly, sign a definitive agreement promptly, and demonstrate closing certainty.

The next day, January 10, 2014, Trudeau responded by phone indicating that Mallinckrodt was considering an all-cash offer at a 20-30% premium to Cadence's then-current 30-day average closing price, which Trudeau calculated at approximately $11.25 to $12.25 per share. However, Trudeau insisted that Mallinckrodt would proceed only on an exclusive basis given the magnitude of the premium being offered. During this call, Schroeder and Trudeau discussed the factors the Board would weigh: premium above trading price, fundamental values, market precedents, speed, and certainty of closing.

At the JP Morgan Healthcare Conference from January 13 to 15, 2014, Cadence's management held meetings with Party A and five other pharmaceutical companies to gauge acquisition interest. In the January 13 meeting with Party A, Schroeder again emphasized the Company's strong preference for substantial cash consideration. Party A responded positively and agreed to explore all-cash financing options. Schroeder encouraged Party A to submit a specific indication of value and proposed transaction structure in advance of the Board's January 23, 2014 meeting. The other five pharmaceutical companies did not follow up with indications of interest.

Also at the conference, on January 14, 2014, Schroeder, Trudeau, and other executives from both companies met in person to conduct deeper due diligence on intellectual property and commercial matters. At this meeting, Trudeau disclosed that Mallinckrodt had retained Deutsche Bank Securities Inc. as its financial advisor.

On January 17, 2014, Trudeau called Schroeder to raise Mallinckrodt's proposal materially. Trudeau stated that Mallinckrodt was prepared to offer $14.00 per share in all cash, with no financing contingency. In response to Trudeau's renewed request for exclusivity, Schroeder declined, stating that Cadence would not enter into an exclusivity agreement. Immediately following this call, Trudeau sent a written non-binding proposal letter offering $14.00 per share contingent on Cadence agreeing to 30 days of exclusive negotiations. The letter was accompanied by a separate highly confident financing letter from Deutsche Bank. On that same day, Cadence's common stock closed at $11.73 per share on the NASDAQ Global Select Market, representing approximately a 19% premium to Trudeau's proposed price.

The Board called a telephonic meeting on January 20, 2014, to assess all developments. Lazard and Centerview presented preliminary financial analyses, concluding that there was a low likelihood that any of the other potential parties identified at the JP Morgan conference would be willing or able to offer $14.00 or more per share, based on their DCF analyses, comparable company multiples, industry knowledge, and assessment of other potential players. The financial advisors noted that even if a competing party emerged after a public announcement, the provisions of a low termination fee and strong fiduciary out would allow the Board to consider superior proposals. The Board expressed its continuing desire for a higher price, emphasized the strategic value of speed relative to pending litigation and intellectual property matters, and stressed the importance of closing certainty. The Board instructed Lazard and Centerview to communicate to Deutsche Bank that Cadence remained committed to maximizing shareholder value and would continue discussions provided that timing and terms likely maximized value. The Board authorized advisors to seek a higher price, to communicate that Cadence would not grant exclusivity, and to emphasize that speed and closing certainty were critical. The Board concluded that Mallinckrodt's offer of $14.00 per share was sufficiently preemptive that the Board would defer commencing a formal market solicitation if Mallinckrodt would increase its offer to $15.00 per share. The Board authorized management to cooperate with Mallinckrodt's diligence inquiries.

On January 21, 2014, Lazard and Centerview held a call with Deutsche Bank to communicate the Board's position. Lazard and Centerview stated that if Mallinckrodt raised its price to $15.00 per share, the Board was inclined to delay a formal third-party solicitation process during a brief period for due diligence and negotiation. However, Lazard and Centerview clarified that Cadence would need to respond to any unsolicited inquiries from other parties in the interim. They also indicated, at Board direction, that Mallinckrodt could immediately begin accessing a virtual data room containing Cadence's nonpublic business and financial information. On the same day, Latham & Watkins and Wachtell Lipton (counsel to Mallinckrodt) held a telephonic meeting to discuss key transaction terms, including termination fees, the deal price, the mechanics of a potential 'market check' of selected parties, and any marketing periods related to Mallinckrodt's debt financing efforts.

Two developments unfolded on January 22, 2014. First, the CEO of Party A called Schroeder to report that Party A was seriously considering an offer at a premium to trading price but that a significant equity component would be necessary and the transaction would be subject to Party A shareholder approval. The CEO indicated he had obtained Board approval and engaged a financial advisor. However, he declined to indicate a specific proposed value and stated he was not yet positioned to present a written offer. Schroeder responded that Cadence had received an attractive proposal from another party and that Party A would need to move quickly with a more specific, aggressive all-cash-focused offer in advance of the January 23 Board meeting.

Also on January 22, 2014, Trudeau called Schroeder with a significant refinement to Mallinckrodt's offer. Trudeau suggested that Mallinckrodt might bridge the $14.00-$15.00 gap by offering $14.00 per share in cash plus a contingent value right with a value of approximately $1.00 per share tied to unspecified near-term milestones. Schroeder indicated he would discuss the proposal with the Board.

On January 23, 2014, the CEO of Party A left a voicemail with Schroeder reiterating interest but without providing specific details.

That same day, the Board held its regularly-scheduled in-person meeting at Cadence's San Diego headquarters, with Lazard, Centerview, and Latham & Watkins representatives present, along with management. Lazard and Centerview reviewed their most recent financial analysis and again discussed their assessment that the likelihood was low that any other identified potential buyer would offer $14.00 per share or more, based on DCF analysis, comparable company multiples, industry knowledge, and the assessed capacity of alternative players. They also discussed with the Board the likelihood that if any competing party emerged with a superior offer after public announcement of a Mallinckrodt transaction, such a party would not be deterred by termination fee and fiduciary-out provisions if those provisions were appropriately structured. Latham & Watkins reviewed the Board's fiduciary duty principles in the context of third-party acquisition interest.

The Board discussed its ongoing desire for a higher price, the critical importance of speed of the transaction process relative to pending litigation and intellectual property matters, the significance of closing certainty for any publicly announced transaction, and the value of permitting other buyers to propose superior terms. Weighing all these factors against Mallinckrodt's offer (which the Board and advisors characterized as 'preemptive'), the Board agreed to defer a formal third-party market solicitation process in exchange for (1) a low termination fee, (2) appropriate fiduciary-out provisions to permit consideration of superior proposals, and (3) rapid advancement of due diligence and transaction documentation. The Board instructed management and advisors to seek a higher price from Mallinckrodt, to communicate that Cadence would not grant pre-signing exclusivity, and to emphasize the paramount importance of speed and closing certainty.

Following this Board meeting, Schroeder called Trudeau to propose a counter-offer of $14.50 per share with an additional $0.50 per share in contingent consideration tied to a near-term milestone, for a total potential value of $15.00 per share. Schroeder communicated that Cadence would work expeditiously with Mallinckrodt to complete due diligence and negotiate the merger agreement for potential announcement on February 6, 2014. In exchange for a low termination fee and appropriate fiduciary-out provisions, Schroeder indicated that Cadence would not make outbound calls to third parties but would continue to respond to any inbound inquiries from interested parties while negotiations with Mallinckrodt continued.

Trudeau responded that given the significance of Mallinckrodt's offer, Mallinckrodt would not continue negotiating unless Cadence agreed not to conduct a pre-signing market check. Trudeau further stated that Mallinckrodt would not accept a 'go-shop' provision in the merger agreement but would be reasonable regarding other terms important to Cadence.

Counsel for both parties exchanged merger agreement drafts beginning January 27, 2014. Latham & Watkins' initial proposal included a more limited 'match period' for Mallinckrodt in response to a superior alternative proposal. Wachtell Lipton's initial draft proposed a 3% Company termination fee and indicated that Mallinckrodt would not be required to close until completion of a 'marketing period' for Mallinckrodt's debt financing. Wachtell Lipton's draft further provided for a 4% reverse termination fee if Mallinckrodt failed to obtain committed debt financing proceeds, but stated that the Company could not specifically enforce Mallinckrodt's obligation to consummate the transaction.

On January 31, 2014, Latham & Watkins sent a revised draft merger agreement to Wachtell Lipton, proposing revised match rights, termination fee level, specific performance remedies, and marketing-period mechanics. Critically, representatives of Latham & Watkins communicated to Wachtell Lipton that deal certainty was of paramount importance to Cadence and that Cadence would not accept any financing-related conditionality on Mallinckrodt's obligation to close, regardless of the size of a reverse termination fee. This position reflected the Board's determination that equity and debt financing certainty were essential to justifying the recommendation to shareholders.

The Board held another telephonic meeting on January 31, 2014, with Lazard, Centerview, and Latham & Watkins representatives present. The advisors reported on their recent conversations with Deutsche Bank regarding the revised Mallinckrodt offer. Latham & Watkins updated the Board on the ongoing merger agreement negotiations. In light of Mallinckrodt's strong bid, the agreement on a low termination fee, and appropriate fiduciary-out provisions, the Board confirmed its decision to refrain from making outbound calls to third parties but to respond to inbound inquiries, including from Party A. The Board instructed management and Latham & Watkins to work expeditiously to complete remaining due diligence and finalize the merger agreement.

After intensive negotiation throughout early February 2014, the parties reached final agreement on a Merger Agreement dated February 10, 2014. The final offer was $14.00 per share in all cash, with no contingent value right component (the parties had settled on the $14.00 base price during negotiations). The Merger Agreement included no financing contingency, reflecting Cadence's insistence on closing certainty. The termination fee was 3.3% of equity value (~$41 million). On February 10, 2014, the Cadence Board unanimously approved the Merger Agreement and recommended that shareholders accept the tender offer.

Mallinckrodt commenced the tender offer on February 19, 2014, at $14.00 per share, with an initial expiration date of March 10, 2014, which was subsequently extended to March 18, 2014. The offer expired at 12:00 midnight New York City time on March 18, 2014. As of the expiration date, 78,396,180 shares had been validly tendered (representing 87.9% of the outstanding shares at that time, as calculated by the depositary, Computershare Trust Company). Mallinckrodt accepted all validly tendered shares for payment.

Following the successful completion of the tender offer and satisfaction of all remaining merger conditions set forth in the Merger Agreement, Mallinckrodt and Cadence consummated the merger on March 19, 2014, without a stockholder meeting, pursuant to Section 251(h) of the Delaware General Corporation Law. Cadence became the surviving corporation and an indirect wholly owned subsidiary of Mallinckrodt. Trading in Cadence's common stock was halted on NASDAQ on March 19, 2014.

## Rationale

According to the SC 14D9 filing, the Company Board and management determined that a potential sale of the Company was in the best interests of shareholders due to the risks of remaining independent as a single-product company in a highly regulated industry.

The Board articulated several rationales for recommending the transaction:

1. **Single-Product Risk**: Cadence's business model depended entirely on sales of OFIRMEV (intravenous acetaminophen). The Board noted the risks involved in continuing to grow revenues and maintain market position for a single commercial product against potential generic competition and the significant competitive pressures and regulatory environment in the pharmaceutical industry.

2. **Patent Coverage and IP Risk**: The Company's long-term value depended on maintaining patent protection for OFIRMEV. The Board highlighted risks related to pending and potential future patent infringement lawsuits, patent re-examinations, and the risk of generic products competing with the single product earlier than anticipated. However, the November 14, 2013 favorable ruling by the U.S. District Court for the District of Delaware (upholding the validity of OFIRMEV patents and finding infringement of generic applications) substantially mitigated these concerns and likely strengthened Cadence's independent valuation at the time discussions with Mallinckrodt advanced.

3. **Strategic Value of Speed and Certainty**: The Board emphasized the importance of speed relative to upcoming business events, such as pending litigation, key intellectual property matters, and potential generic competition. The Board valued the certainty of an all-cash transaction without financing contingency, which eliminated execution risk and ensured predictable shareholder value. The Board characterized Mallinckrodt's offer—particularly the absence of financing contingency—as reflecting a preemptive valuation, and the financial advisors indicated a low likelihood that competing bidders would emerge with superior terms.

4. **Valuation Support**: The Company's financial advisors, Lazard and Centerview, provided discounted cash flow analyses indicating an implied equity value of $7.50 to $9.00 per share based on management's operating plans. The $14.00 per share offer substantially exceeded these DCF-derived valuations. The advisors reviewed comparable precedent transactions in the pharmaceutical industry and concluded that the $14.00 offer represented a strong valuation relative to public market multiples and comparable transactions. The advisors assessed that, based on industry knowledge and assessment of potential alternative buyers at the JP Morgan Healthcare Conference, it was unlikely that other parties would offer $14.00 per share or more.

The SC 14D9 does not separately articulate Mallinckrodt's strategic rationale, but it is evident from the interaction history that Mallinckrodt sought to acquire Cadence's OFIRMEV asset, portfolio of pain-relief products, market position, and customer relationships, likely for integration into Mallinckrodt's own specialty pharmaceutical and pain-management portfolio.
