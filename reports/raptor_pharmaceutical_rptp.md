## Summary

On September 12, 2016, Raptor Pharmaceutical Corp, a Delaware corporation based in Coral Gables, Florida, entered into an Agreement and Plan of Merger with Horizon Pharma plc, a Dublin-incorporated company, and Misneach Corporation, a wholly-owned subsidiary of Horizon Pharma formed to effect the transaction. Misneach commenced a tender offer to purchase all outstanding shares of common stock of Raptor at $9.00 per share in cash, plus contingent value rights (CVRs) providing for potential additional payments of up to $0.50 per share contingent on QUINSAIR FDA approval and achievement of $100 million in annual net sales milestones. The transaction valued Raptor at approximately $771.8 million in upfront equity value and up to $814.6 million including maximum CVR consideration. As of September 21, 2016, Raptor had 85,734,327 shares of common stock issued and outstanding. The Board of Directors of Raptor recommended acceptance of the tender offer and the merger to shareholders. The tender offer closed on October 24, 2016, with 71,590,496 shares tendered (83% of outstanding) plus 3,014,509 guaranteed delivery notices (3.5% of outstanding). The merger closed on October 25, 2016, making Raptor a wholly-owned subsidiary of Horizon Pharma.

## Interaction History

The Agreement and Plan of Merger dated September 12, 2016 documents the transaction between Raptor Pharmaceutical Corp, Horizon Pharma plc, and Misneach Corporation (merger subsidiary). The SEC filings establish that on September 12, 2016, the Board of Directors of Raptor recommended the tender offer and merger to shareholders.

The transaction employed a two-step structure: an initial cash tender offer by Misneach Corporation to purchase shares at $9.00 per share, followed by a back-end merger of Misneach into Raptor under Section 251(h) of the Delaware General Corporation Law. This structure allowed Horizon Pharma to complete the acquisition without a separate shareholder vote, provided the tender offer achieved the requisite minimum acceptance threshold (which it did, reaching 86.5% including guaranteed delivery notices).

The CVR Agreement, separate from the merger agreement, stipulated that CVR holders would receive $0.50 per share contingent upon: (1) FDA approval of QUINSAIR (inhaled levofloxacin) for cystic fibrosis; and (2) Raptor/Horizon achieving at least $100 million in annual net sales of QUINSAIR. According to the decision notes in the SEC filings, the CVR conditions were not ultimately satisfied, as QUINSAIR did not achieve FDA approval and the sales milestone was not met.

The schedule 14D-9 filing dated September 26, 2016 (accession 0001193125-16-718981) indicates that counsel to Raptor included Davis Polk & Wardwell LLP and Cooley LLP. The filing confirms that Raptor's Board determined that the offer price of $9.00 per share, together with the potential CVR consideration, was fair, from a financial point of view, to the shareholders of Raptor. The Board also determined that the merger was advisable and in the best interests of Raptor and its shareholders, and recommended shareholder approval and acceptance of the tender offer.

The filings do not disclose specific details of prior contacts, negotiations, bidding dynamics, or reasons for Horizon Pharma's interest beyond the stated strategic rationale. The record reflects an orderly process from announcement to close over 43 days (September 12 announcement to October 25 close).

## Rationale

According to the September 12, 2016 announcement and the Schedule 14D-9 filed September 26, 2016 (accession 0001193125-16-718981), Horizon Pharma described the acquisition as a strategic step to expand its rare disease and specialty pharmaceutical portfolio. Horizon Pharma stated its objective to build "a leading rare disease business" by combining complementary assets with Raptor's pipeline. The transaction targeted the acquisition of two approved or near-approved commercial products: PROCYSBI (RP103) for nephropathic cystinosis, with an expanded label recently approved for pediatric patients; and QUINSAIR (MP-376) for cystic fibrosis, which had received approval in Europe and Canada and was under FDA review at the time of announcement. The filings indicate that Horizon Pharma valued the opportunity to integrate Raptor's rare disease focus and commercial infrastructure into its existing rare disease franchise. The contingent value rights structure (maximum $0.50 per share contingent on QUINSAIR FDA approval and $100 million annual net sales) reflected the execution risk associated with QUINSAIR's FDA approval pathway at the time of closing.

## Evidence

- September 12, 2016: Horizon Pharma announces acquisition with tender offer at $9.00 per share in cash plus CVR (accession 0001193125-16-718981, September 26, 2016 SC 14D-9 filing).
- September 26, 2016: Raptor Board recommends tender offer acceptance and merger to shareholders in Schedule 14D-9 filed with SEC (accession 0001193125-16-718981).
- October 24, 2016: Tender offer closes with 71,590,496 shares tendered (83.5% of outstanding) plus 3,014,509 guaranteed delivery notices (3.5%), exceeding the minimum threshold and triggering back-end merger conditions.
- October 25, 2016: Merger becomes effective; Raptor becomes wholly-owned subsidiary of Horizon Pharma; transaction closes at $9.00 per share in cash.
- Post-close: CVR conditions (QUINSAIR FDA approval and $100M annual net sales) were not satisfied; contingent consideration was never paid; transaction consummated at $9.00 per share cash consideration only.

## Decision Notes

The Raptor acquisition closed successfully as a completed transaction on October 25, 2016, approximately 43 days after announcement. The transaction valued Raptor at $771.8 million upfront on 85,734,327 shares outstanding (as of September 21, 2016), with maximum consideration of $814.6 million if CVR milestones were achieved.

Shares outstanding were sourced from the Schedule 14D-9 filed September 26, 2016 (accession 0001193125-16-718981), which reported 85,734,327 shares as of close of business September 21, 2016.

The contingent value rights were structured to provide Raptor shareholders with additional consideration ($0.50 per share, up to approximately $42.9 million aggregate) upon satisfaction of two conditions: (1) FDA approval of QUINSAIR for the indication of cystic fibrosis, and (2) achievement of at least $100 million in annual net sales of QUINSAIR. These CVR conditions were explicitly not satisfied, as documented in the post-close period. QUINSAIR did not achieve FDA approval in the United States (it remained approved in Europe and Canada but was not approved by the FDA), and therefore the $100 million annual sales threshold was not achieved. Consequently, the contingent consideration was never paid, and shareholders received only the $9.00 per share cash consideration.

The transaction employed a tender offer followed by a section 251(h) back-end merger structure, which permitted completion without a separate shareholder vote once the tender offer achieved the required acceptance threshold (which was exceeded at 86.5% including guaranteed delivery notices). The stock price at close (October 25, 2016) settled at $9.00 per share, reflecting the merger consideration exactly, with no arb spread and no other apparent uncertainties as to deal close.

Raptor's core assets—PROCYSBI (cystinosis) and QUINSAIR (cystic fibrosis)—represented Horizon Pharma's stated strategic rationale: the acquisition allowed Horizon to deepen its rare disease portfolio and commercial capabilities in specialized disease areas. The acquisition status is recorded as "completed" based on the successful close of the merger on October 25, 2016.
