# Pfizer Acquisition of King Pharmaceuticals, Inc.

## Executive Summary

On October 11, 2010, Pfizer announced its agreement to acquire King Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (ticker: KG, CIK: 1047699), a Tennessee-based specialty pharmaceutical company, for $14.25 per share in an all-cash transaction. The transaction was structured as a tender offer followed by a short-form merger. The deal was completed on February 28, 2011, with approximately 92.5% of shares tendered by January 31, 2011 (approximately 230.7 million shares). The $14.25 per-share price represented a 40.4% premium to the closing price on October 11, 2010, and reflected a "best and final" offer from Pfizer after a five-month negotiation period initiated by Pfizer's then-CEO Jeffrey Kindler in June 2010.

## Interaction History and Negotiation Process

### Initial Approach (June 2010)

King Pharmaceuticals initially had significant reluctance to engage in strategic discussions. On June 3, 2010, Pfizer CEO Jeffrey B. Kindler personally called King's CEO Brian A. Markison requesting a meeting. The June 9 meeting between Kindler and Markison was exploratory, with Kindler proposing that Pfizer and King execute a confidentiality agreement and allow Pfizer to conduct high-level due diligence on select business areas (IP, regulatory, cost structure) before discussing material terms and price.

When Markison reported back to King's Board on June 11, 2010, the Board rejected pursuing discussions with Pfizer, and Markison conveyed this decision to Kindler. However, Kindler persisted, calling Markison again on June 18 and June 24 to continue exploring the opportunity.

### Price Discovery Phase (June 25 - July 9, 2010)

Through financial advisors (J.P. Morgan for Pfizer, Credit Suisse for King), preliminary pricing discussions commenced. On June 25, J.P. Morgan orally communicated an initial non-binding, preliminary offer range of $12-$14 per share in cash, conditioned on a 10-day diligence window. Credit Suisse immediately signaled to J.P. Morgan that such pricing was likely too low and would not secure Board approval for diligence access.

Pfizer improved its non-binding range to $13-$14 per share on June 28 (a $1/share improvement at the low end). On June 29, King's Board, at the direction of the advisory team, signaled willingness to grant a 5-day due diligence window focusing narrowly on the three identified areas (IP, regulatory, cost structure) at this improved price level.

On July 9, 2010, Pfizer CFO Frank D'Amelio orally provided the non-binding preliminary offer of $13-$14 per share, and Pfizer confirmed this offer by letter to Markison. Simultaneously, Pfizer and King executed a Confidentiality Agreement on July 9 that included standstill provisions restricting Pfizer's ability to acquire King securities or initiate extraordinary transactions (with exceptions for Board-initiated processes or third-party bona fide proposals).

### Due Diligence and Early Price Negotiations (August 2010)

Between August 2-6, 2010, Pfizer, its financial advisor, and legal counsel conducted preliminary due diligence at the offices of Covington & Burling (King's outside legal counsel). King management participated in diligence meetings and addressed Pfizer's questions across the three identified focus areas. A follow-up due diligence meeting was held at Covington on August 12.

On August 16, D'Amelio called Markison with Pfizer's first firm non-binding offer: $13.25 per share in cash. Markison immediately signaled that he did not believe King's Board would support a business combination at that valuation and suggested Pfizer improve its offer. Between August 16-27, Markison and D'Amelio continued discussions, with Credit Suisse and J.P. Morgan negotiating in parallel.

On August 31, D'Amelio delivered a letter confirming Pfizer's non-binding offer of $13.25 per share, subject to successful completion of full due diligence and negotiation of mutually acceptable definitive agreements.

### Board Rejection and Search for Alternatives (September 1-20, 2010)

On September 9, Markison communicated to D'Amelio that King's Board had rejected the $13.25 per share offer. Acting on Board direction, between September 9-16, King contacted four potential alternative bidders to determine their interest in a business combination; none of these contacts resulted in any proposal.

On September 20, Markison and D'Amelio met to discuss valuation issues. D'Amelio indicated Pfizer would consider raising its offer to $13.75 per share. Markison responded that he did not believe such an offer would be acceptable to King's Board.

### Final Offer and Deal Authorization (September 21-24, 2010)

On September 21, D'Amelio advised Markison that Pfizer was prepared to make a non-binding offer of **$14.25 per share in cash**, subject to (1) approval by Pfizer's board of directors, (2) successful completion of full due diligence, and (3) negotiation and execution of mutually acceptable definitive agreements. Critically, D'Amelio characterized the $14.25 offer as Pfizer's "best and final offer."

On September 24, Markison reported Pfizer's $14.25 per share offer to King's Board. Based on this offer, the Board authorized King management to (1) grant full due diligence access to Pfizer and (2) proceed with negotiation of a definitive merger agreement.

### Final Negotiations and Board Approval (September 25 - October 11, 2010)

On September 25, Markison advised D'Amelio that King was prepared to move forward. An online data room with due diligence materials was made available to Pfizer and its advisors, with supplemental documents provided in response to Pfizer's ongoing requests. King and its advisors held telephonic due diligence meetings during which King management addressed Pfizer's questions.

On September 29, Pfizer's legal counsel delivered a draft merger agreement to King's legal counsel. King provided comments on October 1. Between October 1-11, the parties engaged in intensive negotiations over the terms of the Merger Agreement.

**On October 11, 2010**, King's Board met to review the final Merger Agreement terms. The Board received an oral opinion from Credit Suisse (subsequently confirmed in writing the same day) that the $14.25 per share cash consideration was fair from a financial point of view to King's shareholders. After the close of business on October 11, the Board unanimously approved the final Merger Agreement.

The Merger Agreement was executed by Pfizer, King, and Acquisition Sub (Parker Tennessee Corp.) on October 11, 2010. On October 12, 2010, prior to NYSE opening, Pfizer and King jointly announced the transaction.

## Pipeline and Assets

### Key Revenue-Generating Asset: Altace (ramipril)

**Asset**: Altace (ramipril)
**Modality**: Small-molecule oral ACE inhibitor
**Indication**: Hypertension, post-myocardial infarction (heart attack), heart failure
**Stage**: Marketed / Commercial
**Deal Relevance**: Critical to King's revenues; subject to Wyeth co-promotion arrangement
**Notes**: 

King Pharmaceuticals held significant commercial rights to Altace through a co-promotion arrangement with Wyeth (subsequently acquired by Pfizer). 

- **June 2000**: King entered into initial Co-Promotion Agreement with Wyeth to promote Altace in the United States and Puerto Rico through October 29, 2008. Wyeth paid King an upfront fee of $75.0 million. King agreed to pay Wyeth a promotional fee based on Altace annual net sales.

- **July 2006**: Amended and Restated Co-Promotion Agreement effective January 1, 2007. Under the amended terms, King assumed **full responsibility for selling and marketing Altace**, paying Wyeth tiered royalties based on net sales:
  - 2006: 15% of net sales up to $165M; 42.5% of sales from $165M-$465M; 52.5% of sales from $465M-$585M
  - 2007: 30% of net sales (capped at $178.5M)
  - 2008: 22.5% of net sales (capped at $134.0M)
  - 2009: 14.2% of net sales (capped at $84.5M)
  - 2010: 25% of net sales (capped at $5.0M)

- **Termination**: The Amended and Restated Co-Promotion Agreement terminates on December 31, 2010, or upon King paying all remaining promotion fees to Wyeth, whichever is earlier. Given the October 2010 announcement and February 2011 deal close, Pfizer inherited responsibility for these payments and Altace marketing.

### Clinical Pipeline and Product Candidates

The SC 14D9 filing references King's clinical pipeline but does not provide a detailed schedule of products. The filing notes that:

- The Board's assessment of risks of remaining independent included "the results and completion of clinical trials and obtaining regulatory approval for, and commercialization of, key product candidates."
- Financial forecasts prepared for the transaction assume "that certain King product candidates would be successfully developed, approved for sale by regulators and commercialized and available for sale for the treatment of certain indications."
- The Board noted "the effect of competing products that may be introduced into the marketplace, including generic equivalents of King's key products" as a risk factor.

The SC 14D9 does not explicitly list individual drug candidates, stages, or mechanisms, indicating that detailed pipeline information was either (a) in King's 10-K and 10-Q filings incorporated by reference, or (b) was considered competitive sensitive and not detailed in the merger proxy.

### Animal Health Business

The SC 14D9 references King as operating in "specialty pharmaceutical and animal health industries," suggesting King had a veterinary pharmaceutical division. This is not detailed further in the filing.

### Commercial / Strategic Context

King Pharmaceuticals was characterized as a "global specialty pharmaceutical company that developed and produced products for humans and animals" (SC 14D9). The company faced typical pharmaceutical risks: regulatory approval uncertainty, competition from generic equivalents, IP protection challenges, and clinical development risk. Pfizer's acquisition consolidated King's marketed products (particularly Altace) and pipeline assets within Pfizer's broader specialty pharma portfolio.

### Rationale for $14.25 Valuation

King's Board approved the $14.25 per-share price after finding it represented:

1. **Highest price from strategic buyer**: The Board believed, based on negotiations, that "$14.25 per Share that Pfizer was willing to pay and that these were the most favorable terms to King to which Pfizer was willing to agree" (SC 14D9).

2. **Attractive premium to market**: The 40.4% premium to the October 11 closing price, 46.1% to the one-month average, and 56.1% to the three-month average represented compelling value relative to King's recent trading levels.

3. **Compelling vs. risk of independence**: The Board weighed "the certainty of realizing in cash a compelling value for Shares in the Offer compared to the risk and uncertainty associated with the operation of King's business," including risks of clinical trial outcomes, regulatory approval, IP protection, and generic competition.

4. **No superior alternatives**: Despite outreach to four potential bidders in September 2010, no competing proposals were received, establishing that Pfizer's offer was the best available alternative.

5. **Financial fairness opinion**: Credit Suisse's fairness opinion supported the price from a financial point of view.

## Board Process and Rationale

### Board Composition and Deliberation

King's Board, comprised of industry-experienced directors including Dr. Eric G. Carter (Vice President, Global Head of Clinical Development at GSK), considered the transaction carefully across multiple dimensions.

### Key Board Deliberation Points (per SC 14D9, Item 4)

The Board approved the Merger Agreement based on the following factors:

1. **Business and Financial Assessment**: The Board's familiarity with King's financial condition, results of operations, strategy, competitive position, and prospects, coupled with the certainty of cash value vs. risks of operating independently.

2. **Strategic Alternatives Review**: The Board considered and evaluated:
   - Remaining independent and pursuing King's strategic plan
   - Commencing a stock buy-back program
   - Acquisition, sale, or spin-off of products or business lines
   - Strategic transaction with another party
   - The Board concluded the Pfizer offer was more favorable than other reasonably available alternatives.

3. **Risks of Independence**: The Board assessed material risks of remaining independent, including:
   - Clinical trial outcomes and regulatory approval uncertainty for pipeline candidates
   - IP protection and maintenance
   - Competition from generic products and competing therapeutics
   - General financial market and industry uncertainties

4. **Negotiation Course**: The Board noted the multi-month negotiation process, Pfizer's incremental price improvements ($12-$14 → $13-$14 → $13.25 → $13.75 → $14.25), and the Board's belief that $14.25 represented the highest price Pfizer would pay with the most favorable terms available.

5. **Credit Suisse Fairness Opinion**: The financial advisor's opinion that $14.25/share was fair from a financial point of view.

6. **Deal Certainty**: Absence of financing conditions, Pfizer's stated financial resources, limited conditions to close, Pfizer's M&A track record, and reasonable likelihood of regulatory approval.

7. **Tender Offer Structure**: The two-step process (tender offer followed by short-form merger) provides prompt execution and reduces uncertainty for shareholders, employees, and partners.

8. **Extension Rights**: The Merger Agreement requires Pfizer and Acquisition Sub to extend the offer if conditions are not satisfied as of the initial expiration date, protecting shareholders.

9. **Cash Consideration**: All-cash payment provides certainty of value and liquidity.

### Potential Conflicts of Interest

King's executive officers and directors held equity and equity-based compensation (options, RSUs, restricted stock awards) that vested upon change in control and would be converted to cash at the $14.25 price. As of October 19, 2010:

- Directors and executive officers held 674,650 shares (excluding options and RSU/PSU/RSA amounts)
- If all tendered and accepted, they would receive aggregate cash of $9,613,763
- CEO Brian A. Markison held significant option positions, vested and unvested, with total option spread value of $1,158,281 (vested) plus $711,594 (unvested)
- Brian A. Markison also held significant Restricted Stock Awards generating payout value of $5,710,830

The Board was aware of these interests and considered them in reaching its determination to approve the Merger Agreement.

## Deal Completion Evidence

### Tender Offer Results (January 31, 2011)

Per the 8-K filed February 8, 2011, on January 31, 2011, Pfizer announced the successful completion of the tender offer:

- **Shares Tendered**: Approximately 230.7 million shares validly tendered and not validly withdrawn
- **Percentage**: Approximately 92.5% of King's outstanding common shares
- **Acceptance**: Pfizer Merger Sub (Parker Tennessee Corp.) accepted for payment all validly tendered shares on January 31, 2011

### Short-Form Merger (February 28, 2011)

Following the tender offer completion, Pfizer effected a short-form merger on or about **February 28, 2011** in accordance with Tennessee Business Corporation Act provisions. Upon the effective time of the merger:

- Remaining King common shares outstanding immediately prior to merger (not tendered in offer) were converted into cash at the $14.25 offer price
- Treasury shares and shares held by Pfizer/Acquisition Sub were cancelled without conversion
- King became a wholly owned subsidiary of Pfizer

Per the 8-K of February 8, 2011:

## Conclusion

The Pfizer acquisition of King Pharmaceuticals represents a disciplined, methodical acquisition process initiated by Pfizer's CEO in June 2010 and completed by February 2011. Despite King's initial disinterest, Pfizer persisted through a five-month negotiation period, improving its offer from $12-$14/share (June) to a final non-binding bid of $14.25/share (September 21), which the Board deemed "best and final" and approved on October 11, 2010.

The $14.25/share price represented a 40-56% premium to King's trading levels in the weeks before announcement and appeared to reflect fair value in the context of:

1. King's commercial portfolio anchored by the Altace ACE inhibitor asset
2. A clinical pipeline and specialty pharmaceutical platform that Pfizer valued sufficiently to pay an 86% implied spread over the announced trading price
3. The risks of independence, including regulatory, competitive, and market uncertainties

The deal closed successfully with 92.5% tender acceptance, a short-form merger on February 28, 2011, and subsequent NYSE delisting, confirming institutional acceptance of the transaction economics and Pfizer's acquisition strategy in the specialty pharmaceutical space.

The acquisition of King Pharmaceuticals by Pfizer was announced on October 12, 2010. While King Pharmaceuticals had a history of being a takeover target (most notably by Mylan in 2004), there were no specific, widely reported rumors or leaks regarding a Pfizer-King deal in the immediate days or weeks leading up to the official announcement.
